## Week summary

The basin spent W33 in steady recession at the terminal reaches while Bryson Dam cycled through two turbine on/off pulses and Carillon executed a significant step-down late in the week. Carillon opened its spillway briefly on Aug 4 evening — the first spillway use in this episode — then closed it and ran turbine-only through Aug 10. Bryson shifted from 32% spill (Aug 4 daily mean) to 91% spill (Aug 8) as operators idled turbines to 35 m³/s for roughly 40 hours (Aug 7 T20Z through Aug 8 T15Z), then resumed twice with a second shutdown overnight Aug 9–10. Bryson headpond remained continuously above the 104.67 m operating ceiling all week (daily means 105.34–105.44 m; Day 68–74 of exceedance). Lac Coulonge declined from 106.09 m midnight (Aug 4) to 106.01 m (Aug 10), a gentle 8 cm drop with no flood concern. Hull dock held 109–118 cm below the §15.3.5.1 trigger throughout.

The single worst data-quality event was the Aug 5 Des Joachims 995 m³/s artifact: ORRPB's conditions-page afternoon provisional implied a physically impossible remaining-hours mean (≤ −394 m³/s); the claim made it into the Aug 5 headline before the sanity-check guardrail existed, was retracted in the Aug 6 brief, and confirmed at 474 m³/s. The guardrail added Aug 6 correctly blocked five subsequent corrupted Des Joachims provisionals (Aug 6–9); none of those appeared in a headlined claim. A separate data-quality issue surfaced: ORRPB conditions-page "confirmed" values for Aug 8 were revised upward > 24 h after publication (Des Joachims 367→641, Témiscaming 353→393), but the cluster DB (OPG/PSPC agency pipelines) does not reflect those revisions — see human queue.
