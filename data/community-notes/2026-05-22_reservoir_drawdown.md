# Two questions, not one: were the reservoirs drawn down — and was the river kept low?

*Community note, 2026-05-22. Suitable for sharing in the Northern
Reservoirs / Ottawa River / Tourism / Wildlife / Flood Watch group's
Files section. Every figure below is a computed result from public Water
Survey of Canada and Ottawa River Regulating Planning Board (ORRPB)
data — method and sources at the bottom. Companion to Exhibit B (Winter
Baseline) in the case file and to the daily-brief series in
`freshet-public/data/daily-briefs/`.*

---

## The question in the thread

After the 2026 freshet the flood-watch group asked the obvious thing:
did the river regulators do a decent job? Most replies were a bare "yes"
or "no." One was specific and checkable — Gilbert Dionne's: that the
reservoirs and basins "were not lowered to minimum operating levels by
April 1."

We pulled the public record to check it. The honest finding is that this
is **two questions wearing one coat**, and they have opposite answers:

1. Were the **storage reservoirs** drawn down before the freshet? — Mostly yes.
2. Was the **river itself** carried into the freshet at a normal winter
   level? — No.

Most of the thread argues past this distinction. It's the whole story.

## Question 1 — the storage reservoirs: mostly drawn down

There are 13 principal reservoirs in the basin. Using ORRPB's published
daily levels and each reservoir's published operating limits, here is
where each one sat on **April 1, 2026**, as a percent of its operating
band (0 % = at the low operating limit, 100 % = at the high limit):

**Drawn down hard — at or near the operating floor:**

| Reservoir | Apr 1 | | Reservoir | Apr 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kipawa | −2 % | | Dozois | 18 % |
| Des Joachims | 6 % | | Rapide-7 | 20 % |
| Poisson Blanc | 8 % | | Quinze | 24 % |
| Timiskaming | 10 % | | Lady Evelyn | 33 % |
| Baskatong | 13 % | | | |

**Not meaningfully drawn down:**

| Reservoir | Apr 1 | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mitchinamecus | 43 % | still easing down — low point was Apr 1 itself |
| Bark Lake | 43 % | its Madawaska water-management plan *schedules* refill from April 1 — partly by design |
| Kiamika | 45 % | low point Apr 2 |
| Cabonga | 60 % | the outlier — **never dropped below 54 % all winter** (low was Jan 20) |

Nine of thirteen were drawn down to a third of their band or below;
several — Kipawa, Des Joachims, Poisson Blanc, Timiskaming — were
essentially on the floor. Read as "the storage reservoirs weren't
emptied," Gilbert's claim does not hold.

**It holds even less for the upper river.** Lac Coulonge — the
Fort-Coulonge / Mansfield reach, where much of this discussion
originates — sits on the main-stem Ottawa *above* the Gatineau and
Lièvre confluences. Every storage reservoir that feeds it (Timiskaming,
Kipawa, Quinze, Rapide-7, Dozois, Lady Evelyn) was at **33 % of band or
lower** on April 1. The three under-drawn reservoirs — Cabonga, Kiamika,
Mitchinamecus — are on the Gatineau and Lièvre, which join the Ottawa
*downstream* of the upper-river communities. They never touched Lac
Coulonge's water.

Cabonga at 60 % is a real "could have given back more" — but for the
**lower** Ottawa, below the Gatineau confluence, not for the upper river.

## Question 2 — the river itself: carried in high

Drawing the storage reservoirs down is not the same thing as starting
the freshet from a normal river level. And on that second measure, the
record is clear — this is the finding of **Exhibit B (Winter Baseline)**
in the case file, and fresh ORRPB data confirms it.

Lac Coulonge's mean level over **Jan 1 – Feb 27, 2026 was 106.55 m**.
Against the prior nine winters (2017–2025) that is **+14 cm**; against
the full ~50-year published record it is **+5 cm**. Both are above
normal — the recent decade had itself been run somewhat low, so the
"+14 cm" figure is partly a comparison to a low recent baseline; the
honest range is "5 to 14 cm high depending on which baseline."

What removes the ambiguity is **persistence**. Measured against ORRPB's
own published historical daily percentiles, Lac Coulonge in 2026 sat:

- **above the historical median on 53 of 59 days** through February–March, and
- **above the 90th percentile on 41 of those 59 days.**

Spending two-thirds of the winter in the top tenth of the historical
record is not weather noise — it is a sustained operating state. The
river was being carried high, and (Exhibit B, Fig 2b) it stayed
elevated through March and into April rather than being drawn down
ahead of the melt. Exhibit B argues that an elevated winter baseline
carries forward onto the peak; whether that transfer is one-for-one is
a modelling assumption, but the elevated winter baseline itself is not
in doubt.

Lac Coulonge went on to peak at **108.63 m on April 20** — the
fourth-highest in the modern record, and about 0.4 m below the
property-survey 20-year flood line. A large freshet; not a record one.

## Why both answers can be true at once

Storage drawdown and river-reach level are **different levers**, and
they can even pull against each other. Emptying a headwater reservoir
means *releasing water* — which flows downstream and tends to hold the
main-stem reaches, Lac Coulonge among them, *up*. So "the reservoirs
were drawn down" and "the river was too high all winter" are not a
contradiction; they can both be true, and in 2026 both were.

That is exactly why the useful management question is not "draw the
reservoirs down" *or* "keep the river low." It is coordinating the two:
snowpack-indexed drawdown across the whole system — the big storage
reservoirs **and** the run-of-river reaches — so the river reaches a
normal winter level *and* the reservoirs hold buffer. That is the
case file's standing ask, and nothing in the 2026 data argues against
it.

## Where this leaves the thread

- **Gilbert Dionne** — "reservoirs not lowered to minimum by April 1":
  as a statement about *storage* it is mostly false, and entirely false
  for the reservoirs feeding the upper river. But the instinct under it
  — the river was mismanaged into the freshet too high — is sound. It is
  just better aimed at the **winter river baseline** (Exhibit B) than at
  a reservoir count.
- **Jim Coffey** — "managed the recession well, but could draw down
  earlier and lower": the recession half is consistent with the data
  (every reservoir absorbed the freshet and is now easing off 80–99 %
  full); the "earlier and lower" half is precisely the reach-baseline
  gap above.
- **Steve Deon** — "not record-breaking, a large top-5 freshet": correct,
  and consistent with the peak above — 2026 was the fourth-highest
  modern peak at Lac Coulonge, not the highest.
- The flat "NO" / "definitely not" replies, and the unqualified "YES,"
  both miss that the answer is split: **storage, mostly yes; river
  baseline, no.**

## The verdict

On the public evidence: the regulators **drew the storage reservoirs
down** — most of them hard, the upper-river ones especially — but
**carried the river itself into the freshet high**, ~5–14 cm above its
winter norm, and held it there through March. The first is competent
operation. The second is the part residents felt, and the part the data
supports them on. The fix is not a louder argument about reservoir
levels; it is system-coordinated, snowpack-indexed drawdown that
includes the river reaches, and an ORRPB that publishes the April-1
numbers so this can be audited without a scraper.

## Sources & method

- **Reservoir & river levels:** ORRPB per-location pages
  (`ottawariver.ca/location/<name>/`), daily series for 2026-01-01 →
  2026-05-22, and ORRPB's published historical daily percentile
  envelopes for Lac Coulonge.
- **Operating limits:** ORRPB per-reservoir System Constraints tables;
  "% of band" = (level − low limit) / (high limit − low limit).
- **Geography of confluences:** the Gatineau River joins the Ottawa at
  Gatineau, the Lièvre near Masson, the Madawaska at Arnprior — all
  downstream of the Fort-Coulonge / Mansfield reach.
- **Companion:** Exhibit B (Winter Baseline) in the case file, which
  treats the Lac Coulonge winter-baseline question in full.
- This note presents *computed conclusions* (percent-of-band, winter
  means, percentile counts) and credits ORRPB as the data source; it
  does not reproduce ORRPB's raw daily tables. It is a public-data
  adjudication, not an ORRPB statement. Corrections welcome — especially
  an authoritative April-1 reservoir snapshot from the operators.
