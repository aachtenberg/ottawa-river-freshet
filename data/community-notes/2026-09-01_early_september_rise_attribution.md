# The rise is real and bigger than "slightly higher." But the reservoirs did not cause it: Lake Timiskaming passed 54 percent more water while its own level never moved

**Compiled 2026-09-01, in response to Dan's September 1 comment on the Ottawa River Flood Watch page. The comment gets the direction right and understates the size: flows are not slightly higher, they are running 21 to 47 percent above the August 22 to 23 trough and 121 to 141 percent of the seasonal median. The cooler and wetter attribution checks out. The one item that needs adjusting is "increases in flows from the reservoirs have been the primary reason." On the Ottawa main stem the reservoirs are passing water through, not releasing it from storage, and the level gauges on those reservoirs are what prove it.**

## Plain language summary

Something did change in the last ten days, and anyone who noticed it was not imagining it.

Through the middle of summer the upper Ottawa was genuinely low. Between July 10 and August 20, Lake Timiskaming's outflow ran below the seasonal median on 40 of 42 days, and Otto Holden on 39 of 42. That is the "previous low levels" the comment refers to, and it was a real upper-basin condition rather than a basin-wide one. Over that same stretch Britannia, down at Ottawa, was below median on only 13 of 42 days.

Starting around August 22 that reversed. Lake Timiskaming's outflow went from 399 to 484 cubic metres per second, Otto Holden from 419 to 528, Des Joachims from 434 to 543. Bryson is up 47 percent. Every gauge on the main stem is now running above its seasonal median for the date, most of them by 30 percent or more.

The weather explanation is correct. Compared with the two weeks before it, the last two weeks of August were about 2 degrees cooler basin-wide and considerably wetter in the places that matter for runoff: North Bay went from 18.7 mm to 64.7 mm, Rouyn from 51.1 to 92.2, Maniwaki from 36.9 to 81.9. Cooler air also means less water lost to evaporation and to trees, so more of the rain that falls actually reaches the river.

The part worth correcting is the idea that reservoir releases drove this. There is a simple test. A reservoir that is releasing water out of storage goes down. A reservoir that is merely passing along water arriving from upstream stays where it is. Lake Timiskaming increased its outflow by 54 percent between August 18 and August 30, and across that entire window its level moved inside a 9 centimetre band and finished within 3 centimetres of where it started. The Quinze reservoir did the same thing: outflow up 27 percent, level flat. Those reservoirs did not give up this water. They received it and handed it on.

There is one genuine exception, and it is on the Gatineau rather than the Ottawa. Baskatong has increased its outflow from 152 to 248 cubic metres per second and has paid for it, dropping 39 centimetres in 13 days. But its neighbours moved the opposite way over the same period: Poisson Blanc cut its outflow by 48 percent and Cabonga is down to 7. So even on the Gatineau the reservoir contribution is partly self-cancelling.

The short version: rain and cool weather did this, and the reservoirs mostly got out of the way.

One practical note for anyone watching a gauge and seeing nothing. The rise shows up clearly on the free-flowing reaches and not at all on the regulated head ponds, because the head ponds are held steady on purpose. Carillon sat between 41.11 and 41.12 metres for fourteen straight days while the flow through it changed. That is not a broken gauge. It is what a regulated pool is supposed to do.

## The reservoir test, in numbers

This is the part of the note worth keeping, because the same question comes up every time the river rises.

If a reservoir is the source of extra water downstream, its own level has to fall. If the level holds while outflow climbs, the water is arriving from upstream and the operator is simply passing it along.

| Reservoir | Outflow, Aug 18 to Aug 31 | Level over the same window | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timiskaming** | 314 to 484 m³/s, **+54%** | 179.16 to 179.18 m, entire range 9 cm | **Pass-through.** Not the source |
| **Quinze** | 200 to 252 m³/s, **+26%** | 262.92 to 262.96 m, range 5 cm | **Pass-through.** Not the source |
| **Rapide-7** | 161 to 157 m³/s, flat | 308.93 to 308.97 m | No contribution |
| **Dozois** | 142 to 166 m³/s, +17% | 343.74 to 343.60 m, down 14 cm | Small genuine contribution |
| **Baskatong** (Gatineau) | 152 to 248 m³/s, **+63%** | 221.39 to 221.00 m, **down 39 cm** | **Genuine drawdown.** The real exception |
| **Poisson Blanc** (Gatineau) | 172 to 90 m³/s, **−48%** | 200.58 to 200.03 m | Cutting back, offsets Baskatong |
| **Cabonga** (Gatineau) | 21 to 7 m³/s, **−67%** | falling slowly | Cutting back |

Net effect on the Gatineau at Rapides-Farmers: 204 m³/s on August 22 to 296 on August 31, a genuine 45 percent increase, but that is Baskatong's storage plus 81.9 mm of rain at Maniwaki, partly offset by the two reservoirs pulling the other way.

Net effect on the Ottawa main stem from reservoir operations: close to nothing. The upper-basin increase is inflow.

## Current standing against thresholds

Nothing here is close to anything.

- **Lac Coulonge** 106.22 m at 13:00 UTC September 1, etat 0 (fully normal), about 78 cm below the 107.00 m pre-alert threshold and 128 cm below the 107.50 m minor flood line.
- **Hull dock** (1-3675) 41.40 m, well below the 42.61 m servitude trigger, so the Carillon section 15.3.5.1 ceiling is not in force. Carillon amont is sitting at 41.12 m regardless.
- **Bryson** is passing 527 m³/s with turbines at zero and 100 percent of flow over the spillway. Under run-of-river operation total flow is conserved, so this is a generation decision and not a flood-management move, and there is no downstream gap. It does mean more water than usual on the spillway side for anyone on the water near the structure.

## What we cannot say

- **Why any individual operator moved.** The telemetry shows the moves, not the reasoning. Baskatong's drawdown is consistent with generation scheduling, with fall refill positioning, and with routine recession management. We cannot separate them.
- **Whether it continues.** This is a rain-funded interruption of a summer recession. Without further precipitation the recession resumes. The ORRPB forecast updates Mondays around 4 PM Eastern and is the right place to look for the outlook rather than this note.
- **Anything about September 1 itself.** See the caveat below.

## Data caveat on the September 1 numbers

The most recent ORRPB day is preliminary and frequently revised. In today's slot both Otto Holden and Des Joachims publish a flow of **0**, which is a placeholder rather than a reading, and Chenaux (347) and Chats Falls (551) both drop sharply against their August 31 values (613 and 719) in a way that reads as a partial-day artifact.

Every comparison in this note is therefore anchored on **August 31**, with September 1 treated as provisional. This is the same failure mode that produced the August 5 "mid-valley surge" headline that had to be retracted the following day. See [Mid-valley surge correction](2026-08-06_mid_valley_surge_correction.md).

## Source and methodology

- **Flows and levels at Témiscaming, Otto Holden, Des Joachims, Chenaux, Chats Falls, Britannia, Carillon, Pembroke, Grenville, Thurso and Lake Coulonge**: ORRPB published daily series and the ORRPB per-station historical median (1991 to 2020), retrieved via the project's `orrpb_river_flows`, `orrpb_river_levels` and `orrpb_station_history` ingesters. Per ORRPB's terms this note presents derived statistics only (changes, percentages of median, day counts), not the raw daily tables.
- **Reservoir levels and outflows**: `reservoir_readings`, agencies as published (PSPC/WSC for Timiskaming, HQ for Baskatong, Cabonga, Dozois and Rapide-7, MELCC for Quinze and Kipawa, EG for Poisson Blanc).
- **Release splits at Bryson (3-46), Rapides-Farmers (3-67) and Carillon (3-60)**, and **levels at Hull dock (1-3675) and Carillon amont (1-2968)**: Hydro-Québec hourly open data, aggregated to daily means.
- **Lac Coulonge live level**: Quebec Vigilance station 1195, hourly.
- **Temperature and precipitation**: ECCC climate daily, stations Barrage Témiscamingue, North Bay A, Rouyn, Val-d'Or, Parent, Maniwaki Airport and Pembroke Climate. Windows are the 14 days August 18 to 31 against the 14 days August 4 to 17. Pembroke and Ottawa CDA have incomplete coverage in the earlier window and their comparison is weaker as a result.
- Reservoir level readings are daily values at 00:00 UTC. Flow comparisons use August 18 as the pre-change baseline because that is the last day before the Témiscaming rise begins.
