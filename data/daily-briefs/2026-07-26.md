## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Outflow from Lake Timiscaming — the large natural lake at the top of the Ottawa River basin — ticked up slightly today compared to yesterday's provisional low, reaching about 333–354 cubic metres per second depending on the data source (two independent measurements; the difference is within normal data uncertainty for the most-recent provisional day). The flow is still well below the "sub-400" threshold it first crossed fourteen days ago, and it remains barely twelve percent of the early-May freshet peak. This is a minor uptick from yesterday's trough rather than any new trend; the overall picture is late-summer baseflow conditions with gentle day-to-day oscillation.

The fourteen tracked storage reservoirs across the basin are all sitting within two centimetres of yesterday's levels — uniformly flat. This is the expected late-summer pattern: inflows and outflows are closely balanced, and there is no bulk absorption or release of storage. The town gauge at Mattawa, a mid-valley station about halfway down the river, showed a notable drop of sixteen centimetres today to 152.17 metres. That is a large single-day change for this station, which has been oscillating in a narrow two-to-four centimetre band all week; the number should be treated as provisional and may be revised upward tomorrow. The Pembroke gauge, further downstream, showed essentially no change (down one centimetre), which is inconsistent with a genuine large-scale flow reduction arriving from upstream — supporting the likelihood that the Mattawa reading is a data artifact. We will confirm in tomorrow's brief.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge — measuring Lac Coulonge directly, several kilometres upstream of the Bryson Generating Station via a winding and narrowing channel — rose a further five centimetres overnight and through the day to reach 106.08 metres at 4 PM local time. The ORRPB midnight average confirms the same: 106.08 metres, up five centimetres from yesterday's revised midnight value. This is now a second consecutive day of rising after approximately three weeks of gradual decline. At 106.08 metres, the lake remains well within normal summer conditions — it would need to climb nearly ninety-two centimetres to reach the lowest flood-advisory threshold of 107.00 metres. No flood concern.

At the Bryson Generating Station, which sits a good distance downstream of where Lac Coulonge's south end narrows and winds to the dam, operators partially reversed yesterday's dramatic spill cut. Recall that yesterday evening the flow over the spillway was slashed from about 90 cubic metres per second to about 41 — a more-than-halving. Today the spill has been restored to about 67 cubic metres per second, recovering roughly two-thirds of the previous cut. As a result, the dam pond has essentially levelled off: it gained only one centimetre since yesterday (reaching 105.58 metres), halting the rapid twenty-centimetre rise that set a new all-time high yesterday. The dam pond remains ninety-one centimetres above its normal operating ceiling of 104.67 metres and has been above that ceiling for fifty-nine consecutive days — but the rate of rise has stopped for now.

The hydraulic sequence is worth noting plainly: yesterday evening's spill cut caused the lake upstream to rise overnight (lake now at 106.08 m); today's spill restoration has stabilised the dam pond. If the restored spill is sustained, the lake should begin to ease again. The ORRPB forecast is unchanged from July 21 and calls for stable conditions through the coming week. No flood watch; no precipitation flag.

## Notes

- **Bryson spill oscillation context:** In two days, operators have cut spill by 54% and then restored about two-thirds of what was cut. The headpond ended those two days with a net gain of 1 cm (still at an all-time high). The most likely interpretation is a short tactical adjustment — possibly related to grid dispatch scheduling — rather than a deliberate policy decision to impound or release storage. The 58-day-and-counting breach of the operating band ceiling is the structural condition; the daily swings in spill are the operating tactics within that elevated context.

- **Lac Coulonge two-day rise — hydraulic cause:** The connection between Bryson's spill cut yesterday and the lake's two-day rise is consistent with the hydraulic link between the lake and the dam pond. Bryson's cut reduced the draw on the upstream channel; water that would have flowed through the dam backed up slightly. With spill restored today, this backing-up effect should diminish and the lake should stabilise or begin to ease — though there is lag in the system. Tomorrow's readings will clarify.

- **Mattawa suspect reading:** A −16 cm single-day drop at Mattawa with no corresponding signal at Pembroke is anomalous. The prior brief already noted that Jul25's provisional Mattawa reading (152.41 m) was revised down 8 cm in today's data (to 152.33 m).
