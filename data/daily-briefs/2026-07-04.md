## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Témiscaming outflow — where the upper Ottawa River basin drains southward out of Lake Timiscaming — continued rising on July 4, reaching 935 cubic metres per second according to the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's daily series, with an independent PSPC cross-check confirming 927 m³/s. This is the highest daily figure since the main spring freshet peak more than two months ago. The pattern of upward revisions to preliminary values continues: the July 3 reading, which was reported in yesterday's brief at 862 m³/s, has now been revised upward to 903 m³/s in the database — making today's 935 the second consecutive day above 900 m³/s. The precipitation event the ORRPB flagged in its July 2 update is playing out larger than early measurements suggested.

Mattawa — a town roughly midway down the valley, about two-thirds of the way from Lake Timiscaming to the dam chain — appears to have crested, at least for this wave. Its measured level eased 5 centimetres overnight to 152.88 metres, ending a four-day streak of consecutive rises that peaked at 152.93 metres yesterday (the highest level recorded in this monitoring series). Pembroke, further downstream, is still rising, up another 9 centimetres today to 111.69 metres. The pattern — crest passed Mattawa, still building at Pembroke — is the classic signature of a pulse moving through the valley. Reservoir operators are passing the surge downstream rather than absorbing it: four of the fourteen tracked storage reservoirs are now falling, ten are flat, and none are rising.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The government gauge at the Mansfield waterfront on Lac Coulonge — the monitoring point on the lake itself, well upstream of and separate from the dam downstream — recorded a markedly larger jump today than on the preceding three days. The 4 PM Eastern reading of 106.54 metres was 14 centimetres above the same hour yesterday, roughly double the pace of the preceding two days. The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's midnight series confirms the acceleration: the lake registered 106.52 metres at midnight (preliminary), up 14 centimetres from the revised midnight level of 106.38 metres the night before. That is the fourth consecutive midnight rise and the largest single overnight gain in this episode. The lake remains fully in the normal flood category — 46 centimetres below the pre-alert advisory threshold of 107.00 metres — but the rate of rise has clearly quickened. Property owners on Lac Coulonge should expect continued increases through at least the July 7 ORRPB update.

The day's most operationally notable development is at the Bryson Generating Station — the dam well downstream of the Mansfield properties, where the south end of Lac Coulonge's channel eventually narrows and reaches the dam. Two stations in the regulated chain feeding the Bryson dam pond cut their releases sharply today: the Rapide-2 station dropped from about 250 cubic metres per second to about 157, and the Rapide-7 station dropped from about 256 to about 155 — each a roughly 38% cut, sustained steadily since at least 2 PM Eastern. Bryson simultaneously stepped up its own total discharge by 8%, driven almost entirely by increased spill (now 54% of total release). Despite these combined adjustments, the dam pond above Bryson still rose a further 7 centimetres to 105.48 metres today, extending the dam pond's exceedance of its 104.67-metre permitted operating ceiling to 37 consecutive days and 81 centimetres above ceiling. The natural runoff arriving at the Bryson vicinity from the Lac Coulonge watershed during this precipitation event is currently outpacing the combined effect of reduced upstream inputs and increased spill. Whether the Mattawa crest having passed will translate into easing inflow at the Bryson dam pond in the coming days remains to be seen — travel time through the cascade is variable.
