## In plain language

The Ottawa basin continues its steady summer drawdown, though today's numbers carry a significant correction to yesterday's brief. The outflow from Lake Timiscaming was reported yesterday as having crossed below 400 cubic metres per second for the first time since spring — but that preliminary figure has now been revised upward to 403 cubic metres per second. Today's preliminary reading comes in at 430 cubic metres per second, a slight apparent uptick from the revised yesterday. Both numbers sit comfortably in the 400–450 range that is characteristic of mid-July summer baseflow; the basin is undeniably in full summer recession, but the sub-400 milestone is still just ahead, not behind us. This is now Day 72 since the primary freshet peak in early May, and Day 6 of outflows below 700 cubic metres per second.

A similar correction applies to yesterday's Mattawa reading. The preliminary data had suggested a dramatic 23-centimetre single-day drop at the gauge roughly two-thirds of the way between Lake Timiscaming and the dam chain — but the confirmed value shows the lake actually fell only 4 centimetres from July 10 to July 11. Today's preliminary reading shows Mattawa down another 14 centimetres, which is more in line with the gradual upper-valley recession one would expect. Pembroke, another 130 kilometres downstream, fell 5 centimetres — Day 5 of its gentle, steady decline.

Of the fourteen tracked basin storage reservoirs, one (Lake Timiscaming itself) is clearly falling as its outflow outpaces the inflow from above, while one (the Des Joachims forebay, where an Ontario Power Generation station sits on the mid-Ottawa) shows a notable 8-centimetre rise, likely reflecting an operational dispatch cycle. The other twelve are essentially flat. This is normal summer management: operators are not aggressively building or releasing storage, simply letting the system find its natural level.
