## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Today marks Day 40 of the recession from the May 2 freshet peak. The flow past Témiscaming — the gauge that captures the entire upper Ottawa basin — dropped to 632 cubic metres per second, the second consecutive day below 700 m³/s and the lowest reading of the spring season. For comparison, the outflow at that same gauge was nearly 2,741 m³/s at the May 2 peak. The basin has shed roughly three quarters of that volume in six weeks, now approaching typical early-summer base conditions.

The main-stem station at Mattawa, halfway down the valley from the upper basin, is essentially flat at 152.51 metres — the slight uptick over the past two days in the official database is consistent with the increased water being routed through the Hydro-Québec cascade further downstream rather than any new precipitation influx. Pembroke, further downstream, continues its gradual decline to 111.60 metres. The basin's 14 tracked storage reservoirs are exceptionally quiet: Lake Témiscaming itself is the only one showing any meaningful movement — a 3-centimetre rise at both its independent gauges — while twelve are flat within measurement noise, and none are falling. The rising lake level is slightly counterintuitive given that the outflow exceeds the inflow from the upstream Quinze dam, but is explained by significant additional tributary and shoreline drainage still reaching the lake directly.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property pulled back slightly today after two days of rising: the live 4 PM reading was 106.36 metres, about 3 centimetres below yesterday afternoon's level. The official overnight reading showed the midnight level at 106.38 metres — so the lake appears to have peaked sometime late June 9 and has since eased. Either way, the lake is fully normal: more than 60 centimetres below even the lowest preliminary notice level (107.00 m). The regional authority's forecast, last updated June 9 and unchanged today, calls for levels along the main stem to remain fairly stable or decrease gradually through next week. The next scheduled update is June 16.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — showed the first drop in five consecutive days, falling 11 centimetres to 105.33 metres by 3 PM. This is a significant directional change. The headpond remains 66 centimetres above the top of its normal 47-centimetre operating range, so the abnormal condition that has persisted since May 29 is not over — but the trend has shifted. Hydro-Québec appears to have made a major operational move today: the flow through the upstream dam immediately above Bryson (Rapide-7) increased by about 22% compared to the same time yesterday, while Bryson itself continued passing nearly as much water as it did yesterday, with 45% flowing through the spillway rather than the turbines. The combination of Lac Coulonge easing slightly and the cascade routing more water has been just enough to tip the headpond from rising to falling. At Carillon, the basin-terminal dam near Hawkesbury, total releases fell another 10% and the spillway remains closed, indicating no downstream flood pressure.

- **Mattawa data revision note.** The DB shows large revisions at Mattawa: June 8 revised from 152.58 to 152.45 m (−13 cm). This is operationally rational given the headpond situation: the bottleneck is at Bryson/Lac Coulonge, not at Carillon.
