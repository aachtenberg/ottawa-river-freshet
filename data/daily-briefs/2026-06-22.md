## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The surge that had been building at the head of the watershed for five days has crested and is retreating. Outflow from Lake Temiscaming — the river's largest natural lake, straddling the Ontario-Quebec border — peaked at 843 cubic metres per second on June 21 and fell back to 761 m³/s today, a 10% pullback. This is almost certainly a preliminary figure and could be revised slightly, but the directional signal is clear: after six straight days above 700 m³/s, the pulse that rainfall earlier in the week delivered to the upper basin is now draining through. The two major generating stations feeding the mid-river reach — the Quinze facility just downstream of Lake Temiscaming and the Rapide-7 facility further down — stepped down sharply in a single day, by 14.5% and 18.2% respectively, consistent with operators reducing output as the inflow spike subsides. The large storage reservoirs across the basin remain largely unchanged: thirteen of the fourteen impoundments tracked held within two centimetres of yesterday's level.

The pulse that left Temiscaming did not vanish — it is now arriving at Mattawa, roughly 200 kilometres downstream. The midnight level there jumped four centimetres to 152.78 metres, exactly matching the high-water mark of the third rise reached on June 19. The two-day decline that followed that June 19 peak has been entirely erased. Whether this becomes a fourth distinct rise or simply a plateau before conditions resume downward will depend on what's still in transit from Temiscaming — but since the Temiscaming surge peaked yesterday and is already retreating, the pulse currently at Mattawa likely represents the bulk of what is coming. The next 24–48 hours should clarify the direction. Pembroke, in mid-valley, edged barely down (2 cm), while Britannia, near Ottawa, jumped a notable 7 cm — downstream of the system's main headwater pulse, likely responding to elevated flow arriving from the Carillon reach.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at Lac Coulonge proper read 106.33 metres at 4 PM — essentially unchanged from yesterday, rising by less than one centimetre in the past 24 hours. With 67 centimetres of separation from the pre-alert level at 107 metres, this body of water is firmly in normal territory. The slow, gentle upward drift of the past week continues, but at this pace, there is no near-term concern for lakeshore properties.

Today brought the first genuine positive development at the dam pond in front of the Bryson Generating Station, the backed-up water surface sitting at the foot of Lac Coulonge. After reaching a new all-time high of 105.56 metres yesterday, the pond dropped nine centimetres today — its biggest single-day decline since the breach began 25 days ago. The permitted upper ceiling for operating the pond is 104.67 metres; at 105.47 metres, the pond still sits 80 centimetres above that ceiling, so the situation remains extraordinary, but the trend has finally reversed. The operators released slightly more water today (515 m³/s vs 494 m³/s yesterday, all still through the open spillway), and with the upstream feed from the Quinze and Rapide-7 stations stepping down sharply, the conditions for continued pond reduction are in place. The dam's turbines have now been fully offline for eight consecutive days. No public timeline for restart has been announced.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast has been unchanged since June 17. Tomorrow, June 23, is the board's next scheduled update — the first in six days — which will clarify whether the board is maintaining the weekly freshet cadence or shifting to its lower-frequency summer monitoring posture.
