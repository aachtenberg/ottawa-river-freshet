## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin's post-peak recession reached its 44th day today. The outlet of Lake Timiskaming — the gateway gauge where the northern half of the basin drains into the Ottawa River — registered 673 cubic metres per second, the sixth straight day below 700 m³/s and well into the long tail of the freshet. The same station was releasing 773 m³/s one week ago. Both tracking sources (the regional authority and the federal monitoring service) now agree on that figure. Note that the June 13 reading — which was reported as 666 m³/s in yesterday's brief — has been revised down to 642 m³/s by both sources; today's 673 m³/s is a preliminary figure that may also be revised.

The mid-valley precipitation pulse — the surge of runoff from the June 9–10 rain event that had been pushing down the system for the past several days — has now passed through the Mattawa gauge station, roughly halfway between the northern basin and Lake Coulonge. After rising about 18 centimetres over two days, the Mattawa level fell 13 centimetres today to 152.38 metres, and the June 13 midnight reading has been revised down from the 152.62 metres reported in real time to 152.51 metres, confirming the peak came and went during the day. The pulse has now cleared Mattawa and is working through the Des Joachims reservoir on the mid-Ottawa. The level at Otto-Holden — just below Des Joachims — jumped 12 centimetres today, a clear confirmation that the pulse has propagated past the reservoir and is now in the reach between Des Joachims and Pembroke. Pembroke remained essentially flat (down just 2 centimetres), suggesting the leading edge of that pulse has not yet reached it.

The basin's reservoir inventory is very quiet. All fourteen tracked waterbodies showed changes within 2 centimetres overnight. Two — Lake Timiskaming and Bark Lake — were right at the 2-centimetre boundary on the rising side, consistent with normal refill posture during recession. No active large-scale storage filling or release surges are underway.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property effectively paused today. The 4 PM water level (106.23 metres) was essentially identical to yesterday afternoon — up just half a centimetre — while the official midnight reading ticked up 3 centimetres (from 106.23 to 106.26 metres). The gentle multi-day decline appears to have stalled, which is consistent with the mid-valley pulse now propagating through the reach above. The property remains 77 centimetres below the lowest alert threshold of 107 metres, so there is no flood concern; the stall is simply the pulse starting to arrive.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — held flat today at 105.34 metres, the same reading as last night. This is the 17th consecutive day that the headpond has been above its normal operating band, which tops out at 104.67 metres. The pond is currently 67 centimetres above that ceiling. After the 8-centimetre reversal yesterday, today at least brought stability rather than further deterioration. The total water flow through the dam was essentially unchanged (down less than 1 cubic metre per second), suggesting Hydro-Québec held its operating posture steady.

The most notable operating event today happened at the far end of the river — the Carillon dam near Hawkesbury, the last major structure before the Ottawa joins the St. Lawrence. Carillon cut its total water releases by more than 8 per cent, from about 2,280 to about 2,092 cubic metres per second. That is the largest single-day reduction at this terminal dam in recent weeks. The probable reason is that inflow from the Gatineau River tributary (which enters just above Carillon) was declining, so Carillon trimmed output in step. When Carillon releases less water, the river above it backs up slightly; the gauge at the Hull/Gatineau waterfront, just upstream, rose 5 centimetres today as a result. All of this remains well below any regulatory trigger or flood concern, but the operating shift is worth tracking.

The regional authority's forecast — unchanged since June 9 — still describes conditions as "fairly stable or decreasing." The next forecast update is due **tomorrow, June 16 at 4 PM ET**. That update should reflect the mid-valley pulse, the Des Joachims pulse now propagating to Pembroke and beyond, and any updated outlook for Lake Coulonge.
