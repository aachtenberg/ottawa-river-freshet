The Témiscaming outlet — where Lake Timiscaming drains into the Ottawa River — is running at a provisional 377 cubic metres per second today, up from the confirmed 335 yesterday. Today marks Day 101 since the spring-freshet peak of roughly 2,741 cubic metres per second in early May, and Day 30 of continuous flow below 400 cubic metres per second. Pembroke, a mid-valley gauge about 200 kilometres downstream of Lake Timiscaming, slipped another 8 centimetres overnight to 111.09 metres, continuing the expected gradual post-freshet decline. Mattawa, closer to Lake Timiscaming, measured 152.39 metres — up 15 centimetres from yesterday's overnight figure per the ORRPB conditions table, though Mattawa has large day-to-day swings driven by overnight pool storage at upstream dams, so this jump is not unusual for that station.

The mid-valley from Otto Holden downstream to Des Joachims — two Ontario Power Generation stations between Lake Coulonge and Pembroke — is showing elevated flows in today's provisional data. The provisional daily average at Des Joachims stands at 676 cubic metres per second, and the live afternoon reading at 3 PM Eastern time reached 791 cubic metres per second. Otto Holden shows 489 cubic metres per second provisional and 619 cubic metres per second live. These are substantially above yesterday's confirmed values (421 and 381 cubic metres per second respectively), but they are within the range of recent confirmed figures — Des Joachims was confirmed at 733 cubic metres per second just two days ago. Four independent data-quality checks were run on both stations (whether the daily-average figure is feasible, where the extra water would have to come from, whether pool levels are consistent with the claimed release, and whether downstream gauges show a corresponding response). All four checks passed at both stations. The most plausible driver is elevated tributary inflow — particularly the Coulonge River drainage, which feeds into the Ottawa reach between these two stations. Notably, the Otto Holden pool rose 23 centimetres overnight despite elevated releases, which means tributary inflows were actually exceeding outflows — real water coming in, not a data artifact. These are still provisional daily averages and will finalize overnight; tomorrow's brief will compare them to the confirmed values.

All 14 tracked storage reservoirs showed negligible change overnight — within a couple of centimetres — consistent with normal post-freshet summer drainage.
