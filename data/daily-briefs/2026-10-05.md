## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa River basin continues its quiet autumn recession. Outflow from Lake Timiskaming finalized at 318 cubic metres per second for October 4 — the 17th straight day below 400 m³/s since September 18 — and today's midnight reading from the reservoir gauge shows approximately 363 m³/s, a modest uptick that keeps the streak running at 18 days provisionally. We are 156 days past the May 2 seasonal peak of 2,741 m³/s.

Reservoir storage is stable and draining slowly: four of fourteen tracked reservoirs fell modestly overnight while ten held flat. No reservoirs are actively rising. This is normal autumn drawdown behaviour.

The mid-valley reach again produced implausible provisional figures at two stations today: Des Joachims and Otto Holden reported 147 and 126 m³/s respectively, the same failure mode as October 4. The pools at both locations are actually rising slightly overnight — which would be physically impossible at those claimed outflow levels with over 300 m³/s flowing in from upstream — confirming these are partial-day data artifacts. Final values will post tomorrow in the 300–340 m³/s range, consistent with recent days.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge is holding flat at 105.87 metres tonight — the sixth straight day at essentially 105.86 m and 113 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold. Fully normal, etat 0, nothing to report for the property.

A notable operational change happened at the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — well downstream of Lac Coulonge, past the lake's southern narrows and a long winding reach — around late morning today. Between approximately 11 AM and noon Eastern time, operators stopped routing any water through the spillway. All 289 cubic metres per second is now passing through the turbines, where it had been split roughly 60/40 between turbines and spillway for weeks. The total amount of water flowing downstream is essentially unchanged — the downstream effect is nil. This appears to be a seasonal operating adjustment, routing all flow through the turbines as autumn levels settle in.
