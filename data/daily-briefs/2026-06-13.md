## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin's post-peak recession entered its 43rd day today. The outlet of Lake Timiskaming — the gateway gauge for the northern half of the basin — registered around 666 cubic metres per second, its fifth straight day below 700 m³/s and a long way from the 2,700+ peak in early May. The week-over-week picture shows the steady unwind: the same station was releasing 782 m³/s seven days ago.

The mid-valley reach is telling a more active story. For the second consecutive day, the Mattawa gauge — roughly halfway between the upper basin and Lac Coulonge — jumped significantly: 9 centimetres today, after rising 10 centimetres yesterday. The pulse from the June 9–10 precipitation event is still working its way downstream. It has now reached the large mid-valley reservoir known as Des Joachims, just below Mattawa: operators there released 14% more water than the previous day, yet the reservoir level still climbed 3 centimetres — meaning the incoming flow was so high it exceeded even the increased release. The Pembroke gauge, the next station downstream, was essentially flat (down just 1 centimetre), and Lac Coulonge continued its own gradual decline. The pulse has not yet reached Pembroke or the property's lake; travel time suggests it could arrive at Pembroke in one to two days and at Lac Coulonge in two to four days thereafter.

The basin's reservoir inventory is calm: two waterbodies are rising modestly (Poisson Blanc and Des Joachims, 3 centimetres each), and the other twelve tracked sites are flat. No dramatic refill or drawdown signals anywhere.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property eased further today: the 4 PM reading was 106.23 metres, down about 3.5 centimetres from the same hour yesterday. That puts it 77 centimetres below the lowest early-warning threshold — firmly in normal territory with no flood concern. The gradual decline is expected to continue for the next day or two, though the mid-valley pulse now propagating through Des Joachims may slow or temporarily reverse it when it arrives.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — produced the day's notable development. After stalling last week and barely moving yesterday (down just 1 centimetre), the headpond reversed direction overnight and rose 8 centimetres, from 105.26 to 105.34 metres. This extends what is now a 16-day breach of the dam's normal operating band (which runs up to 104.67 metres): the pond is now 67 centimetres above the upper bound. The recovery progress made since the June 9 event peak has been partially erased. The probable cause is straightforward: Hydro-Québec reduced total water releases through the dam by about 25 cubic metres per second (approximately 5%) compared to yesterday, and almost entirely by cutting back the spillway rather than the turbines. When the release falls below what is flowing in from the lake above, the pond backs up. This creates no new flood risk for the property — Lac Coulonge itself is well below any alert threshold — but the operating-band exceedance is now growing rather than closing.

The Carillon dam at the basin's terminus released 2,280 cubic metres per second today (down about 34 from yesterday), with no spillway use. The regional authority's forecast, unchanged since June 9 and not due for refresh until June 16, calls for "fairly stable or decreasing" levels along the main stem. That description fits Lac Coulonge and the lower reaches, but it was written before the mid-valley pulse became visible, and it doesn't anticipate the Des Joachims surge now propagating through the system.
