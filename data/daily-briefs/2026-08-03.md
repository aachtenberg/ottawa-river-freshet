## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin is quiet today. The flow out of Lake Timiscaming — the large lake on the Quebec-Ontario border that is the central pulse-point of the upper watershed — measured 348 cubic metres per second for August 3, down fractionally from the revised 344 cubic metres per second confirmed yesterday. The basin is now on its twenty-second straight day below 400 cubic metres per second, and the broader trajectory remains one of very slow general recession, though the last week has seen the flow oscillate in the 315–348 range rather than marching cleanly downward. There is no dramatic upper-basin development today: reservoir levels across the headwaters — Baskatong, Cabonga, Quinze, Dozois — are essentially flat, with six of fourteen tracked reservoirs rising modestly (most by 4–5 centimetres) and only one, the Kiamika reservoir northeast of Montpellier, rising enough (11 cm) to approach the threshold that would signal deliberate basin-wide water retention.

The gauge at Mattawa — where the Mattawa River meets the Ottawa and the mid-valley reach effectively begins — published a preliminary reading of 152.20 metres in the database, but the ORRPB's own live page shows a confirmed midnight level of 152.42 metres for August 3, continuing a pattern where the database's morning provisional estimate runs approximately 20 centimetres low. The confirmed level is a slight uptick from August 2's confirmed 152.40 metres, and from August 1's 152.36 — three consecutive confirmed days of gentle rise at Mattawa, suggesting a modest pulse of additional flow is moving through the mid-valley. Pembroke, further downstream, is holding flat at 111.08 metres.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The news at the lake and the immediate property stretch is calm. The gauge at Fort-Coulonge reads 106.09 metres as of 5 PM local time — essentially unchanged from yesterday, and still 91 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold of 107.00 metres. There is no flood concern and the lake is not reacting to the dramatic events unfolding downstream (more on that below). The ORRPB midnight level for August 3 is 106.09 metres, up just 1 centimetre from 106.08 metres the night before.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — which sits well downstream of where the south end of Lac Coulonge narrows into the river channel, well removed from the lakefront properties at Mansfield and Fort-Coulonge — fell 6 centimetres today to 105.34 metres. Operators increased total discharge by 6.8% compared to yesterday, pushing the overall flow from around 432 to 461 cubic metres per second, and they did so primarily by opening the spillway further: spillway flow jumped 23% (from 137 to 168 cubic metres per second) while turbine output held essentially flat at 293 cubic metres per second. The bigger headpond drawdown (−6 cm, versus only −1 cm yesterday) is consistent with this stepped-up release. The headpond remains 67 centimetres above the normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres — Day 67 of a continuous exceedance that began in late May — but the direction has shifted: operators are now releasing more water than the lake is delivering upstream, and the pond is slowly drawing down.

The biggest story in today's brief is not at the property at all but at the basin's terminal dam, 300-plus kilometres to the southeast. Carillon — the last dam before the Ottawa River reaches the St. Lawrence — is releasing more than 2,200 cubic metres per second today, essentially double yesterday's 1,131. A significant summer rain pulse hit the mid-valley and lower Gatineau drainage overnight and through this morning, driving an extraordinary surge at every station from Des Joachims through Carillon. The flow at the Gatineau River's mouth (at the Rapides-des-Farmers station, just above where the Gatineau joins the Ottawa) jumped 55% to 313 cubic metres per second, and Carillon doubled. This event is not driven by the upper basin — Témiscaming is barely changed — and it is not posing any flood threat at Lac Coulonge, which sits well upstream of the affected reach and is insulated by the Bryson dam. However, the Hull gauge (at the Gatineau/Hull waterfront, directly upstream of Carillon) rose 15 centimetres overnight to 41.50 metres and is now being watched: it would need to reach 42.61 metres before the spring-flood operating directive for Carillon comes back into effect. At today's level, it is 111 centimetres away.

## Notes

- **Rain event mechanics.** The Aug 3 surge at Carillon appears to be driven by a combination of: (a) the Gatineau River drainage responding to overnight/morning precipitation (Poisson Blanc +9 cm, Kiamika +11 cm both consistent with rain on the Gatineau headwaters; Gatineau at Chelsea 291 m³/s); (b) the distributed mid-valley tributary network (South Nation 152, Rideau 139, Rouge 103 m³/s) contributing to the lower Ottawa reach above Carillon; and (c) operator decisions at Des Joachims, Chats Falls, and Chenaux to release more water as inflows rose. The upper Ottawa (above Des Joachims/Témiscaming) is not the source: Témiscaming flows barely changed (344→348 m³/s).

- **ORRPB forecast obsolescence.** The current ORRPB forecast (last updated July 28) explicitly references "today and tomorrow" rain for July 28–29. The Aug 3 event is a new weather system not covered by that forecast. The Aug4 update (expected 4 PM EDT tomorrow) will be the first authoritative ORRPB commentary on the current conditions, including whether the Hull dock rise and Carillon surge are expected to continue or peak. The Aug4 forecast is the key watch-item for tomorrow's brief.

- **DB orrpb_river_flows pipeline issue.** The 399 m³/s reading for Aug3 Témiscaming in the DB table (`orrpb_river_flows`) appears to be an ingestion artifact — perhaps the pipeline grabbed an intermediate ORRPB data point before the daily average was updated, or picked up a different station's value. All three independent checks (ORRPB page table, ORRPB real-time feed, PSPC reservoir reading) agree the correct value is 348 m³/s ±9. The pipeline should be reviewed to prevent future bad ingestions from entering the historical record without a revision flag.

- **Bryson headpond direction change.** The −6 cm drawdown today (105.40→105.34 m) is the first meaningful multi-cm pullback since the July 27 spill surge (which briefly dropped the pond 27 cm). The spill increase from 137 to 168 m³/s is the driver. If operators maintain elevated spill through the basin event, the headpond could draw down noticeably over the coming days. At 67 cm above the 104.67 m ceiling, however, operating-band compliance remains far off.

- **Des Joachims zero-outflow artifact resolved.** Yesterday's brief flagged that Des Joachims reported 0 m³/s outflow for two days while its pool rose 5 cm. Today the ORRPB live and DB both show 765 m³/s outflow from Des Joachims — confirming the prior zero was a telemetry/reporting lag, not a real dam closure.

(b) Will Hull dock rise continue toward 42.61 m trigger, or stabilize as the pulse passes? (c) Will Bryson continue to draw down the headpond with elevated spill?
