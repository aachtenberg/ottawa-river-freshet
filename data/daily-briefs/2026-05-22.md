## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The flow leaving Lake Temiscaming — the upper Ottawa's main gauge of how much water is pushing downstream — dropped to approximately 1,496 cubic metres per second today, the 10th straight day of decline and the first sub-1,500 reading since tracking began this spring. To put that in perspective: the freshet peaked around May 2 at roughly 2,741 cubic metres per second; today's reading is 45% below that peak, and 20 days have passed since the high-water mark. The system is draining steadily and consistently.

The mid-valley is following through. The river gauge at Mattawa fell another 17 centimetres today — the third day in a row of strong declines, totalling about 68 centimetres lost since the brief rain-driven rise of May 18–19. Pembroke dropped 10 centimetres overnight as well (Day 2 of decline). The pulse has cleared. The upper-basin drawdown now fully determines what the lower Ottawa sees in the days ahead, and the picture there is unambiguous recession.

Most headwater storage reservoirs have settled into a quiet, nearly-flat posture: of the 13 tracked, only Baskatong (up 6 cm) and Mitchinamecus (up 3 cm) are rising meaningfully. The remaining 11 are within 2 centimetres of where they were yesterday — holding or inching downward. This is a normal post-freshet pattern: operators are absorbing a modest slice of the diminishing inflow into storage, not actively pushing it downstream. No reservoir is rising fast enough (the watch threshold is 10 cm per day) to raise any concern. With Mattawa now declining sharply, the downstream reservoir at Des Joachims — which has been running as a simple pass-through for weeks — is getting closer to the conditions needed to begin deliberate storage accumulation again.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at Fort-Coulonge and Mansfield reversed course today, dropping approximately 6 centimetres over the past 24 hours to reach 107.57 metres by late afternoon. That puts the lake only about 7.5 centimetres above the minor-flood threshold of 107.50 metres — down from nearly 14 centimetres yesterday. At the current pace of decline, the lake could exit minor flood within the next one to two days, barring any surprise rain.

The dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson Generating Station, operated by Hydro-Québec) trimmed its release modestly, down about 14 cubic metres per second (roughly 1.4%). A more notable development: the dam's headpond — the pond that sits between the lake and the dam structure — has slipped to 104.16 metres, which is 4 centimetres below the lower bound of its normal 47-centimetre operating window (104.20 to 104.67 m). This is a minor breach, likely tracking the falling lake level, but worth watching over the next few days as the lake continues to recede.

The river management board's forecast from yesterday — "no major precipitation in sight; faster decline starting next week" — remains the operative guidance. The board has now transitioned to weekly updates, with the next bulletin due Tuesday May 26 at 4 PM Eastern. For the property owner: the water is going down, the pace is encouraging (roughly a week ago the lake was at 107.72 m; it has fallen 15 centimetres since then and is now approaching the minor-flood exit), and the forecast is dry and favourable. Watch the gauge directly until May 26; the next official update will be the weekly bulletin.

At the basin's terminal dam (Carillon, near Grenville, QC), the dam's headpond ticked up 1 centimetre today to 40.52 metres — still 44 centimetres above the regulatory spring-flood ceiling that is in effect while the Hull dock gauge stays elevated. This is now Day 18 of that exceedance. The Hull dock gauge fell another 2 centimetres today to 42.74 m; the activation threshold is 42.61 m, so 13 centimetres remain before the ceiling formally deactivates. The pace of Hull dock decline has been variable (ranging from 1 to 9 cm per day over the past week), so a firm date for deactivation is hard to pin down.
