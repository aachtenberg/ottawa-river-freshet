## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin's main precipitation surge appears to have crested. The Témiscaming outflow — where Lake Timiscaming drains southward at the top of the main-stem cascade — registered 904 cubic metres per second on July 5, down from a revised 929 m³/s the day before. That is the first day of decline after a three-day run that saw flows climb from 629 m³/s (July 2) to the event peak of 929 m³/s (July 4) in response to the precipitation system the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board flagged in its July 2 update. The outflow is still roughly twice the late-June baseline of around 450–500 m³/s, and is the 65th day since the spring freshet peak in early May — but the directional change is the headline. Reservoir operators continue to pass flows through rather than hold them: five of the fourteen tracked storage reservoirs are now slightly falling and none are rising, a straightforward pass-through posture.

The river tells the same story at the monitoring stations downstream. Mattawa — roughly two-thirds of the way from Lake Timiscaming to the regulated dam chain — recorded 152.89 metres at midnight, essentially unchanged from the night before (+1 cm). It is still 4 centimetres below the series high of 152.93 metres set on July 3, and the four-day rise streak that ended on July 4 has given way to a plateau. Pembroke, farther downstream, rose 3 more centimetres overnight to 111.73 metres — still climbing, but the pace has dropped from 9 centimetres the night before to 3. A station near Britannia (the Ottawa area, downstream of Carillon) reversed from yesterday's apparent fall and rose 4 centimetres, consistent with the crest propagating into the lower valley. The overall pattern — Témiscaming declining, Mattawa plateaued, Pembroke decelerating, lower valley just picking up the wave — is textbook post-peak propagation.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The government gauge at the Mansfield waterfront on Lac Coulonge registered 106.57 metres at 4 PM Eastern today, just under 3 centimetres above the same hour yesterday. That is a dramatic deceleration from the 14-centimetre leap recorded over the same window on July 4. The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's midnight series confirms the slowdown: from July 4 midnight to July 5 midnight the lake gained 4 centimetres (106.52 to 106.56 metres), down from 14 centimetres the night before. The lake is still rising, but the rate has collapsed. It remains in the fully normal range — 44 centimetres below the 107-metre pre-alert advisory threshold — and property owners can expect continued gradual increases until the wave from the mid-valley fully passes, with the pace now clearly easing.

At the Bryson dam pond — well downstream of Mansfield, reached by following the narrowing channel at the south end of Lac Coulonge several kilometres down to the dam — water levels fell 10 centimetres to 105.38 metres at 3 PM Eastern. This is the first decline since July 1, reversing a two-day run of increases that accompanied the precipitation event. The two stations immediately upstream of the Bryson dam pond (Rapide-2 and Rapide-7) remained in their sharply reduced operating posture for a second consecutive day — each releasing roughly 155–156 cubic metres per second, less than two-thirds of the 250–256 range they held before Wednesday's operational cut. Bryson itself increased its spill by about 10% and now routes just over half its total discharge through the spillway. The combined effect has turned the dam pond level. That said, the dam pond remains 71 centimetres above its permitted 104.67-metre operating band ceiling, and this is Day 38 of the exceedance — the single-day improvement, while directionally significant, has not materially changed the regulatory picture.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast, last updated July 2, says flows and levels "are expected to increase gradually over the next few days." The increases largely materialized in the days since: Témiscaming ran up nearly 500 m³/s above its July 2 level, Lac Coulonge gained 30–40 centimetres. Now, three days into the event, the upper basin is beginning to ease. The next ORRPB update is scheduled for July 7, which will be the first opportunity for official guidance on the recession timeline.
