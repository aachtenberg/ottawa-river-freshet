Taken together, both readings confirm the system is producing about the same outflow it was a week ago (352 m³/s on July 22). No coordinated storage release or active retention is underway. The basin is in a quiet, stable summer posture at roughly 17 straight days below 400 cubic metres per second at Timiscaming — well into the post-freshet baseline.

The Pembroke gauge further downstream is more reliable on first publish and shows 111.17 metres, up five centimetres from yesterday's confirmed value — a gentle upward tick consistent with the rain event passing through.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake gauge at Fort-Coulonge reads 106.025 metres as of 4 PM local time today — up about 2.5 centimetres from the same time yesterday. After yesterday's gentle decline paused by rain, today the rain's effect is showing: the lake has ticked slightly upward. It remains roughly 97.5 centimetres below the lowest flood-advisory level of 107.00 metres, and there is no flood concern of any kind. The midnight water-level series published by the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board confirms the same picture: the lake stood at 106.03 metres at midnight versus 106.00 metres the night before — a three-centimetre overnight rise.

At the Bryson Generating Station — which sits several kilometres downstream of where Lac Coulonge's southern arm narrows and winds its way down to the dam — operators modestly increased spillway flow today, from about 121 cubic metres per second yesterday to about 132 m³/s today, a 9.4 per cent increase. Despite this, the dam pond behind the station held flat at 105.36 metres — the same level as yesterday — which tells the hydraulic story clearly: the additional spill is precisely offsetting the rain-driven inflows still arriving from the Coulonge watershed and surrounding tributaries. The pond is now 62 consecutive days above its normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and remains 69 centimetres above that ceiling. The operators appear to be actively managing: they are increasing the spillway to absorb ongoing inflows without letting the pond rise further.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast is unchanged from yesterday's July 28 update, still noting that "water levels and flows are expected to increase gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week depending on locations." The next update is scheduled for August 4. For Fort-Coulonge, the rain adds a modest upward bias this week, but with the lake almost a metre below the first advisory threshold and operators actively spilling at Bryson, the buffer is substantial.
