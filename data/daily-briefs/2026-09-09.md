This is above 400 m³/s for what is provisionally the 14th straight day (confirmed through September 8, when the figure finalized at 435 m³/s — the Sep 8 brief's provisional 355 m³/s was a low partial-day reading that revised upward by 80 m³/s at finalization). The Quinze dam — the controlling Hydro-Québec facility at the top of the cascade feeding into the lake — is passing about 274 m³/s, roughly in line with recent days. The lake's own level eased slightly (−4 cm overnight), consistent with outflow marginally exceeding inflow. Three or four of the basin's fourteen tracked storage reservoirs are now losing water day-over-day (including Baskatong, the Temiscaming reservoir itself, and Des Joachims) while one small Ontario reservoir (Lady Evelyn) ticked up slightly. This shift from the almost entirely static picture of recent weeks is a normal seasonal signal: as summer ends, operators gradually let reservoirs ease back toward pre-winter operating levels rather than holding water.

The mid-valley reach continues a gentle weeks-long trend.
