## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin remains in a post-freshet holding pattern, but the flow story has a new chapter this week. The daily average flow out of Lake Timiscaming — the large Quebec-Ontario border lake that is the central pulse-point of the upper watershed — confirmed at 326 cubic metres per second for August 3, down from an intraday peak of around 349 that was cited in yesterday's brief. (The intraday high was real; the daily average simply smoothed it out.) For August 4, the running daily average through 3 PM Eastern is 333 cubic metres per second (ORRPB) or 325 m³/s (cross-checked against the PSPC reservoir gauge), placing the basin on its 23rd straight day below 400 cubic metres per second. The broader trajectory is oscillating gently in the 313–344 range rather than marching cleanly downward — there is no breakout either direction.

Further downstream, the gauge at Mattawa — where the Mattawa River joins the Ottawa, marking the start of the mid-valley reach — is rising today. The database shows a provisional midnight level of about 152.38 metres for August 3, and the ORRPB live reading at 2 PM Eastern today was 152.44 metres, a modest gain. Note that there is a discrepancy in the Mattawa record: the ORRPB's midnight table now shows 152.16 metres for August 3, while the database shows 152.38 metres. Yesterday's brief read 152.42 metres from the ORRPB live feed at 10 PM Eastern. All three numbers are inconsistent in ways that suggest the "confirmed" value yesterday was a live reading rather than a finalized midnight average. Pembroke, further downstream, is falling: 111.05–111.06 metres today, down roughly 6 centimetres from August 3.

The biggest story in the headwaters is not at Témiscaming at all but in the Gatineau River drainage. The Kiamika reservoir — northeast of Montpellier in the upper Gatineau watershed — rose 15 centimetres today alone, and the Poisson Blanc reservoir rose 7 centimetres. Both reflect the same rainfall that drove yesterday's massive Carillon surge, and both confirm the water is still working its way through the system. The upper Ottawa main stem (Témiscaming) is barely affected; the energy in the system today is entirely in the Gatineau and the mid-valley Ottawa tributaries.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property stretch is calm and holding steady. The gauge at Fort-Coulonge (Vigilance station 1195) read 106.10 metres at 4 PM local time, up one centimetre from midnight and essentially unchanged from yesterday's 106.09 metres. The lake remains 90 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold of 107.00 metres. There is no flood concern, no pending forecast change affecting this location, and nothing in the upstream signal — Témiscaming, Mattawa, or the Bryson dam pond — that would suggest an imminent rise here.

The ORRPB issued its first forecast update in seven days this afternoon at 3:13 PM Eastern. The new text acknowledges the rain event driving the ongoing mid-valley surge and says that flows and levels "are expected to start decreasing gradually over the coming week." This is significant — the ORRPB is now projecting a gradual decrease rather than the stable-or-gently-rising language from the previous (July 28) forecast. For the property, this means the current summer-low stretch is expected to persist.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — located well downstream of where the south end of Lac Coulonge narrows and winds down to the dam, not visible from or adjacent to the Mansfield and Fort-Coulonge properties — reversed course today. Yesterday operators had stepped up their spillway release markedly; today they backed off, cutting total discharge by 26 cubic metres per second (−5.6%), almost entirely by reducing the spillway flow from 168 to 139 cubic metres per second (−17.4%). As a result, the headpond rose 4 centimetres to 105.38 metres — a one-day rebound after the prior day's 6-centimetre drawdown. The headpond remains 71 centimetres above the normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres (Day 68 of a continuous exceedance), but the direction of movement day-over-day is now reversed again, back upward.

The ongoing major story — for the second consecutive day — remains the extraordinary high flows at Carillon, the basin's terminal dam about 300 kilometres to the southeast. Flow there settled slightly to around 2,100 cubic metres per second at 3 PM Eastern, still nearly double what is typical for early August. A new development today: Carillon opened its spillway for the first time in this episode, releasing 102 cubic metres per second over the gates while the turbines ran at near-maximum capacity. This confirms the turbines alone can no longer handle the volume of water arriving; the excess is going over the top. The Hull gauge — the waterfront gauge just upstream of Carillon that determines whether the spring-flood directive for the dam is in effect — plateaued today at 41.52 metres (midnight), essentially unchanged from August 3. It remains 109 centimetres below the 42.61-metre trigger that would activate the spring-flood operating ceiling. That ceiling is still dormant, now 71 days running.

## Notes

- **ORRPB forecast shift.** The change from "increase gradually or remain fairly stable" (July 28) to "continue rising … expected to start decreasing gradually over the coming week" (August 4) is an important milestone. ORRPB is now acknowledging the peak-near-at-hand scenario. The "continue rising" phrase covers the active Carillon/Britannia surge; the "decreasing gradually over the coming week" phrase is the projection. The Lac Coulonge reach is not expected to see any meaningful response to this event based on current data (106.10 m, 90 cm below pre-alert, Bryson acting as buffer upstream).

- **Carillon spillway mechanics.** The opening of Carillon's spillway (101.58 m³/s) when total flow is ~2,100 m³/s is straightforward: turbine capacity appears to be at or near maximum (~1,999 m³/s turbined), and any inflow above that threshold goes over the spillway gates. This is normal dam operation during high-inflow events. The spill magnitude is modest (4.8%) and not a regulatory concern at the current Hull dock level (41.52 m, 109 cm below the 42.61 m trigger). What matters is whether the Hull level begins to rise again in response to sustained high releases; today's plateau suggests it may not.

- **Gatineau drainage still absorbing.** Kiamika (+15 cm) and Poisson Blanc (+7 cm) reservoirs in the Gatineau headwaters continue to absorb inflow. The declining Rapides-Farmers release (Gatineau mouth, −25.7%) suggests the peak Gatineau contribution to the Ottawa has passed. However, the basin water is still moving downstream, sustaining the Britannia/Carillon levels for now.

- **Mattawa data quality note.** The three inconsistent August 3 Mattawa values (152.16 ORRPB table / 152.38 DB / 152.42 prior brief) underscore the difficulty in interpreting "midnight" vs live readings from the ORRPB website during active events. The ORRPB table's midnight values for Mattawa appear to occasionally lag or use a different measurement window than the live readings. For this brief, the DB (152.38 m Aug3) and ORRPB live (152.44 m Aug4 at 2 PM) are treated as the primary reference. The discrepancy may self-resolve when Aug4's midnight value finalizes tomorrow.

- **Témiscaming revision note.** The Aug3 downward revision (348 live → 326 confirmed) is unusual. Prior revisions have been upward (the DB provisional underestimates the confirmed, as noted for Mattawa's pattern). The intraday peak at T13:00 UTC of ~348 m³/s was real, but the second half of the day apparently ran lower (perhaps 300–310 m³/s), pulling the daily average down to 326. The pattern of upper-basin flows oscillating 313–344 over 10 days makes any given daily number somewhat noisy.

- **Next brief watch-items.** (a) Will Carillon begin to decline from the ~2,100 m³/s level, or does a new wave arrive? The ORRPB forecast projects gradual decrease over the week. (b) Will the Hull dock rise resume or continue to plateau? (c) Poisson Blanc: if it rises ≥10 cm tomorrow, the active retention flag triggers. (d) Aug4 Témiscaming confirmed value (expect near 325–333 range). (e) Bryson headpond trajectory: did operators step back to let it rise again, or will they resume elevated releases?
