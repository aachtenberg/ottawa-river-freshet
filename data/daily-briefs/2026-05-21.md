## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa River crossed a significant milestone today: for the first time since the spring freshet surge began, the daily outflow from Lake Temiscaming (the departure point of the upper Ottawa toward Mattawa and the rest of the river) dropped below 2,000 cubic metres per second — landing at approximately 1,740. That is the ninth consecutive day of decline and places the flow roughly a third below the freshet peak estimated around May 2. The upper basin is clearly and steadily pulling back.

The mid-valley has followed suit. The river gauge at Mattawa fell sharply — about 18 centimetres in a single day — continuing a two-day decline that has erased the rain-driven rise of last weekend. Pembroke, which received a transit pulse of water yesterday (+10 cm), has now reversed and is declining again (−5 cm today). The pulse has cleared. Upstream operators are also throttling back: the Quinze dam (which releases the upper Ottawa's flow into Lake Temiscaming's downstream reach) cut its release by about 11% today; the Îles dam, one step further downstream, cut by nearly 8%. The cascade is pulling in.

Most headwater storage reservoirs are rising 1–3 centimetres per day — Baskatong, Dozois, Mitchinamecus, and others. This is exactly what operators are supposed to do during a recession: absorb a portion of the diminishing inflow rather than letting it all pass downstream. No reservoir is rising fast enough to trigger any concern (the watch threshold is 10 cm per day; the highest today is 3 cm). Normal refill posture.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at Fort-Coulonge and Mansfield rose a further 4.5 centimetres today, putting it at roughly 107.64 metres — now 13.5 centimetres above the minor-flood threshold of 107.50 m. This is the second consecutive day of rise since the lake reached a recent low on May 19. The exit from minor flood is further delayed.

The dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson Generating Station, operated by Hydro-Québec) held its releases nearly flat at about 1,019 cubic metres per second, up a marginal 1.7% from yesterday. The dam pond is sitting at 104.21 metres — just barely above the lower bound of its normal 47-centimetre operating window (104.20 to 104.67 m), essentially the same position as the past two days.

The river management board (ORRPB) issued its last daily freshet bulletin today, at 3:35 PM Eastern. The forecast says the river is "stabilizing or increasing slightly following recent rainfall," will "decline gradually over the next few days," and then "decline more rapidly starting next week" as the weather stays dry. Critically, this is the **final daily bulletin for the 2026 spring freshet** — the board is switching to weekly forecasts starting Tuesday, May 26. Property owners will have less frequent official guidance after today.

For the property owner: the lake is still 13.5 cm above minor flood, and today's continued rise means a quiet exit is not imminent. The upstream picture is good — the basin is pulling back strongly — and the board sees no major rain in the coming days. If that holds, the lake should begin declining again within a day or two and could clear minor flood within roughly a week. However, the first weekly bulletin won't arrive until May 26; watch the gauge directly in the meantime.

At the basin's terminal dam (Carillon, near Grenville, QC), the dam's own pond remains 43 centimetres above the regulatory spring-flood ceiling that is formally in effect while the Hull dock gauge stays elevated — today is Day 17 of that exceedance. The trigger gauge (Hull dock) has dropped 5 centimetres today and is now 15 centimetres above its activation threshold; at the current pace of decline, deactivation could come within the next few days, though the rate has been variable.
