## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Three milestones crossed today in the upper Ottawa basin. The flow leaving Lake Temiscaming (river management board preliminary reading) reached 1,314 cubic metres per second (m³/s) — the first time it has fallen below 1,400 since the spring freshet peaked. That puts us 24 days past the May 2 peak of roughly 2,741 m³/s, with the river now at barely half its high-water mark. Today is Day 6 with flow below 2,000 m³/s and Day 4 below 1,500 m³/s. The descent has been steady — roughly 60–120 m³/s per day for the past week — with no disruption from precipitation. The next round-number milestone to watch is sub-1,000, which at current rates is perhaps one to two weeks away.

The mid-valley picture is holding steady. Mattawa — the level gauge where the Ottawa descends out of the northern basin — held essentially flat for the fourth straight day (153.03 m, +2 cm from yesterday's midnight), showing the upper basin is still delivering a moderate daily pulse of water southward but no longer accelerating it. Pembroke is on its seventh consecutive day of slow decline (-3 cm today, 112.17 m). Between those two points, the Des Joachims reservoir (operated by Ontario Power Generation) added another 15 centimetres of stored water overnight, reaching 151.16 m at midnight — the second consecutive day of significant accumulation (it gained 25 cm the day before). OPG is holding and storing the inflow arriving from the north rather than passing it straight downstream. This is the expected post-freshet behaviour and gently reduces the pulse of water arriving at the property in the short term.

Across 14 tracked headwater reservoirs, comparing the latest midnight data (May 24 to May 25), 5 are rising, 6 are holding steady within two centimetres, and 3 are falling modestly. Des Joachims (+25 cm May 24→25) is the only reservoir exceeding 10 cm per day. The broader basin is in refill posture — absorbing rather than releasing the declining spring pulse — which is normal and expected at this point in the recession.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at Fort-Coulonge and Mansfield crossed its final milestone today. The midnight reading is 106.90 metres — 10 centimetres below the 107.0-metre pre-alert threshold — and the river management board's own map now colours it green, meaning "normal." The real-time gauge at the lake registered etat 1 (vigilance watch, the lowest monitoring state) at 106.897 m as of 9 PM EDT. That is a decline of about 8 cm since yesterday's midnight reading and about 14 cm since 4 PM EDT yesterday measured by the real-time gauge. The property's lake has not been in the "normal" category since before the spring freshet peaked in early May.

The dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson Generating Station) cut its total release by another 8.5% today, entirely through the spillway (down 11.9%), as less lake water presses over the spillway at the lower level. Notably, the dam's backed-up pond between the lake and the turbines reversed its recent downward streak: after dropping to 26 centimetres below the normal operating floor yesterday, it recovered 15 centimetres today to 104.09 m, now only 11 cm below the lower limit of the normal operating range (104.20 m). The turbines held steady. The pond is still slightly outside its normal range but heading back in the right direction.

The river management board updated its weekly bulletin today at 3:35 PM EDT. The message is clear: flows and water levels along the Ottawa main stem are now "within seasonal values" and expected to remain stable or decrease gradually over the coming week. This is a deliberate step down from last week's language, which described conditions as "above average for this time of year." The next update is not until June 2 — there is nothing imminent to watch for on the precipitation side. For the property owner, the bottom line is simple: the water is down, the river board calls conditions normal, and there is nothing in the current basin posture pointing toward a reversal.

One significant development at the lower end of the basin warrants a brief note for the record: the monitoring trigger at the Hull gauge (just upstream of Carillon, the basin's terminal dam near Grenville) fell below its activation threshold of 42.61 m today, ending a 22-day formal monitoring period under the river management plan. This is a regulatory milestone but has no direct effect on the property's water level — Carillon is 350 km downstream.
