## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Lake Temiscaming posted a provisional outflow of 400 cubic metres per second today — right at the round-number threshold that the streak of "above 400 m³/s" has been tracking since August 27. The finalized value for yesterday (September 9) came in at 409 m³/s, confirming that streak at Day 14; whether today's reading (which may reflect a partial-day average) finalizes above or at 400 is uncertain. The Quinze dam at the top of the Hydro-Québec cascade held steady at about 277 m³/s. Lake Temiscaming itself crept up 5 centimetres overnight, consistent with inflow from the Quinze dam and other tributaries slightly exceeding the 400 m³/s the lake is releasing downstream — a normal late-summer picture where the watershed is still slowly draining.

The most notable upstream event today is at the far downstream end of the basin: Carillon, the terminal dam before the Ottawa River joins the St. Lawrence, stepped up sharply — from about 1,162 m³/s yesterday afternoon to 1,602 m³/s this evening. That is a 38% jump in a single day, confirmed both by the Hydro-Québec real-time hourly readings and by the partial-day average on the Ottawa River Board's website (1,576 m³/s as of mid-afternoon). The Gatineau River arm — which joins the Ottawa just above Carillon — also increased about 8–12% across both of its control points. Mid-valley relay stations (Rapide-7, Bryson) show no corresponding change, pointing to rain-driven direct runoff in the lower Ottawa and Gatineau watersheds as the likely driver. Pembroke was flat at 111.17 metres.

The Mattawa gauge — between North Bay and Pembroke — shows 152.20 metres this evening, down 23 centimetres from yesterday's finalized value. Given that Mattawa has shown a systematic pattern this September where afternoon provisional readings understate or overstate the daily average by 10–20 centimetres before finalization, this reading is flagged as provisional with the same caution applied throughout the month. No property impact is expected from any of the above.
