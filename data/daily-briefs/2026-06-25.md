## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper basin continues its gradual unwind, now well into the tail of a secondary surge that peaked around June 20. Outflow from Lake Temiscaming — the pinch point where the vast upper watershed drains into the Ottawa River main stem — came in around 616 cubic metres per second in today's preliminary reading, up a hair from yesterday's revised value of 608. That small uptick is almost certainly noise in the preliminary data: the last five preliminary Temiscaming readings have each been revised downward when the authoritative federal gauge values came in. The broad trend is unambiguously downward — outflow is now roughly 170 cubic metres per second below the June 20 peak of 785, and about 2,100 below the primary freshet peak in early May. The reservoir balance across the basin remains completely neutral: the storage lakes are neither stockpiling water nor hurrying to drain it.

The mid-valley is continuing to clear. Water levels at Mattawa — about 200 kilometres downstream of Lake Temiscaming — fell 23 centimetres overnight to 152.34 metres, a solid decline day. Pembroke, further downstream, dropped 3 centimetres. Both gauges are declining steadily, reflecting the receding pulse of the secondary surge now working its way down the main stem. (A note on yesterday's brief: the June 24 Mattawa preliminary reading of 152.32 m was revised significantly upward to 152.57 m in the final data — so yesterday's claimed "largest single-day drop in brief record" of 35 centimetres was incorrect. The actual June 23→24 drop was 10 centimetres, and today's June 24→25 drop is 23 centimetres, which is large but not record-breaking.)

The mid-cascade is showing some notable restructuring today. Two generating stations in the stretch between the Temiscaming region and the Bryson dam significantly cut their output: the Rapide-2 station cut releases by 34.6 percent (from roughly 322 to 210 cubic metres per second), and the Rapide-7 station cut by 9.2 percent. The basin-terminal dam at Carillon also eased its release rate by about 5.8 percent, routing everything through its turbines. These moves appear deliberate — both sites have been holding steady at the new levels for several hours — and likely reflect operators stepping flows down in line with the falling inflow from the upper basin.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge fell about 6 centimetres today to 106.13 metres, a moderate pace — intermediate between the sluggish 3 centimetres per day seen the day before and the faster 10+ centimetres per day seen last week. The lake is fully in normal territory, sitting 87 centimetres below the pre-alert level of 107 metres. There is no prospect of the lake returning to flood levels absent significant rainfall.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge (the backed-up water surface in front of the Bryson Generating Station) resumed its drawdown after yesterday's brief rebound. The pond dropped 4 centimetres to 105.37 metres — still 70 centimetres above the operators' permitted upper ceiling of 104.67 metres, and well outside the range where turbines could restart. All generating units at Bryson remain shut down, now for the eleventh consecutive day. The dam is releasing 460 cubic metres per second entirely through its open spillway channels, down about 4 percent from yesterday. At this pace, the pond remains on a slow recovery trajectory toward the operating range.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast is unchanged: conditions are "expected to decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." The board moved to its weekly summer monitoring cadence on June 23; the next update is June 30 at 4:00 PM EDT.

## Notes

- **Rapide-2 step-down context:** The Rapide-2 station sits in the mid-Ottawa cascade between the Des Joachims OPG facility and Rapide-7 (HQ). At 210 m³/s, it is releasing substantially less than either its upstream feed (Des Joachims reservoir outflow at 683 m³/s from reservoir_readings) or the sites immediately downstream (Rapide-7 at 235 m³/s, Bryson at 460 m³/s). The implication is that one or more intermediate reservoirs in the Rapide-2 reach are being actively held back. This may reflect a deliberate storage strategy by HQ as they manage the cascade drawdown, but the magnitude warrants follow-up in tomorrow's brief to confirm whether it persists or normalizes.

- **Des Joachims OPG outflow swing:** The Des Joachims reservoir outflow (from `reservoir_readings`) jumped from 424 to 683 m³/s overnight while the reservoir level held flat at 152.16 m. This implies inflow to Des Joachims also increased by roughly the same amount. The source of the inflow increase is not immediately clear from the data — it may be tributary runoff or revised data. Worth monitoring.

- **Bryson timeline:** The headpond at 105.37 m is 70 cm above the 104.67 m operating ceiling. Even at the faster −12 cm/day pace seen June 22–23, return to the operating ceiling would take ~6 days (around July 1). Yesterday's +6 cm rebound showed that operator choices about release rates can easily reverse progress. Today's −4 cm is a positive step but the trajectory remains highly operator-dependent.

- **Temiscaming preliminary revision pattern:** Five of the last five preliminary Temiscaming values have been revised downward when finalized. The average downward revision over that period is approximately 15–20 m³/s. Today's preliminary of 616 should be treated with that caveat — the final value may be closer to 595–605 m³/s.
