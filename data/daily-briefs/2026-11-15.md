## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The outflow from Lake Timiskaming averaged 401 cubic metres per second on November 15, one unit above the 400 mark that this journal has been tracking, and 24 more than a week earlier. It is 197 days since the May 2 peak of 2,741. Flows of this size are ordinary for mid-November.

The Mattawa gauge read 17 centimetres higher than the day before, while Pembroke slipped 5 centimetres. The Mattawa rise did not last: the next day's value was 28 centimetres lower, so it reads as noise in a jumpy daily series rather than a rise in the river. Among the fourteen storage reservoirs, 11 were steady, 2 fell slightly (Lake Timiskaming four centimetres, Lady Evelyn three) and Poisson Blanc rose four centimetres.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake gauge at Fort-Coulonge read 106.047 metres at 9 PM UTC on November 15, about one centimetre higher than a day earlier. It remains 95 centimetres below the first alert level and 145 centimetres below the minor-flood level. The river board's November 10 outlook expected stable conditions for the week.

Well downstream, the Bryson Generating Station ran unchanged from the day before: about 372 cubic metres per second in total, roughly a fifth of it over the spillway and the rest through the turbines. The dam pond behind it stayed about 91 centimetres above its normal operating ceiling, as it has for months. The basin's last dam at Carillon was also steady.
