## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The most notable reading today comes from Lake Timiscaming, the natural lake at the top of the Ottawa River basin. The preliminary outflow figure for July 21 is 283 cubic metres per second from one source and 293 from a PSPC cross-check — both at or just below the 300-cubic-metre-per-second mark for the first time in the confirmed record of this recession. If these numbers hold after the usual 24-to-48-hour revision cycle, this would mark a genuine sub-300 milestone for the upper basin. However, the same situation arose on July 18, when a reading of 295 appeared to confirm sub-300 — only to be revised upward to 363 the following day. Today's reading must be treated as provisional. What is not in doubt is the overall direction: the basin has been systematically shedding flow since the spring peak in early May, and today's reading — whatever its final value — represents the lowest reading of this recession period. The sub-400 streak (all daily readings below 400 cubic metres per second) is now nine consecutive days.

All major storage reservoirs in the upper basin are essentially flat — every one within a centimetre or two of yesterday's level. The sole marginal exception is the Des Joachims reservoir pool on the Ontario side, which rose 5 centimetres — a tiny amount consistent with routine operator dispatch variability. This is a normal late-summer baseflow pattern; operators are neither actively retaining nor releasing large volumes.

A significant correction from yesterday's brief is required. Two anomaly flags reported on July 20 were based on preliminary data that has since been substantially revised. First, the Mattawa gauge reading for July 20 has been revised from 152.14 metres to 152.45 metres — a 31-centimetre upward correction. This completely reverses the picture: what appeared to be a "21-centimetre single-night drop" was actually a 32-centimetre rise from July 19's revised level of 152.13 m. Second, the reported OPG Des Joachims dispatch cut — described as a 42% reduction from 406 to 237 cubic metres per second — is also incorrect. The revised daily average for July 20 is 388 cubic metres per second, a much more modest change from 406. The Mattawa data comes from Water Survey of Canada, which routinely revises its recent readings as quality control is applied; the two-to-three-day-old readings should generally be considered preliminary. Both anomaly flags from yesterday are retracted. Today's Mattawa is 152.33 metres — a mild 12-centimetre decline from the (now-revised) July 20 value of 152.45 m, which is a normal daily fluctuation.
