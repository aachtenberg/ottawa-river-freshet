## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper basin has crossed a quiet but meaningful milestone today: outflow from Lake Témiscaming — the pinch point where the enormous upper Ottawa watershed narrows into the main river — has dropped below 600 cubic metres per second for the first time in at least four weeks. The final reading for yesterday (which yesterday's preliminary data showed as 616 m³/s) was revised sharply downward to 551 m³/s — the largest single-day preliminary revision in the brief record — retroactively making yesterday the first sub-600 day. Today's reading came in at roughly 539 to 550, making it the second consecutive day below that threshold. This is not a crisis or an anomaly; it is simply the basin settling back toward base-flow conditions, passing milestone after milestone on the way down from the May 2 primary peak of nearly 2,750 cubic metres per second.

The upper generating stations in Hydro-Québec's cascade responded in kind today. Three sites in the regulated stretch above the Bryson dam — Première-Chute, Quinze, and Îles — each stepped down flows by 6 to 11 percent. This is the expected downstream transmission of declining inflows from Lake Témiscaming: as less water enters the regulated corridor, operators reduce generation proportionally. The site that made headlines yesterday for a large cut (Rapide-2, down 35%) is holding steady at its new lower level — confirming that yesterday's move was deliberate and not a data artifact. Storage reservoirs across the basin are nearly all holding flat: 13 of 14 reservoirs moved by less than 2 centimetres overnight. The single exception (Des Joachims reservoir, down 5 centimetres) reflects natural routing, not operator intervention.

At Mattawa — a city about 200 kilometres downstream of Lake Témiscaming — the water dropped 28 centimetres overnight, making it five consecutive days of decline. That is the largest single-day drop at Mattawa in several weeks and reflects the secondary surge (which peaked around June 20 near Témiscaming) fully clearing the mid-valley reach. Pembroke, further downstream, also fell 10 centimetres. The recession is broad-based and accelerating in the mid-valley. Yesterday's brief noted a 152.34-metre reading for Mattawa that has since been revised upward to 152.46 metres by the monitoring database — a pattern of systematic upward corrections to preliminary overnight data that has now appeared on three consecutive days.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the foot of the Lac Coulonge watershed (visible from Mansfield properties along the Ottawa) is fully normal and continuing its unhurried decline. Today's reading of 106.10 metres sits 90 centimetres below the pre-alert level of 107.00 metres — a comfortable margin that, absent significant rainfall, is only going to grow. The rate of decline eased to about 3 centimetres per day today, down from 6 centimetres the day before. This is not unusual: as the lake continues to drop, the remaining driving head between it and the river below diminishes, naturally slowing the drawdown.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the backed-up water surface in front of the Bryson Generating Station — is essentially holding flat at 105.38 metres. Despite flows through the spillways easing slightly (from 460 to 445 cubic metres per second), the pond barely moved. It still sits 71 centimetres above the operators' permitted upper operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and all generating units remain idle — now for twelve consecutive days. At the current pace of drawdown (essentially zero today), a return to the operating range could take weeks, though any operator decision to push more flow through the dam could accelerate that significantly.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast is unchanged from June 23 and will not be updated again until June 30 at 4:00 PM EDT: conditions are described as "within seasonal values" and expected to "decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." No flood warnings, no further-rise flags, no special advisories. The picture is quiet.

## Notes

- **Sub-600 context:** The first sub-600 day (June 25 final = 551 m³/s) marks the basin crossing below a threshold not seen since at least May 28 (the start of the 30-day proxy window, at 1,280 m³/s). At 550 m³/s, Témiscaming is running at roughly 20% of the May 2 primary peak and is approaching the range where summer base-flow conditions become the dominant driver rather than snowmelt/freshet. The secondary surge (peak June 20 = 785 m³/s) has been fully erased.

- **Preliminary revision pattern deteriorating:** The Témiscaming preliminary has been revised downward on each of the last several days, but the magnitude today (−65 m³/s) is roughly 3× the typical revision seen in May and early June. This may reflect a data ingestion issue on the ORRPB side, or it may reflect genuine difficulty in estimating Témiscaming outflow on a same-day basis. Until the pattern stabilizes, the day-count milestones for sub-600 should be treated as subject to retroactive adjustment.

- **Mattawa corrections:** Three consecutive upward revisions to Mattawa midnight preliminary data suggests a systematic lag in the WSC data pipeline. This matters for milestone claims: the June 25 brief's "−35 cm largest single-day drop" was wrong (actual June 24 change was −10 cm after revision), and yesterday's −23 cm claim for June 25 is now revised to −11 cm. Today's −28 cm claim for June 26 is provisional.

- **Rapide-2 gap:** With Rapide-2 at 210 m³/s and Des Joachims at 421 m³/s outflow, approximately 211 m³/s of flow is being held in the intermediate reach between those two sites. This structural storage gap is now in its second day. If Rapide-2 normalizes to ~421 m³/s (matching Des Joachims pass-through), it would represent a +100% step-up in release — watch for this in tomorrow's brief.

- **Bryson timeline:** At the current +1 cm/day drift, the headpond (105.38 m) will not reach the operating ceiling (104.67 m) for months. Even at the fastest drawdown seen this breach (−12 cm/day on June 23), it would take ~6 days. The pace of drawdown is entirely operator-controlled — a decision to increase releases significantly could change the timeline in hours.

- **Guardrail probe results:** Proxy (freshet.xgrunt.com/history): HTTP 200 ✓ (112 b). ORRPB forecast: HTTP 200 ✓ (130,739 b). Both sources healthy. No outage language used.
