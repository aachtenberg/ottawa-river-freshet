## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The story at the head of the watershed continues to be one of quiet, post-flood stability — with a notable asterisk for how the provisional numbers keep getting revised downward. Today's first-published outflow reading from Lake Timiscaming, at the top of the Ottawa River system, is 327 cubic metres per second, eighty-nine days after the spring freshet peak near 2,741 cubic metres per second on May 2. A simultaneous cross-check instrument reads 319 cubic metres per second — an eight-unit gap well within the normal uncertainty range for first-day estimates. Both figures sit comfortably below 400 cubic metres per second, extending what is now an 18-day run below that threshold and confirming that the watershed's main snowmelt and rain pulse is long past.

Yesterday's outflow reading — published as 349 cubic metres per second in last night's brief — has since been revised down to 324, and the day before's reading has been revised further (now 321, down from 331, which was itself a major revision from an initial 381). The pattern is consistent: first-day Témiscaming estimates have been running well above confirmed values throughout this period, and today's 327 should be treated as directional only. What we can say with confidence is that outflow is essentially flat on the week — 327 today versus 342 a week ago — with a very slight declining tendency.

Across the basin's fourteen tracked storage reservoirs, virtually nothing moved overnight. One small reservoir (Kiamika) ticked up 3 centimetres, and the large Lake Timiscaming dropped 3 centimetres — both essentially at the noise floor of the measurement system. The basin is in a textbook post-freshet summer holding pattern. Mid-valley, the Mattawa gauge reads 152.32 metres today — this is a first-day provisional figure that has reliably been revised upward by 20 or more centimetres on every previous day this monitoring period; the confirmed value will almost certainly be in the 152.50 metre range when published tomorrow. Pembroke, further downstream, is a more reliable same-day reading and shows 111.07 metres — down 6 centimetres from yesterday as the rain event of July 28–29 continues to drain through the system.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge reads 106.045 metres as of 4 PM local time — up about 2 centimetres from yesterday afternoon. The lake continues to tick gently upward in the wake of last weekend's rainfall, but remains almost a metre below the lowest flood-advisory level of 107.00 metres. This is not a flood situation by any measure, and the very slow rate of rise (2 centimetres over 24 hours) means the lake is far from any threshold of concern.

At the Bryson Generating Station — which sits well downstream of where the southern end of Lac Coulonge narrows into the river channel, not adjacent to the shoreline properties — the picture is almost unchanged from yesterday. Total discharge at the station held essentially flat at 421 cubic metres per second. The dam pond behind the station edged up one centimetre to 105.37 metres, now 63 consecutive days above its normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres and 70 centimetres above that ceiling. Spillway flow ticked up slightly again, which appears to be operators continuing to match the inflows still arriving from the Coulonge watershed as the July rain pulse works its way through.

The most significant development today is not at Bryson or the lake directly — it is at Carillon, the basin-terminal dam more than 300 kilometres downstream. Carillon's total release surged another 16 per cent to 1,298 cubic metres per second, its third consecutive day above 1,000 cubic metres per second and a new monitoring-period high. The two Gatineau River sites upstream of Carillon both jumped roughly 15 per cent as well, reflecting Gatineau watershed flows from the same rain event now passing through. None of this affects the Fort-Coulonge properties directly, but it illustrates the basin-wide scale of the late-July rain pulse. The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast remains unchanged from its July 28 update, still noting that "water levels and flows are expected to increase gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week depending on locations." The "today and tomorrow" precipitation language in that forecast referred to July 28 and 29, which have now passed. The next scheduled update is August 4.

## Notes

- **Carillon acceleration: what's driving it.** Three days of sharp Carillon increases (1,006 → 1,117 → 1,298 m³/s) from a summer rain event is unusual. The cascade math: Bryson at ~421 m³/s, Paugan/Rapides-Farmers at combined ~400 m³/s (Gatineau), plus local tributary inflows between Bryson and Carillon, easily accounts for 1,298 m³/s at the terminal dam. The interesting case-file dimension: in the pre-2017 record, a mid-July rain event of this scale would typically see smaller downstream peaks. This data point merits filing under the "post-2017 volume regime" evidence.

- **Témiscaming provisional data quality — now a clear pattern.** Four major provisional-to-confirmed downward revisions in a row: Jul25 (+13 prov, confirmed lower), Jul26, Jul27, and now Jul28 (381→321, −60 m³/s total). The first-day ORRPB/PSPC estimate appears to consistently overstate outflow during or immediately after rain events. Today's 327/319 should be expected to confirm in the 300–320 range tomorrow.

- **ORRPB note language flag.** The July 28 forecast says "additional precipitation expected across the basin today and tomorrow." As of July 30, both those days are now in the past. If ORRPB holds to its August 4 weekly cadence without an interim update, the forecast will be 7 days stale when next published — during which time Carillon climbed to 1,298 m³/s. Worth noting in the case file as an example of the weekly-notice cadence's limitations during an active rain event.

- **Bryson headpond slow upward creep.** The pond has gone from 105.36 (Jul27-28-29 flat/flat) to 105.37 m today. Operators are not allowing it to fall, and are not restoring the operating band compliance seen in the July 27 correction. The 70 cm above-ceiling position is now 63 days old.

(c) Témiscaming Jul30 confirmed value (expected tomorrow: likely 300–320 m³/s). (d) Mattawa Jul30 confirmed value (expected +20 cm revision to ~152.50 m).
