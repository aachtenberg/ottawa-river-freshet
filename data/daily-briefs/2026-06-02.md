## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The recession at Lake Témiscaming has continued its gradual decline, with the finalized daily average for yesterday (June 1) coming in at just over 1,060 cubic metres per second — the first time since the May 2 freshet peak that the outflow fell decisively below the 1,200 mark, and briefly even below 1,100. Today the lake is rebounding slightly to around 1,160–1,190 m³/s, so the basin appears to be oscillating in the 1,000–1,200 range rather than plunging straight through. Either way, the system is now 32 days past the peak and the recession is firmly entrenched — the outflow on Day 32 is less than half what it was at the height of the spring runoff.

The big news in the upper basin today is a coordinated reduction in releases from three of Hydro-Québec's regulated sites above the property: the Quinze dam (which feeds Lake Témiscaming from the north) cut its release by about 27%, Première-Chute by about 17%, and Îles by about 18%. These reductions — each in the 130 to 200 cubic-metre-per-second range — are consistent with operators stepping back from their elevated freshet-season operating rates as natural inflow from snowmelt subsides. The reservoir levels at these sites barely changed, confirming that this is a managed drawdown of release rates rather than a shortage of water.

Mid-valley, the surge of extra water that Ontario Power Generation released from the Des Joachims reservoir on May 30–31 has passed its peak at the Mattawa gauge (roughly halfway between Lake Témiscaming and the property) and is now receding — the Mattawa level dropped 4 centimetres overnight, Day 2 of the post-pulse decline. At the same time, Des Joachims itself is still rising (+12 cm overnight to about 152.1 m), as OPG continues to absorb more water than it is releasing in that immediate stretch. At 12 of the 14 monitored reservoirs across the basin, levels are essentially unchanged — a picture of a basin in slow, controlled drainage with no new forcing.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The river at Fort-Coulonge has gone completely flat. For the second consecutive day, the midnight water level reads 106.36 metres — the same as the day before, and 75 centimetres lower than it was a week ago. In practical terms, the property is 64 centimetres below the first warning level (the "pre-alert" level at 107.00 m) and about 114 centimetres below minor flood. All warning flags are off. The near-complete stall in the rate of decline — from 9 cm/day a week ago to zero — reflects the Des Joachims pulse having fully arrived: the extra water coming from upstream is matching the natural drain rate almost perfectly. As that extra water passes through over the next day or two, the decline should resume.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — Bryson Generating Station's headpond — is showing the first encouraging sign since it overflowed its normal range five days ago: it fell 15 centimetres today, from 105.20 m to 105.05 m. It is still 38 centimetres above the top of its normal operating band (104.20 to 104.67 m), so the breach is not over, but the operator appears to have opened the spillway significantly — all of today's increased flow went through the spillway (+21%), not the turbines (which were unchanged). Whether this is a deliberate decision to drain the headpond ahead of next week's forecast or a response to reduced natural inflow from the upper cascade is unclear, but the direction has finally reversed.

The ORRPB published its scheduled weekly bulletin today (3:33 PM EDT) and confirmed that conditions along the river are "within seasonal values" and expected to "remain stable or decrease gradually over the coming week." Crucially, the next scheduled update is June 9 — a full seven days away — which officially marks the transition from the freshet's daily-monitoring phase to the board's standard summer cadence. For the record of this event, this is the date the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board considered the 2026 freshet operationally closed.

## Notes

- **Bryson headpond: path to resolution.** The headpond is now at 105.05 m, 38 cm above the band ceiling. At today's drop rate of 15 cm/day, it would return to 104.67 m in about 2.5 days — by June 4–5 — IF inflow remains at today's level. However, the upper-cascade reductions at Première-Chute, Quinze, and Îles will likely decrease Bryson inflow over the next 1–2 days, which could accelerate the drain. Alternatively, if the operator reduces spill again (as at Carillon), the drain rate will slow. Watch the June 3–4 readings.

- **Sub-1,100 at Témiscaming — anomaly or new equilibrium?** The June 1 daily average of 1,066 m³/s represents a notable single-day dip below the ~1,200 plateau maintained since May 29. The ORRPB live showed 1,234 m³/s at 14:00 ET on June 1 (per yesterday's brief) but the daily average finalized at 1,066. This implies significant flow variability within June 1 — likely lower flows in the morning/evening bracketing a midday peak. June 2's ~1,162–1,192 m³/s rebounds, suggesting the sub-1,100 reading was transient. Whether this reflects PSPC managed drawdown or natural flow variability is unclear from the data available.

- **ORRPB cadence milestone — case-file implication.** The transition to weekly updates is a meaningful record marker for the case file: it establishes June 2 as the date the regulatory apparatus officially deems the 2026 freshet closed.
