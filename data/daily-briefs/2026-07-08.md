## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The post-event recession at Lake Timiscaming has brought the flow out of the lake back below 600 cubic metres per second — specifically to 594 cubic metres per second today, the lowest reading since June 30. That sounds like a dry technical number, but what it means is that the Canada Day weekend precipitation event has now fully flushed through the upper basin: inflows are back near the pre-event baseline, and the system is in a steady drain posture. The federal operator (Public Services and Procurement Canada) and the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's own flow table both report 594 m³/s, giving high confidence in the reading. This is Day 8 of consecutive decline since the July 4 peak of 928 m³/s. Worth noting: a revision in today's database shows that July 7's flow was actually 624 m³/s — not the preliminary 705 m³/s reported yesterday — so the event's above-700 episode lasted four days (July 3–6), not five as yesterday's brief stated. The basin crossed back below 700 on July 7 and is now back below 600 today.

About two-thirds of the way between Lake Timiscaming and the dam chain, the Mattawa gauge is showing a very rapid fall: down 32 centimetres overnight to 152.18 metres. This is Day 3 of decline at Mattawa, and the rate is impressive — the monitoring station at Mattawa is essentially returning to pre-event levels at roughly the same speed it rose. Pembroke, further downstream, shed another 12 centimetres to 111.52 metres, Day 2 of decline there. The wave from the Canada Day event is now clearly past at all monitored points from Timiscaming to Pembroke.

Note on yesterday's revision: the July 7 Mattawa value in yesterday's brief (152.41 m) was a preliminary that has been revised upward to 152.50 m, and the July 6 value has been further revised down from 152.76 m to 152.54 m. The practical effect is that the big single-day drop at Mattawa happened today (July 7→8: −32 cm) rather than yesterday as yesterday's brief implied. Storage reservoirs across the basin remain in a pure drain posture: 2 of 14 tracked reservoirs are gently falling, 12 are flat, and none are rising.

The dam continues to run its turbines while passing roughly half its total outflow through the spillway.
