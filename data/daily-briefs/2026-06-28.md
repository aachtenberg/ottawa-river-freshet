The upper Ottawa watershed hit another milestone today: flow out of Lake Témiscaming dropped to exactly 400 cubic metres per second, the first time it has fallen to that level since the spring freshet began. At the May 2 peak, this same gauge was carrying nearly 2,750 cubic metres per second; today's flow is roughly 15% of that figure. The basin is firmly in early-summer recession, approaching base-flow territory. One important caveat: today's reading is a preliminary daily average, and round numbers (exactly 400) are a common signature of estimated rather than finalized data. Tomorrow's brief will carry a correction if the final figure differs — recent preliminary readings have consistently revised downward, so the true value may be slightly below 400.

The river at Mattawa is now at 152.26 metres, which is 17 centimetres lower than it stood even on June 26, before the apparent pulse. This is consistent with the June 27 rise having been a brief, rain-driven event in local tributaries — not a sustained surge from the upper basin. Pembroke, further downstream, was still easing gently as of last night's report (no June 28 reading yet — the data comes in with a normal one-day lag). The main-stem system south of Mattawa continues to ease quietly.

Across the basin's storage reservoirs, essentially nothing moved today — all 14 reservoirs tracked by the system are within a centimetre of yesterday. The reservoir at Des Joachims (a major generating station midway down the Ottawa valley) had risen 6 centimetres yesterday; today it leveled off. This is a normal summer holding pattern, with operators keeping water rather than pushing it downstream.

- **Sub-400 context:** At 400 m³/s (preliminary), Témiscaming is now at roughly one-third of the late-June typical base-flow reference used by the ORRPB's seasonal percentile comparisons. The secondary surge (peak June 20 = 785 m³/s) was fully erased by June 25; the basin is now declining below even pre-surge baseline. Typical late-summer base flow for the upper Ottawa is in the 200–350 m³/s range, suggesting the system could reach that threshold within one to three weeks at the current rate of decline (roughly 50–80 m³/s per day), assuming no significant precipitation.

- **Mattawa oscillation pattern:** The June 27 rise (+22 cm) and June 28 correction (−39 cm) continue the pattern of high-amplitude swings in the Mattawa midnight series — four of the past six prior-day values have revised, sometimes by 20–30 cm. The June 28 preliminary (152.26 m) is itself subject to this same revision risk. The underlying trend at Mattawa (−46 cm over 7 days) is real, but day-to-day readings carry significant noise at this station.

- **Des Joachims gap watch:** The persistence of the ~186 m³/s gap between Des Joachims outflow (424 m³/s) and Rapide-2 (238 m³/s) for three or more consecutive days suggests ongoing storage management in the mid-reach. With Des Joachims now flat (0 cm reservoir level change, vs +6 cm yesterday), the water being "held" is likely in the river reach itself rather than the reservoir. As downstream demand increases or flows normalize, a Rapide-2 step-up of ~186 m³/s would flow through to Bryson and, after a travel-time delay, to Carillon.

- **Hull dock −12 cm:** Today's 12-centimetre drop at Hull dock (41.59→41.47 m) is the largest single-day move at that station in recent weeks. The station is now 114 cm below the §15.3.5.1 trigger — a significant buffer. No regulatory implication, but noted for the record.
