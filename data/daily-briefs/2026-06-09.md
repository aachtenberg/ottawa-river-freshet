### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property is now showing a second consecutive day of rising levels, confirming that yesterday's uptick was not a one-day blip. Overnight the level reached 106.30 metres — 5 centimetres above the previous midnight reading — and continued rising through the afternoon to 106.39 metres by 4 PM. Even with this uptick the lake is well within completely normal territory: 61 centimetres below the lowest cautionary alert level (107.00 m) and more than a metre below the minor flood threshold (107.50 m). No action is required at the property, and the ORRPB regional forecast (updated this afternoon for the first time since June 2) expects levels to remain stable or ease.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — continued its push into record territory. By 3 PM it reached 105.44 metres, another new event high, now 77 centimetres above the upper limit of the dam's normal operating range. This is Day 12 of the headpond sitting above that limit. What makes today notable is that Hydro-Québec has been releasing more water than ever during this episode — 514 cubic metres per second total, with 43 percent passing through the spillway — yet the headpond is still rising. This pattern confirms the problem is inflow-driven: more water is entering the reservoir from Lac Coulonge and its watershed than the dam can discharge. Lac Coulonge itself rising simultaneously (and now above its typical June level) is consistent with this picture.

The two dams immediately upstream on the HQ cascade (Rapide-2 and Rapide-7) that made large release cuts yesterday partially reversed course: Rapide-2 stepped back up about 5 percent. Whether yesterday's cuts were a grid-dispatch decision rather than a deliberate headpond management move is still unclear; today's data suggests no lasting drawdown intent. At Carillon, the basin's terminal dam near Hawkesbury, total releases fell another 4 to 5 percent (2,099 m³/s, down from 2,207 yesterday). The spillway remains closed, the headpond is stable, and there is no flood concern at the downstream end of the system.

## Notes

- **Bryson headpond: inflow-driven, not release-driven.** The simultaneous conditions — Lac Coulonge rising, Bryson releasing at episode-high rate, headpond at episode-high — point clearly to a situation where the watershed is still delivering more volume to the system than the dam can pass through. The failed headpond relief from Rapide-2/7 cuts confirms that the upper HQ cascade feeders are not the dominant driver. Lac Coulonge lateral drainage into the headpond forebay is. This has implications for the case file: the headpond level is being set by natural hydrological forcing, but the 12-day duration of the exceedance — and Hydro-Québec's ability to manage via the spillway — remains a question about operating posture rather than pure hydrology.

- **Lac Coulonge reversal.** Two consecutive rising days after 7+ days of gradual decline represents a directional change. The likely mechanism: the Bryson headpond, sitting 77 cm above the normal upper limit, may create reduced hydraulic draw from Lac Coulonge (the headpond is so high that the gradient between lake and headpond is reduced). This is a tentative physical interpretation — the data pattern is consistent with it, but not conclusive.

- **ORRPB forecast updated but silent on Bryson.** The June 9 update is the first official ORRPB assessment since before the headpond broke its event high, and since Carillon's two-day operational pivot. The forecast contains nothing about either development. The "fairly stable or decrease gradually" language applies to the main stem generally; it does not address the Bryson headpond situation (which is a HQ operational issue, not a main-stem flood-routing issue, from ORRPB's perspective).

- **Revision note.** PSPC Témiscaming June 8 revised from 771 to 727 m³/s (−44 m³/s); June 7 from 774 to 768 m³/s (−6 m³/s); June 6 from 791 to 782 m³/s (−9 m³/s). All sub-800 milestones remain valid. June 9 preliminary (684 m³/s) should be treated as provisional; expect possible revision of ±20–50 m³/s in tomorrow's brief.

- **Des Joachims uptick.** The Des Joachims reservoir rose 4 cm despite OPG outflow jumping from 890 to 972 m³/s. This single-day signal is within normal variability but may reflect mid-valley flow variations worth watching if it persists.
