## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The mid-season third wave is still building. The gauge at Mattawa — where flows from the northern half of the Ottawa's drainage area enter the main stem — reached 152.81 metres at midnight, its highest reading during this third pulse and now 20 centimetres above the secondary crest recorded June 12. The town of Mattawa remains 29 centimetres below the level reached during the spring's primary flood in early June, but the third rise shows no signs of topping out.

At the head of the basin near the Quebec-Ontario border, the outflow from Lake Temiscaming has been holding nearly flat around 770 cubic metres per second for three consecutive days. This is the headwater signal sustaining the Mattawa rise, and it has not yet resumed its post-peak decline.

Further downstream, Ontario Power Generation's Des Joachims dam continues to release elevated volumes. The midnight average was around 880 cubic metres per second, with intraday estimates on the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's website running above 1,200. That is more than double the Temiscaming outflow, meaning the Des Joachims reservoir is drawing down its own stored water and amplifying the downstream wave. That amplification is arriving at Pembroke — the last gauge before the water enters the lower Ottawa — where the level rose 5 centimetres overnight to 111.59 metres. Across the broader basin, nearly all reservoir levels are steady within a centimetre; only the Des Joachims impoundment itself rose (10 cm overnight), which is the normal signature of a large dam absorbing an incoming surge before passing it on.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the foot of Lac Coulonge has been essentially flat for 24 hours, holding at about 106.26 metres — essentially unchanged from yesterday and well below any flood threshold, with the nearest alert level 74 centimetres away. There is no near-term flood concern for the shoreline community.

At the Bryson dam — which controls the outflow from Lac Coulonge — there is one piece of genuinely encouraging news: for the first time in 22 days, the water backed up behind the dam dropped slightly, falling 3 centimetres to 105.51 metres. This is the first easing since the headpond began rising on May 29. It remains 84 centimetres above the dam's permitted upper operating limit, but the direction has finally reversed. The turbines are still completely offline — their fifth consecutive day without generating power — and all roughly 488 cubic metres per second of flow is going through the open spillway. Without turbine operation, headpond recovery will be slow. No public timeline for turbine restart has been announced.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board forecast has not been updated since early Wednesday morning and is not due again until Monday. It continues to warn that gradual level increases are possible over the coming week, with rapid increases possible if heavy rainfall arrives. Given that the third pulse is still building upstream and the Des Joachims surge is working through the mid-valley, the lakeshore community remains in a steady watch situation.
