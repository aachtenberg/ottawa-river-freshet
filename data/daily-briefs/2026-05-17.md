## In plain language

The lake at Fort-Coulonge and Mansfield dropped about 5 centimetres overnight — the sharpest single-day decline since early May — and is now 16 centimetres above the level where it officially counts as a minor flood. That's a genuine positive shift after more than a week of near-stall conditions (1–2 cm per day). If today's pace holds and the weather stays dry, the property could drop below the minor-flood mark around May 20 or 21, about 3–4 days from now.

The dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson, operated by Hydro-Québec) is passing roughly the same total water as yesterday, but its own holding pond dropped 8 centimetres today and is now sitting just 4 centimetres above the lower edge of its normal operating range. Operators are likely to ease back their releases slightly in coming days to keep the pond from dipping below that floor. Meanwhile, at the basin's terminal dam (Carillon, near Grenville, Quebec), something notable happened this morning: operators shifted about 530 cubic metres per second from the power turbines to the open spillways — keeping total water output essentially unchanged, but altering how the dam is run. This appears to be a generation or maintenance decision rather than a flood-management move, since the total flow through the dam didn't change.

The river management board's forecast has not been updated since Thursday, May 15. Their next bulletin is not expected until Tuesday, May 19. The standing forecast calls for stable water levels for the next few days, followed by a gradual decline later next week. No further rises or new rain events are flagged.

For property owners: this is the most encouraging gauge reading in nearly two weeks. Three or four dry days could be enough to push the lake below minor-flood status. One concern to watch: the regulatory ceiling at Carillon that is supposed to limit how high the dam's headpond sits during spring floods continues to be exceeded — now 43 centimetres above the limit and into its 13th consecutive day — and the downstream gauge that triggers that ceiling has barely budged in 48 hours. The transition to normal operating posture at Carillon depends on that upstream gauge dropping first.
