## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The outflow from Lake Timiscaming came in at a preliminary 365 cubic metres per second today, which appears slightly higher than yesterday's confirmed figure of 327. The preliminary figures in this series are routinely revised — yesterday's preliminary of 349 confirmed 22 units lower at 327 — so today's reading carries similar uncertainty. What's clear regardless is that we're in the third consecutive day with the Timiscaming outflow below 400 cubic metres per second. That threshold wasn't crossed until July 13th, after a 75-day post-freshet recession from the May 2nd spring peak.

The seasonal draw-down is now unmistakable at the Mattawa gauge, which tracks the upper-middle Ottawa valley. The midnight reading there was 152.13 metres, a 21-centimetre drop from yesterday's confirmed 152.34. This is the second consecutive day below the narrow oscillation band (roughly 152.35–152.55 metres) that had prevailed for most of the past ten days, confirming the break is real rather than a data artifact. Mattawa is now 22 centimetres below that band's lower edge. The Pembroke gauge, farther downstream, was flat overnight at 111.22 metres.

Several upper Hydro-Québec cascade sites showed significant step-ups in releases today: a generating station upstream in the cascade (Première-Chute) released 30 percent more water than yesterday, the Îles station 21 percent more, and the Quinze station 11 percent more. These are large single-day moves and may reflect a change in dispatch scheduling or reservoir management decisions. Partially offsetting this, the Gatineau River tributaries entering the Ottawa near the basin's terminal dam dropped sharply (around 27–28 percent each), and Carillon itself ran at much lower dispatch levels today — roughly 1,250 cubic metres per second through mid-afternoon, versus 1,660 at the same time yesterday. The lower Carillon output may also reflect reduced releases from the Des Joachims generating station (OPG facility upstream), which continues to show near-zero flow in the tracking data.

Across the 14 tracked storage reservoirs, 13 are flat within measurement noise and one (the Dozois reservoir in Quebec) fell 3 centimetres overnight. No reservoirs are filling rapidly. The basin is in a quiet late-season state with normal post-freshet baseflow dynamics.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The Vigilance gauge at the Fort-Coulonge waterfront read 106.10 metres at 4 PM today — a rise of about 8 centimetres from yesterday afternoon, ending what had been a 9-day declining trend since the Canada Day event peaked on July 5th. The overnight ORRPB midnight series confirms the same reversal: 106.10 metres (preliminary) for the July 15 midnight reading, up 7 centimetres from the confirmed 106.03 at midnight July 14. The ORRPB conditions website independently shows 106.1 metres at 3 PM, consistent with both readings. The cause of the reversal isn't apparent from available data — modest local rainfall in the Lac Coulonge watershed is the most plausible explanation, but the routine doesn't have weather data to confirm it. In any case the lake is thoroughly normal for mid-July: 90 centimetres below the level at which any informal advisory would begin.

Several kilometres downstream of the property — reached by following the south end of the lake as it narrows and winds down through several kilometres to the Bryson Generating Station — the dam pond fell 11 centimetres today to 105.28 metres, while the dam's total output climbed 17 percent to 529 cubic metres per second. Almost two-thirds of that release (63 percent) is now going through the spillway rather than the turbines — consistent with several days of heavy-spill operation. The intraday pattern today was different from yesterday's complex morning-turbine/afternoon-spill shift: today Bryson moved to majority-spill operation around 6 AM local time and stayed there all day. The dam pond remains 61 centimetres above the top of its standard operating range, in its 48th consecutive day above that ceiling.

The ORRPB forecast is unchanged from the update issued yesterday afternoon: water levels are described as "within seasonal values" and expected to "decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." The next update is scheduled for July 21st. The transient Lac Coulonge rise today would not be inconsistent with "remain fairly stable" language — it's a small move within a normal seasonal range.
