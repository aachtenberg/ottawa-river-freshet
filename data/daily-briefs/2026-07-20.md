Mid-valley gauges tell a story of shifting operator dispatch. That is the largest single-night drop at Mattawa in the recent brief record and almost certainly reflects a sharp cut in output from Ontario Power Generation's Des Joachims dam (an OPG facility on the Ontario side of the river), which shed 169 m³/s of release overnight — from 406 m³/s to 237 m³/s, a 42% reduction. Pembroke, further downstream, slipped 2 centimetres. All 14 reservoirs with comparable level data are essentially flat — one mild outlier is the Timiskaming reservoir level, which dipped 5 centimetres (likely tracking the reduced Lake Timiscaming outflow), while all others held within a centimetre.

A note on Carillon — the basin's terminal dam — that corrects yesterday's framing. The hourly Hydro-Québec reading at Carillon at 3 PM today was 1,182 m³/s, higher than yesterday's 1,098. But the ORRPB daily average for Carillon tells a different story: 1,016 m³/s for today, down from 1,022 yesterday and 1,037 the day before. The daily average is a better representation of actual basin throughput; the HQ hourly figures show intraday dispatch variability that can look dramatic without reflecting a meaningful operational trend. On that basis, Carillon is in a very gentle declining trend, not rising. Yesterday's "Carillon reversal" flag was based on the hourly reading and should be read in that context.

The lake is in its fully normal summer range and sits about 95 centimetres below the 107-metre level at which any informal advisory attention begins. Several kilometres downstream — reached by following Lac Coulonge's south end as it narrows and winds a considerable distance down through the lake's outlet before arriving at the Bryson dam — the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station experienced a sharp reversal today. After three consecutive days of the pond creeping upward to within 5 centimetres of the June 21 all-time recorded high (105.56 m), Hydro-Québec operators significantly increased flow through the spillway: spill jumped from 97 to 138 cubic metres per second, a 43% increase, while turbine output held essentially flat. Total release rose by 40 m³/s to 429 m³/s. The result was a 17-centimetre drop in the dam pond level, from 105.51 m yesterday to 105.34 m today. The pond is still well above its standard operating range — 67 centimetres above the nominal 104.67 m ceiling — and Day 53 of the headpond breach continues. But the creep toward a new all-time record has been arrested: the pond is now 22 centimetres below the June 21 high.

The ORRPB forecast, unchanged since July 14, calls for gradual decrease or stable conditions. The next update is expected tomorrow, July 21 at 4 PM EDT — the first update in a week.

## Notes

- **Sub-300 correction is the highest-priority continuity note.** The July 18 "295 confirmed" value was a large preliminary PSPC revision that proved inaccurate. The +68 m³/s upward revision (to 363 m³/s) is unusually large for PSPC; it may reflect a reporting artifact, a revised upstream-inflow accounting, or integration of additional gauge data. Whatever the cause, the current series (327–372 m³/s for the sub-400 streak, trending toward 333) remains well above 300. The first genuine sub-300 reading, if it comes, will appear clearly in the series.

- **Bryson drawdown was almost certainly deliberate.** Three consecutive days of small headpond gains (+15/+2/+2 cm, Jul17–19) brought the pond to 5 cm from the Jun21 all-time record. Operators responded on Jul20 with a 43% spill increase. The pond dropped 17 cm. This is a consistent pattern with operators managing an informal upper ceiling for the headpond; the standard ceiling (104.67 m) was breached 53 days ago and has not been enforced, but the Jun21 record (105.56 m) appears to be a practical upper limit they are avoiding.

- **Mattawa −21 cm is worth watching.** Tomorrow's Mattawa midnight reading will clarify whether this was a one-day dispatch pulse or the start of a sustained level reduction at mid-valley. The Des Joachims cut (406→237 m³/s) was sharp; if that level is maintained, Mattawa should stabilize or decline further. If OPG reverts Des Joachims dispatch upward, Mattawa will recover. Either way, the reading has no immediate implications for Lac Coulonge.

- **ORRPB update due tomorrow (July 21 4 PM EDT).** This will be the first forecast update since July 14 and the first that can reflect the Bryson headpond behaviour, the Carillon intraday variability, and any rainfall outlook for the upper basin. Watch for any language change on "stable vs. decrease" framing.
