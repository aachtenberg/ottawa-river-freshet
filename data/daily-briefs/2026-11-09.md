## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The outflow from Lake Timiskaming, the top of the main stem, averaged 382 cubic metres per second on November 9. That is 191 days after the May 2 peak of 2,741, and the 53rd day in a row below 400. It is a little higher than a week earlier (353), which fits a quiet late-autumn river rather than any new loading.

Further down the valley the gauge at Mattawa eased 5 centimetres and the one at Pembroke was essentially unchanged. Of the fourteen storage reservoirs tracked across the basin, 11 were within two centimetres of the day before, 2 fell slightly (Lake Timiskaming and Lady Evelyn, three centimetres each) and one rose (Poisson Blanc, six centimetres). Nothing in that pattern suggests operators were holding water back.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake gauge at Fort-Coulonge read 106.015 metres at 9 PM UTC on November 9, about two centimetres higher than the same hour the day before. That is normal for the season: 99 centimetres below the first alert level of 107.00 metres and 149 centimetres below the minor-flood level. The river board's weekly outlook, issued November 3, expected levels to stay stable or rise slightly with rain.

The notable event of the day was at the Bryson Generating Station, which sits well downstream of the lake at the end of a long, winding channel and is not next to the Mansfield shoreline. Its turbines were taken off through the morning: output fell from about 168 cubic metres per second to 56 at 7 AM Eastern and to zero from 8 AM, and stayed at zero for the rest of the day. All of the river's flow then went over the spillway instead. The total passing the dam did not drop; it rose from about 358 to 430 cubic metres per second by evening. The dam pond rose about eight centimetres during the switch and was back to its starting level by late evening. Because the dam stores almost no water, this is a change in the route the water takes, not in how much reaches the river below, and it had no effect on the lake.
