Twelve are essentially flat, and two (Lake Témiscaming itself and Poisson Blanc) dipped 3 centimetres — consistent with quiet, continued outflow as the basin settles toward its stable summer level.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Two notable developments today, running in opposite directions.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — reached a new event high for the spring: 105.34 metres at 3 PM today. That is 14 centimetres above the previous peak set on June 1 and 67 centimetres above the upper limit of the dam's normal operating range. This is Day 11 of the headpond sitting above that ceiling, and the trend is upward rather than easing. Late in the afternoon, the two dams immediately upstream on the Hydro-Québec cascade (Rapide-2 and Rapide-7) both made significant release reductions — cutting back by roughly 15–20% in the final data hour before this snapshot. Whether this is the start of coordinated headpond relief at Bryson or simply a grid-dispatch adjustment remains to be seen; the headpond level has not yet responded.

At the same time, Lac Coulonge itself (the Fort-Coulonge gauge used by local property owners) ticked up 4 centimetres overnight to 106.26 metres, interrupting a run of daily declines. This is a small reversal, not an alarm: the lake is 74 centimetres below the first cautionary threshold, still in fully normal territory.

At Carillon — the terminal dam near Hawkesbury, at the far downstream end of the basin — operators completely reversed yesterday's dramatic operational shift. The spillway discharge that had risen to 315 cubic metres per second yesterday was shut to zero, and total releases fell nearly 12%. The dam is now in turbines-only mode. Two major operational changes in two consecutive days is unusual. The Carillon headpond rose 8 centimetres in response. Neither development creates a flood concern at the property; Carillon is at the opposite end of the system.

The ORRPB regional forecast is due for its first update since June 2 tomorrow, Monday June 9. Watch for any revised language on the Bryson headpond exceedance or the Carillon posture changes.
