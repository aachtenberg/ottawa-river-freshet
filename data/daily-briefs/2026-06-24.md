## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper basin is easing faster than the preliminary numbers suggested. Yesterday's estimated outflow from Lake Temiscaming — which appeared to be 748 cubic metres per second — has been revised sharply downward to 673 m³/s once the authoritative federal water-survey data was finalized. That means the "above-700" streak actually ended yesterday (not today), and today's reading of 630 m³/s is already the second consecutive day below that threshold. This is the fastest the discharge has fallen since the June 20 surge peak. It now sits 154 m³/s below where it was just one week ago.

That falling pulse has begun to clear the mid-valley reach in a striking way. The gauge at Mattawa — the first major community about 200 kilometres downstream of Lake Temiscaming — dropped 35 centimetres overnight to 152.32 metres. That is the sharpest single-day decline at Mattawa recorded in these briefs, almost certainly reflecting a wave of reduced flow now clearing the reach. (This is a preliminary midnight reading and may be partially revised, but the direction is unambiguous.) Pembroke, further downstream, is essentially flat (+1 cm). The reservoir balance across the full basin has settled into a neutral posture: all 14 tracked storage lakes held within 2 centimetres of their prior level, and the Des Joachims pool — which had been absorbing a little extra inflow for the past few days — has returned to flat. Operators are neither stockpiling water nor hurrying to release it.

The generating stations along the upper cascade stepped down sharply overnight. Both the Première-Chute dam (the first major facility below Lake Temiscaming) and the Quinze dam cut output by about 10 percent. The Îles and Rapide-2 stations reduced output similarly. At the basin's terminal dam, Carillon, operators cut total releases by about 9 percent and — notably — stopped sending any water through the spillway at all, routing everything through the turbines. Carillon's spillway had been partially open for the past two days as operators worked down elevated water levels; closing it today signals that the headpond there is back within a manageable range.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Today brought a slowdown in the pace of decline at Lac Coulonge. The lake dropped about 3 centimetres over the 24-hour period to 106.19 metres — a substantial deceleration from the 10-centimetre drop recorded the previous day. The lake is fully in normal territory, more than 80 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold of 107 metres, and there is no immediate concern of water returning to flood levels absent heavy precipitation.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge (the backed-up water surface in front of the Bryson Generating Station) did something notable today: it rose 6 centimetres, reversing the two-day drawdown that had been underway since the pond set its all-time high on June 21st. The reason is that operators cut the flow going through the open spillway by about 36 cubic metres per second — a 7 percent reduction in release — while the water arriving from upstream held roughly steady. With slightly more water coming in than going out, the pond filled a little. At 105.41 metres, it is still 74 centimetres above the operators' permitted upper ceiling of 104.67 metres, and all the generating units remain shut down for the tenth consecutive day. The pond is a long way from the operating range that would allow turbine restart.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast is unchanged: conditions are "expected to decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." The board updated to weekly summer monitoring cadence on June 23; the next scheduled update is June 30.
