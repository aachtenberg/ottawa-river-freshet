## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin is on Day 45 of its post-peak recession. The outflow at Lake Timiskaming — the main gate separating the northern half of the basin from the main Ottawa River stem — came in at 696 cubic metres per second today, still barely beneath the 700 m³/s threshold that has held for seven straight days. That figure is slightly higher than yesterday's revised reading of 671 m³/s, and the federal monitoring service puts today's number at 674 — both sources agree to within 22 cubic metres per second, well within normal day-to-day measurement noise. Note that today's reading is preliminary and may be revised.

The mid-valley precipitation pulse — the surge from the June 9–10 rain event — has now largely cleared the mid-Ottawa. The level at Mattawa fell another 11 centimetres overnight (to 152.39 metres), the level at Otto-Holden barely budged (up just 1 centimetre) after yesterday's dramatic 12-centimetre surge, and Pembroke is holding flat. The pulse is now working through the Des Joachims reservoir, which is actively routing a high outflow of 931 cubic metres per second while its own forebay rose 6 centimetres. Pembroke is next in the path; arrival is expected in the next 24–48 hours.

The basin's reservoir inventory remains very quiet. Only one tracked lake — Des Joachims, in active pulse-routing mode — rose more than 2 centimetres overnight. All other thirteen reservoirs were within 2 centimetres of their previous reading, with no active large-scale storage filling or release surges anywhere in the basin.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Today's headline is a major change in how Hydro-Québec is operating the Bryson Generating Station — the dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge. Starting around 9 in the morning local time, the plant's turbines were effectively taken out of service: the volume of water passing through the generators dropped from about 288 cubic metres per second to just 35 — a reduction of nearly 88 per cent. The remaining flow — roughly 463 cubic metres per second — was rerouted through the dam's spillway gates instead. In other words, the plant is currently passing almost its entire water flow as an open bypass, generating almost no electricity. The cause is not stated in public data, but the pattern is consistent with a planned or emergency maintenance outage.

The direct consequence for the dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge is significant: the headpond rose 13 centimetres since yesterday afternoon, reaching 105.47 metres as of 3 PM. That is the highest the pond has been in this 18-day-long operating anomaly — 80 centimetres above the dam's normal operating ceiling of 104.67 metres. The rate of rise (13 cm in one day) is the largest single-day increase in the entire breach period. Whether the headpond stabilizes or continues to climb depends on how long the turbine outage persists. In a parallel development, the Première-Chute plant — the first HQ station upstream in the tributary chain — also shifted about 140 cubic metres per second from turbines to spillway today, a similar but smaller pattern.

Lac Coulonge itself responded mildly: the lake surface fell about 2 centimetres since yesterday, to about 106.22 metres at the midnight reading and 106.21 metres at the 3 PM reading. At that level the property remains 79 centimetres below the lowest alert threshold of 107 metres — no flood concern. The gentle multi-day decline has resumed now that the mid-valley pulse has largely passed through. At the far end of the river, Carillon — the basin's terminal dam — reversed yesterday's large release cut with a comparable increase today (up 9.5%), adjusting to daily inflow variation as it normally does.

The ORRPB forecast has been unchanged since June 9 and is due for its next weekly update tomorrow afternoon at 4 PM ET. That update should reflect the Bryson turbine event, the Des Joachims routing pulse now approaching Pembroke, and the overall easing trend.
