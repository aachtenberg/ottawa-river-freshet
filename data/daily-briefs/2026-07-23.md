## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The daily outflow from Lake Timiscaming — the natural lake at the top of the Ottawa River basin — came in at 365 cubic metres per second today, according to the river regulator's database. A second independent source puts today's number slightly higher at 391 cubic metres per second. Both readings are well under 400 cubic metres per second, making today the eleventh consecutive day that the lake's outflow has stayed below that round-number mark. The confirmed minimum over this stretch remains 328 cubic metres per second, reached on July 14.

Yesterday's provisional outflow reading has been revised — in the unusual direction, downward. Yesterday's brief reported a preliminary reading of 390 (one source) or 378 (cross-check) for July 22. Both sources today confirm 350 cubic metres per second for that day, a downward revision of roughly 40 cubic metres per second. That kind of downward correction has been rare in this year's data; the more common pattern has been upward revisions of provisional readings. July 21 is now being revised in the opposite direction: one source, which had been updated to 348 a day ago, now shows 389, while the cross-check source still reads 348. These day-to-day revisions are a feature of the preliminary data system, and both values remain safely in the sub-400 range regardless. The basic story — the upper lake has been draining at a moderate summer baseflow rate in the 328–391 cubic-metres-per-second range for eleven days — is unchanged.

The basin's major reservoirs are collectively in a settled late-summer posture. Eleven of the fourteen tracked reservoirs are essentially flat, within two centimetres of where they were a day earlier. One mid-valley Ontario reservoir (Des Joachims) ticked up a modest three centimetres. The lake at the top of the basin (Lake Timiscaming itself) dipped slightly, down four centimetres from yesterday, consistent with its outflow running somewhat above recent inflows. The ORRPB's published water levels at Mattawa — the mid-valley town that sits roughly halfway down the basin — have ticked upward slightly the past two days (from 152.31 to 152.38 to 152.43 metres). That level has been oscillating within a roughly 30-centimetre band all summer and shows no sustained trend in either direction; the small uptick over the past two days is within that normal variation. Pembroke, further downstream, continues a gradual multi-week drift downward, ticking down six centimetres today to 111.01 metres.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The ORRPB's midnight-to-midnight average for July 23 confirms 105.97 metres, down three centimetres from yesterday's midnight reading of 106.00. The lake continues its slow, ordinary post-peak decline and sits approximately one metre below the level at which any kind of advisory or pre-alert would begin. There is no flood concern of any kind here.

Several kilometres downstream of where Lac Coulonge's south end narrows and winds its way toward the Bryson dam site, the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station today sits at 105.37 metres — up two centimetres from yesterday, and now in its 56th consecutive day above the station's standard operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, sitting 70 centimetres above that ceiling and 19 centimetres below the all-time recorded high set on June 21. The amount of water flowing over Bryson's spillway today was 95.28 cubic metres per second — down 17.5% from yesterday's 115.52 cubic metres per second. That is a meaningful reduction in spill, the largest single-day change at Bryson in the past two weeks. Total throughput through the dam fell by about five percent. The reason is likely a modest increase in head (the driving pressure of water above the turbines) and a decision to run more flow through the turbines rather than the spillway — turbine flow is essentially unchanged while spill was pulled back. The pond level correspondingly ticked up rather than continuing to fall.
