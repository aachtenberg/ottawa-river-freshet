## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa watershed continues its long slide toward summer base-flow levels. The daily measurement at the outlet of Lake Témiscaming — the gauge that captures how much water the entire upper basin is releasing into the main stem — recorded 400 cubic metres per second as of yesterday's official daily tally, and that figure remained in the database unchanged overnight. We are now in the second consecutive day at or below that threshold, compared to nearly 2,750 cubic metres per second at the May 2 freshet peak. The planning board's official tally for June 29 won't be published until sometime tomorrow, but the steady downward trend makes a similar or slightly lower reading the most likely outcome. At 400 cubic metres per second, the upper basin is releasing roughly one-seventh of its peak-spring rate — well into recession territory and approaching the kind of base-flow volumes typical of a dry midsummer stretch.

About 200 kilometres downstream, the main-stem gauge at Mattawa continues to ease. After an unexplained 22-centimetre overnight surge on June 27 — most likely a rain-driven flush from local tributaries rather than any signal from the upper basin — and a sharp 39-centimetre correction the following morning, the river at Mattawa settled at 152.26 metres as of yesterday's midnight reading. That is 42 centimetres lower than seven days ago and 17 centimetres below even the pre-surge level. No June 29 Mattawa reading has been published yet, but the directional signal is clear. At Pembroke, one step further downstream, the most recently available figure (still lagged by one day) showed the river continuing to ease steadily. Every major upstream storage reservoir — more than a dozen tracked by the system — sat within a centimetre of its prior-day reading. The basin is in a calm summer-holding posture, with operators neither releasing bulk water nor holding back notable amounts.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The water level at the official gauge that tracks the lake condition near the Mansfield waterfront properties is continuing its slow, quiet decline. The most recent hourly reading from that gauge — taken around 9 PM Eastern time last night, just before the telemetry from the Quebec government's water-monitoring network went offline — showed the water at 106.116 metres, about 1.8 centimetres lower than the same hour the previous night. It is currently 88 centimetres below the level that would trigger even a pre-flood advisory (107.00 metres), and close to a metre and a half below the minor-flood threshold. The property area is fully normal.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — an entirely separate location from the lake gauge, accessed by following the narrowing south channel of Lac Coulonge several kilometres down to the dam itself — sat at 105.38 metres as of yesterday evening, the most recent data from Hydro-Québec. This is Day 32 that the dam pond has exceeded the operators' permitted upper ceiling of 104.67 metres, with the water currently sitting 71 centimetres above that limit. It is also Day 15 with all generating turbines sitting idle. Over the past seven days, the dam pond has moved barely 4 centimetres in total, showing no meaningful progress toward returning to the permitted operating range. Without a significant operator-driven step-up in the flow through the spillway gates, the timeline for resolution remains open.

Tomorrow's Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board forecast update — due at 4 PM Eastern, the first new forecast since June 23 — will be worth watching. As of six days ago, the board described conditions as "within seasonal values" and forecast gradual decline or stability. No flood watches are in effect.
