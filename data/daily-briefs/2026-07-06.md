## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa basin's post-event recession deepened today on two fronts. The Témiscaming outflow — the rate at which water drains south from Lake Timiscaming into the main-stem cascade — fell a second consecutive day to 890 cubic metres per second, down from 906 yesterday and well off the event peak of 929 on July 4. This is the 66th day since the spring freshet peak in early May. More significantly, Mattawa — the mid-valley monitoring point roughly two-thirds of the way between Lake Timiscaming and the regulated dam chain — finally broke its two-day plateau and fell 7 centimetres overnight to 152.81 metres. That is the first clear drop since the July 3 series high of 152.93 metres. The precipitation pulse from the long Canada Day weekend has now crested at every upstream station.

The major news today is a coordinated operational adjustment in the Hydro-Québec cascade that unfolded this afternoon (roughly 1–3 PM Eastern time). Three stations in the upper part of the Quebec dam chain — Première-Chute, Quinze, and Îles — each cut their releases by approximately 110–136 cubic metres per second, a reduction of 26–33%. Simultaneously, the two stations immediately upstream of the Bryson dam pond (Rapide-2 and Rapide-7), which had been held at a reduced level for two days in an apparent effort to let the Bryson headpond drain, were restored to roughly their normal operating level. On the Gatineau River, Paugan dam went from dry spillways yesterday to passing 76 cubic metres per second over its spillway — about 27% of its total discharge. The net effect of these changes is still propagating through the system, but the upper-cascade cuts should reduce the volume arriving at Bryson over the next hours to days, while the restored Rapide-2/7 flows reflect the Bryson headpond situation stabilising. Reservoir operators continue in a pure pass-through posture: five of the fourteen tracked storage reservoirs are gently falling and none are rising.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge is essentially flat today. The Vigilance gauge at the Mansfield waterfront read 106.577 metres at 4 PM Eastern — less than one centimetre above the same reading yesterday afternoon. The overnight midnight-to-midnight rise (from 106.57 metres at midnight to 106.60 metres the following midnight) was just 3 centimetres, a significant deceleration from the 4 and 14 centimetre rises earlier in the week. The lake remains in the fully normal flood category, sitting 42 centimetres below the 107-metre level at which a pre-alert advisory would apply, and nearly a full metre below the 107.50-metre minor-flood threshold. The driving force of the Canada Day weekend precipitation event has passed at this gauge.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — reached by following the south end of Lac Coulonge's narrows several kilometres down to the dam, well below the property stretch — fell a further 5 centimetres to 105.33 metres at 3 PM Eastern. This is the second consecutive day of decline after the precipitation-event reversal of July 3–4, and the headpond is now the lowest it has been since July 1. The operating rules require the dam pond to remain between 104.20 and 104.67 metres, and today's level remains 66 centimetres above that upper limit — the 39th consecutive day of the exceedance. The pace of improvement is slow; at roughly 5–10 centimetres per day, returning to compliance would take weeks unless operating conditions change substantially.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast has not been updated since July 2, when it said water levels were "expected to increase gradually over the next few days." Those increases arrived and have now largely peaked. The next update is expected tomorrow, July 7, at 4 PM Eastern — the first formal ORRPB guidance since the basin crested.
