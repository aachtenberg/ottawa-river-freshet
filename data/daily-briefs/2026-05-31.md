## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa River's post-peak recession entered its 30th day today, with the flow leaving Lake Temiscaming at roughly 1,197 cubic metres per second — a new milestone as the first day (on a preliminary basis) that flows have dropped below the 1,200 mark. There is a small footnote: yesterday's brief identified the same milestone prematurely, based on a provisional reading of 1,199 m³/s for May 30 that was subsequently revised upward to 1,205 (just above 1,200). The true sub-1,200 threshold appears to have been crossed today. Since the May 2 freshet peak of approximately 2,741 m³/s, Temiscaming has shed more than 1,500 m³/s — just over half the peak has now receded. The pace of decline has slowed dramatically, to about 8 m³/s per day from the 7-day average of 40 m³/s per day.

The most notable development in the mid-valley today is at Pembroke, the river-level gauge roughly two-thirds of the way between Lake Temiscaming and the property. After 14 or more consecutive days of declining water levels, Pembroke rose 3 centimetres overnight. This is the expected arrival of the elevated flows released from the Des Joachims reservoir on May 30, when the Ontario Power Generation operator opened the gates substantially after a seven-day accumulation period. That pulse is now tracking downstream: Mattawa (halfway along) has been rising gently for three consecutive days, and Pembroke's uptick today confirms the wave is past the halfway mark. It should reach the Lac Coulonge area around June 2–3.

Reservoirs across the basin remain largely stable — 12 of 14 are flat, one is slowly filling (Des Joachims, up 7 centimetres overnight at 151.88 metres), and one is drawing down (Dozois, 3 centimetres lower). This is a normal late-recession posture.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The river at Fort-Coulonge dropped 9 centimetres overnight, reaching 106.38 metres as of midnight. Afternoon readings from the automated sensor confirmed the level was holding steady around that mark throughout the day — virtually unchanged between midnight and 4 PM Eastern. The lake remains firmly in summer-normal territory: 62 centimetres below the first warning threshold (107.00 metres, the "pre-alert" mark) and 112 centimetres below the minor-flood level (107.50 metres). All warning flags remain off.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — Bryson Generating Station's headpond — continued its unusual upward drift for a third consecutive day. At 105.08 metres, it is now 41 centimetres above the top of its normal 47-centimetre operating range (104.20 to 104.67 metres). More strikingly, the operator made a sharp shift overnight: turbine generation was cut by 43% (from roughly 233 to 133 cubic metres per second), while the share going through the spillway jumped from about 58% to nearly 76% of all flow. Essentially, three-quarters of the water is now bypassing the turbines entirely. Despite this near-shutdown of generation, the headpond level continued rising — now into its 11th consecutive day outside the normal operating range (8 days below, then 3 days above the limits). The Des Joachims pulse expected to arrive around June 2–3 may add further inflow pressure before conditions stabilise.

At the basin's downstream terminal — the Carillon dam near Hawkesbury — total releases fell 4% and spillway flows dropped nearly 20%. The regulatory trigger (which requires Carillon to stay below 40.08 metres when the Hull dock gauge exceeds 42.61) remains inactive for a sixth consecutive day; the Hull dock fell further to 42.09 metres today, now 52 centimetres below the trigger threshold. The ORRPB weekly forecast (unchanged since May 26, next update June 2) continues to project conditions "within seasonal values...expected to remain stable or decrease gradually."

## Notes

- **Bryson headpond trajectory:** With the Des Joachims pulse expected to add to Lac Coulonge inflow around June 2–3, Bryson faces two to three more days of potentially elevated upstream supply before any respite. The operator's shift to near-total spillway routing (75.5%) at the expense of generation is consistent with a damage-mitigation posture rather than normal operation. Key signal to watch: if total release increases alongside the turbine-to-spill rebalancing, it would suggest the operator is allowing more water through in aggregate rather than just rerouting. If the headpond continues rising past 105.08 m despite the turbine cutback, that is the next escalating data point.

- **Pembroke uptick and Lac Coulonge deceleration:** The Lac Coulonge decline rate has been 7–15 cm/day over the past week. Today's −9 cm/day could reflect natural exponential-tail deceleration of the recession curve, or the early edge of the Des Joachims pulse. The lake's 112 cm buffer to minor flood is very comfortable regardless.

Milestones near round-number boundaries (sub-1,200, sub-1,000) should be treated as provisional for one day. The correct milestone sequence to date: Sub-1,250 Day 1 = May 29 (1,207 finalized); Sub-1,200 Day 1 = May 31 (1,197 provisional; confirm tomorrow).

- **ORRPB June 2 update:** The next scheduled ORRPB forecast (June 2) may signal a transition to off-season or further-reduced cadence if basin conditions continue normalizing. Worth watching whether the bulletin language shifts from "stable or decrease gradually" toward recreational/summer guidance, which would be a case-file milestone in its own right.
