## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa River Planning Board published a new weekly forecast today — the first update in seven days — and its message is unambiguous: water is going down. The new text explicitly says flows and levels "have decreased over the past week and are expected to continue decreasing gradually or remain fairly stable." The previous forecast from June 23, by contrast, had described an increase following recent rainfall. That rainfall pulse has now worked its way through the system, and the planning board is signalling the basin is back on a declining trajectory. The next forecast is not due until July 7.

At the upper end of the basin, the outlet of Lake Témiscaming (the large lake that straddles the Ontario-Quebec border near the headwaters) released an estimated 518 cubic metres per second today — a jump from the confirmed 450 m³/s on June 29. A brief correction is in order: yesterday's brief reported a provisional June 29 value of 495 m³/s and described a "third day of consecutive rise." The confirmed figure was revised downward to 450, lower than both June 27 (460 m³/s) and June 28 (468 m³/s). The four-day stretch where Témiscaming outflow held below 500 m³/s (June 26 through 29) has now ended: today's 518 m³/s — a preliminary figure subject to possible revision — is back above that round-number threshold. At 518 m³/s, the system is moving about 19% of what it was at the May 2 peak of roughly 2,741 m³/s. The upper basin is clearly in recession; today's reading is likely a day-to-day variation, not a new trend reversal.

Halfway down the valley, the town of Mattawa held essentially flat at 152.15 metres (a barely perceptible 1-centimetre decline from June 29), while Pembroke, further southeast along the main stem, fell 6 centimetres to 111.30 metres. Both stations remain in steady summer-recession mode with no flood concerns. The basin's storage reservoirs were largely unchanged overnight: Poisson Blanc — a downstream Quebec reservoir — fell 7 centimetres, while the upstream reach behind the Des Joachims generating station on the Ontario side rose 8 centimetres. All other reservoirs held steady. The overall balance is 1 falling, 11 steady, and 1 rising — a very calm picture compared to yesterday's more active pattern.

A significant operational reversal happened at the Des Joachims station operated by Ontario Power Generation. Yesterday, OPG had sharply cut their output from about 628 to 347 cubic metres per second — a large step-down. Today they reversed course completely, releasing an estimated 559 cubic metres per second — well above even the pre-cut level. This swing created visible pulses upstream and downstream: the reach above the dam rose 37 centimetres and the reach between Des Joachims and the basin-terminal Carillon dam rose 30 centimetres. The Carillon dam itself, near the Quebec-Ontario border at the bottom of the Ottawa valley, held almost perfectly steady — essentially 1,680 cubic metres per second in and out, unchanged from yesterday.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The government telemetry gauge that tracks conditions near the Mansfield waterfront properties — located on Lac Coulonge itself, not at the dam several kilometres down the lake's narrowing south channel — read 106.128 metres at 4 PM Eastern time today. That is about 1 centimetre higher than the same hour yesterday (106.116 m), but well within the daily noise level. The planning board's official midnight reading for June 30 was 106.12 metres — identical to June 29. Lac Coulonge is flat, sitting about 88 centimetres below the pre-alert advisory level of 107.00 metres and almost a metre and a half below the minor flood threshold. Conditions are fully normal.

The dam pond at Bryson Generating Station — located well downstream of the lake, reached by following the south channel as it narrows and winds several kilometres to where Hydro-Québec's dam sits — entered its second day of resumed turbine operation today. Turbine output tripled from yesterday's restart level of about 35 cubic metres per second up to roughly 128 today, reducing the share of water going through the spillway from 93% to about 73%. Despite slightly lower total releases overall (down about 5%), the dam pond level fell 9 centimetres to 105.37 metres — the first day-over-day easing of the pond in the recent period. That said, the pond remains 70 centimetres above the operators' permitted upper ceiling of 104.67 metres, and today marks the 33rd consecutive day of that exceedance. Whether the turbine ramp-up will translate into sustained pond drawdown will become clearer over the coming days.

The ORRPB's new forecast contains no specific outlook for Lac Coulonge or Bryson — only a general statement that levels are declining basin-wide. No further-increase risk is flagged anywhere in the forecast.
