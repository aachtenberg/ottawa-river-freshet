## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The daily outflow from Lake Timiscaming — the large natural lake at the top of the Ottawa River basin — fell to approximately 323–328 cubic metres per second today, down from 338 m³/s yesterday (two independent sources differ by 5 m³/s, within normal data noise; both show the same downward direction). Today is the thirteenth straight day the lake has been draining at below 400 cubic metres per second; it has not topped that threshold since July 12. The peak freshet outflow of about 2,741 cubic metres per second — reached in early May — is now 85 days in the past, and the lake is releasing barely twelve percent of that volume today. The confirmed minimum of this sub-400 streak was 328 m³/s on July 14.

The fourteen tracked storage reservoirs across the basin are all sitting within two centimetres of yesterday's levels. None is filling or emptying at any meaningful rate — this is normal late-summer baseflow posture, with inflows and outflows very nearly in balance. The mid-valley reach showed minor upticks: the town gauge at Mattawa rose two centimetres to 152.41 m, and the Pembroke gauge rose three centimetres to 111.12 m. Both moves are within the normal day-to-day variation, but the Pembroke uptick is worth watching — it is a small reversal of the gradual multi-week decline that had been underway. Preliminary July 24 readings for both stations have also been revised upward in today's data (Mattawa +7 cm, Pembroke +5 cm from yesterday's reported values), so the decline at both gauges was more gradual than yesterday's brief suggested.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge — located several kilometres upstream of the Bryson Generating Station, connected by a winding and narrowing channel, and measuring the lake directly rather than the dam pond — rose seven centimetres compared to the same reading twenty-four hours earlier, reaching 106.04 metres at 4 PM local time. The ORRPB midnight-to-midnight average for July 25 confirms the same upward move (+4 cm to 106.01 m). This is a trend reversal: the lake had been falling gently for about three weeks, and today it rose. At 106.04 m, the lake remains well within normal summer conditions — it would need to climb more than 96 centimetres to reach the lowest flood-advisory threshold of 107.00 m. No flood concern.

The major story today is at the Bryson Generating Station, which sits a good distance downstream of where Lac Coulonge's south end narrows and winds its way to the dam. Between yesterday afternoon and last night, operators cut the flow over the spillway by more than half — from about 90 cubic metres per second down to about 41 — while keeping the turbines running at roughly the same level. The practical effect: the dam pond has been rising sharply. It gained 20 centimetres in the past 24 hours and reached 105.57 metres this afternoon, a new all-time high — one centimetre above the previous record of 105.56 m set on June 21. The dam pond now sits 90 centimetres above the standard operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and has been above that ceiling for 58 consecutive days.

Cutting spillway flow while the dam pond is already well above its normal operating range is the opposite of what one would expect if the goal were to restore the pond to within its operating band. The ORRPB forecast text is unchanged from July 21 and calls for stable, seasonal conditions through the coming week. No flood watch; no upstream precipitation flag.
