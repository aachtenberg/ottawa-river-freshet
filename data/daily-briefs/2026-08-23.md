## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The rain event that Ottawa River regulators forecast on August 18 has now fully materialized. Lake Temiscaming — the large reservoir that serves as the upper Ottawa basin's main storage buffer — released 403 cubic metres per second on August 22, crossing above 400 for the first time in 41 days. The 40-day run of below-400 output (a summer low-flow period stretching back to July 13) has ended — a milestone, though 403 is barely over the threshold, and the basin remains well within normal summer operating conditions. The August 23 Temiscaming output has not yet been published by the regulators.

The rain event continues to push water through the mid-valley. Mattawa, about 80 kilometres upstream of the property reach, has reached 152.57 metres (Ottawa River regulators' station, early afternoon). The Des Joachims and Otto Holden generating stations again showed inflated numbers on today's live gauge — 939 and 786 cubic metres per second respectively — that are demonstrably inconsistent with the pond levels (the Des Joachims pond fell only 4 centimetres in 15 hours, while genuine 939 m³/s discharge would drain it by over 50 centimetres). The dam pond-level evidence and the downstream Chats Falls reading (533 m³/s — far less than the claimed 939) rule out both the live gauge figures. Provisional numbers for these two stations should not be taken at face value until finalized — a pattern now in its fifth consecutive day.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge rose 4 centimetres overnight, reaching 105.92 metres at midnight and 105.95 metres by mid-afternoon (live gauges agree). This is a gentle local response to the rain event — the lake is still fully normal, about 108 centimetres below the pre-alert level and 158 centimetres below the minor-flood threshold. No concern.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — well downstream from the south end of Lac Coulonge, not visible from or adjacent to the Mansfield properties — rose 12 centimetres overnight to 105.52 metres, even though the station actually released slightly less water than yesterday (362 vs 382 cubic metres per second, still all through the spillway, turbines still offline for a fourth straight day). A rising pond with lower spill means the rain event is pushing more inflow into the pond from above — Lac Coulonge and the river corridor feeding it — than was there before. The pond remains far above its normal operating ceiling, now 85 centimetres above the top of the allowed range. The ORRPB's next forecast update on August 25 will say whether additional rainfall is expected.
