## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Today marks Day 35 of the recession since the May 2 freshet peak, and the upper basin reached a pair of significant milestones in a single day. The flow out of Lake Témiscaming dropped to 749 cubic metres per second — crossing below both 900 and 800 m³/s for the first time since before the spring flood. That is down from 851 m³/s yesterday and 945 m³/s the day before. To put it in perspective: at the height of the freshet on May 2, the same lake was releasing roughly 2,741 m³/s. The flow is now just 27% of that peak and continuing to ease. At the current rate of decline — roughly 100 m³/s per day — a sub-700 crossing could come within days, which would approach normal early-summer base rates.

The mid-valley gauges track the same story. The river at Mattawa — midway between Lake Témiscaming and the property — fell 11 centimetres to 152.62 metres, its fifth straight day of decline and now 49 centimetres below the brief surge peak seen on May 31. The river at Pembroke, another 150 kilometres downstream on the Ontario side, fell 7 centimetres. The whole valley is draining steadily in both directions. Among 14 tracked reservoirs and lakes across the basin, 12 are essentially unchanged, none are rising at any meaningful rate, and only two are gently falling. Des Joachims — the Ontario Power Generation reservoir midway down the main stem — fell 7 centimetres today after rising 7 centimetres yesterday, swinging back as OPG resumed normal outflow. No sign of accumulation anywhere; the basin is passing water through.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The property continues in normal conditions, sitting about 70 centimetres below the first alerting threshold. As of 4 PM today (8 PM UTC), the live gauge reads 106.30 metres — very slightly lower than the midnight reading of 106.32 m recorded on the ORRPB tracking board, which is a typical intraday pattern. Net movement over 24 hours is essentially flat at around −2 centimetres. The lake has been in this 106.30–106.32 m range for the last several days, held there by a combination of the cascade settling after last week's operator adjustments. The ORRPB forecast remains on its post-freshet weekly update schedule (last change June 2, next update June 9): stable or gradually decreasing, no flood concerns, no precipitation flags.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — behind Bryson Generating Station — ticked up 7 centimetres today to 105.10 metres, entering Day 8 above its normal operating range. This is a reversal from yesterday's modest 3-centimetre improvement. The dam is releasing essentially the same amount of water as yesterday, but inflows appear to be slightly exceeding that release. Bryson remains well above its normal 47-centimetre operating window (104.20–104.67 m); at 43 centimetres above the top of that range, resolution is not imminent. Operators appear to be managing the situation without drama — total releases are stable and the headpond elevation, while elevated, is not in new territory for this event.

The most operationally notable development today is at the basin's terminal dam (Carillon, near Hawkesbury, Ontario): the spillway gates have been fully closed. Yesterday, 9% of Carillon's flow was going through the spillways; today it is zero. All 2,341 cubic metres per second is now passing through the turbines. Total release also fell 12% from yesterday. This is a clear operational signal that downstream water levels no longer require active spillway management — the flood season is effectively over at Carillon.
