This also means the "Day 5 of sustained fall" framing from yesterday's brief was wrong; it is retracted.

The outflow from Lake Timiscaming itself also continues to revise. The July 11 confirmed reading has been updated a second time — from 403 (in yesterday's brief) to 422 cubic metres per second. July 12 is confirmed at 428 m³/s, and today's preliminary is 448 m³/s. Despite sounding like an uptick, these numbers are all firmly in the 420–450 range that is characteristic of mid-July summer baseflow on the upper Ottawa. The sub-400 milestone has not been crossed.

The most operationally significant development today is a sharp mid-cascade step-down at two Hydro-Québec generating stations — Rapide-2 and Rapide-7 — which sit on the Ottawa River between the upper valley lakes and the Bryson station. Both cut their releases by roughly a third: Rapide-2 dropped from 237 to 159 cubic metres per second (−33%), and Rapide-7 dropped from 227 to 157 m³/s (−31%). Rapide-7 had been releasing about two-thirds of its water through the spillway gates yesterday; today it is operating entirely on turbines with zero spillway flow. Since both dams are now releasing substantially less water than is arriving from above, their storage pools are backing up — this is a coordinated mid-reach retention manoeuvre. The effect will propagate downstream slowly and may show up at Bryson later tomorrow. Of the fourteen tracked basin reservoirs, the overnight comparison (midnight Jul 12 to midnight Jul 13) shows three with modest 3-centimetre falls (Lake Timiscaming, the Rapide-7 forebay, and Dozois), eleven essentially flat, and none rising — a holding pattern consistent with summer management.
