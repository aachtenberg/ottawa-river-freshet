## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The post-event recession deepened sharply today at every point from Lake Timiscaming to the mid-valley, and the pace of improvement is now notably faster than the previous days suggested. The flow out of Lake Timiscaming into the Ottawa main stem — the single number that most governs what arrives at the dam chain and ultimately at Lac Coulonge — fell 77 cubic metres per second today to 705, its fifth consecutive day of decline since the event peak of 929 on July 4. This brings Témiscaming to its lowest outflow since before the Canada Day weekend precipitation event, and barely above the 700 m³/s round-number marker the system crossed on the way up. The federal operator (PSPC) and the ORRPB's flow table agree exactly on 705 m³/s, so the reading is solid.

The most dramatic number today is at Mattawa, roughly two-thirds of the way between Lake Timiscaming and the dam chain: the midnight level there fell 35 centimetres overnight to 152.41 metres, a single-day drop that undoes nearly three weeks of the Canada Day episode's accumulation. This follows yesterday's already-notable 12-centimetre decline after a three-day plateau; the system is clearly clearing rapidly at Mattawa. Pembroke, further downstream, joined the recession today with a 3-centimetre fall to 111.72 metres — its first meaningful decline after four days of rising. The precipitation-event wave has now clearly crested and is passing at every monitored point from Timiscaming to Pembroke.

Operators in the Hydro-Québec dam chain made further adjustments today consistent with managing a post-event recession. The Rapide-2 generating station — one of two stations that feed directly into the Bryson dam pond — was cut another 30% to 168 cubic metres per second, adding to the coordinated upper-cascade reductions from yesterday. This continues a pattern of cascade flow management: yesterday's big cuts at Première-Chute, Quinze, and Îles (each 26–33% reduced) established a new lower baseline, and today's Rapide-2 cut further reduces the inflow that will reach Bryson over the next hours. A brief spill episode at the Paugan dam on the Gatineau River — the dam produced its first spillway discharge in weeks yesterday — ended today as the spillway was closed. Carillon, the basin's terminal dam at the Quebec border, fell below 2,000 cubic metres per second for the first time in days (1,993 m³/s). Storage reservoirs are in stable pass-through mode: 3 of 14 tracked reservoirs are gently falling (Lake Timiscaming and Poisson Blanc), 11 are essentially flat, and none are rising, consistent with a system draining rather than retaining.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

After a week of gradual climbing, Lac Coulonge has finally turned. The Vigilance gauge at the Mansfield waterfront recorded 106.50 metres at 4 PM Eastern today — 8 centimetres below the same reading yesterday afternoon. The ORRPB's midnight-level series confirms the reversal: July 7's midnight reading of 106.55 metres is 3 centimetres below July 6's, breaking a rise streak that had run uninterrupted from June 30. The lake remains fully in normal range — 50 centimetres below the 107-metre level at which an advisory would apply, and a full metre below the 107.50-metre minor-flood threshold — but the upward momentum from the Canada Day weekend precipitation is now definitively gone.

The timing of today's ORRPB forecast update gives added confidence to this reading. For the first time since July 2 — when the board wrote that levels were "expected to increase gradually" — ORRPB today issued a fresh bulletin acknowledging exactly what the data shows: "water levels and flows have started to decrease and are expected to continue decreasing gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." The board simultaneously shifted from its daily freshet-season update schedule to a once-per-week cadence, with the next bulletin not expected until July 14. This transition to weekly updates is the standard ORRPB signal that the freshet season is formally over; the basin is back in routine summer management mode. No location-specific numeric forecasts were issued for Lac Coulonge, Britannia, or Carillon.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — reached by following the south end of Lac Coulonge's narrows several kilometres downstream through the narrows to the dam, well below the Mansfield properties — fell a further 4 centimetres to 105.29 metres this afternoon. This is Day 3 of consecutive decline and the lowest level since late June 30. The dam pond sits 62 centimetres above its regulatory operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, now in its 40th day of exceedance since May 29. The Bryson Generating Station continues to run its turbines (Day 9 of turbine operation since resuming June 29) while passing just over half its total outflow through the spillway rather than through generation. The ongoing cascade reduction in inflows should support further headpond decline in the days ahead, though returning to the regulatory operating band at the current pace would still take weeks barring a major operational shift.
