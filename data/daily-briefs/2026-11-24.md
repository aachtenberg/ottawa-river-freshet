## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The outflow from Lake Timiskaming averaged 358 cubic metres per second on November 24, the ninth day in a row below 400 and 14 lower than a week earlier. It is 206 days since the May 2 peak of 2,741.

The Mattawa gauge read 15 centimetres higher than the day before, and Pembroke 5 centimetres lower. The daily Mattawa figures have been swinging by this much for weeks without the river downstream following, so the two together read as a flat reach. Thirteen of the fourteen storage reservoirs were within two centimetres of the day before; Kipawa fell four centimetres.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake gauge at Fort-Coulonge read 105.969 metres at 9 PM UTC on November 24, about one centimetre lower than a day earlier. It is 103 centimetres below the first alert level and 153 centimetres below the minor-flood level. The river board issued its weekly outlook that afternoon: where the previous week's said levels would hold or slightly decrease, this one says hold or slightly increase. It carries no flood language.

Well downstream, the Bryson Generating Station kept its turbines steady and trimmed the spillway in steps over the day, from about 62 cubic metres per second to 38 and then 23. The total passing the dam eased from about 358 to 321. The dam pond slipped four centimetres and is still about 87 centimetres above its normal operating ceiling. At Carillon, the last dam in the basin, output sat near 529 cubic metres per second overnight and stepped up to about 915 by mid-morning Eastern time, well below the previous day's peak of roughly 1,440.
