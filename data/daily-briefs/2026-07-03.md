## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The precipitation event that the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board flagged in its early July 2 update is no longer a forecast — it is now showing fully in the measurements. The outlet of Lake Timiscaming, where the upper part of the basin drains southward into the main river, registered 862 cubic metres per second on July 3. That is up from a confirmed 629 cubic metres per second on July 2 (itself a sharp upward revision from the preliminary reading of 517 used in yesterday's brief), and is the highest Timiscaming outflow seen since late May or early June. It decisively broke the 10-day period of sub-700 flows that had run from June 23 through July 2, and it arrived in the system on the same day that the river at Mattawa — a town about halfway down the valley above the dam chain — climbed to its highest recorded level in this monitoring series at 152.92 metres. That is the fourth consecutive day Mattawa has risen, and it is now 76 centimetres above its local low reached just four days ago on June 29.

Pembroke, another mid-valley station further downstream, rose 16 centimetres to 111.60 metres — its highest point in two weeks. The pattern across the mid-valley is consistent: a significant precipitation pulse has loaded into the upper basin and is now moving through the plumbing. The reservoirs tell a different story than yesterday, however. Where yesterday three storage reservoirs were rising (absorbing inflow), today only one is falling and thirteen are sitting essentially flat. Operators are passing the surge through rather than absorbing it into storage. Notably, the Quinze dam — which feeds flow from the northern tributary into Lake Timiscaming — released 401 cubic metres per second, while the lake's outflow was 862 cubic metres per second, meaning about 461 cubic metres per second of additional uncontrolled runoff from direct tributaries and shoreline streams was entering the lake over and above what the Quinze contributed. The basin is loaded.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board has not published a new forecast update today. The July 2 update — which reversed the earlier "decreasing" outlook to say "water levels and flows are expected to increase gradually over the next few days" — remains the current guidance, with the next scheduled update on July 7. Everything measured today is consistent with that forecast.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The government telemetry gauge at the Mansfield waterfront on Lac Coulonge — a separate measurement point on the lake itself, not at the dam downstream — read 106.397 metres at 4 PM Eastern time today. That is about 9 centimetres above the same hour yesterday (106.306 m). The midnight-to-midnight series from the Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board confirms the same direction: from 106.30 metres (July 2) to 106.35 metres (July 3), a rise of 5 centimetres — the third consecutive day of such rises, adding up to 22 centimetres of rise since June 30. At 106.40 metres, the lake remains firmly in fully normal condition: it is 60 centimetres below the pre-alert advisory level of 107.00 metres, and about 110 centimetres below the minor-flood threshold. The current rate of rise (roughly 5–10 centimetres per day at midnight) means the lake would need 6–12 more days at this pace before reaching even the pre-alert advisory. Provided the ORRPB-forecast "gradual" increases stay gradual and do not sharpen, there is substantial buffer before any advisory would be relevant.

What is changing, and is worth watching, is the situation at the Bryson Generating Station — a dam on the south channel where Lac Coulonge eventually narrows and winds some distance below the Mansfield-area properties. The dam pond above that station (a distinct location from the lake gauge, not visible from the Mansfield properties, and well downstream of them) reversed its recent gradual drawdown today. Despite being in the fifth day of turbine operation, the dam pond level rose 14 centimetres to 105.41 metres — a sign that the upstream inflow arriving at the dam is now exceeding what the turbines and spillway together can discharge. The dam pond remains 74 centimetres above its permitted upper ceiling of 104.67 metres, now in Day 36 of that exceedance. The drawdown that had been slowly chipping away at the overfill has stalled and reversed. This is consistent with the Témiscaming surge moving through the system; it will continue to arrive at the Bryson vicinity over the coming days. Property owners on Lac Coulonge should expect the lake to continue rising through the weekend, in line with the ORRPB's forecast of "gradual increases over the next few days."
