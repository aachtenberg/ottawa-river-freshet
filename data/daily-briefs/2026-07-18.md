## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The most notable development today is at the very top of the basin: water flowing out of Lake Timiscaming has dropped to a preliminary reading of 295 cubic metres per second — the first time below 300 cubic metres per second since well before the spring freshet. For context, at the freshet's peak on May 2nd this same gauge was pushing roughly 2,700 cubic metres per second. Today's reading is about 11 percent of that. If it holds after tomorrow's confirmation (a large caveat — yesterday's preliminary of 365 was revised down 29 cubic metres per second to 336), this is the lowest sustained outflow the upper basin has seen since early spring. We are now 78 days past that peak.

Mattawa, the mid-valley gauge above Pembroke, shows 152.31 metres as of yesterday's midnight — the July 18 overnight reading is not yet in the database, so today's direction at Mattawa is unknown. Pembroke ticked up 1 centimetre to 111.14 metres. Neither site is near any flood threshold, and the overall basin storage picture is very quiet: all 12 reservoirs with level readings today are essentially flat, consistent with normal late-summer conditions.

One ongoing story worth watching: the dams at Rapide-2 and Rapide-7, roughly halfway between Bryson and the Carillon terminal dam, are now in their sixth consecutive day of a controlled hold, releasing only about 159 and 157 cubic metres per second respectively even though the upper-cascade dams above them are passing around 200 cubic metres per second. This means they are absorbing roughly 40 to 50 cubic metres per second more than they release — a deliberate impoundment posture that has now lasted nearly a week.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The Vigilance gauge at Fort-Coulonge read 106.09 metres at 4 PM today, up barely 2 centimetres from yesterday afternoon. That is etat 0 — fully normal summer range — sitting 91 centimetres below the 107-metre level at which any informal advisory would begin. The lake is behaving exactly as the ORRPB forecast describes: essentially stable, with a very gentle declining trend over the past two weeks.

Several kilometres downstream, the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station (reached by following the south end of Lac Coulonge as it narrows and winds down past the lake's outlet) was essentially unchanged today at 105.49 metres — up just 2 centimetres from yesterday. The dramatic spillway cuts that drove headlines in the last two weeks have settled: release volumes, turbine output, and spillway flow are all within one to two percent of yesterday's readings. The dam pond has been above its standard operating ceiling of 104.67 metres for 51 consecutive days and is now 82 centimetres above that ceiling, sitting 7 centimetres below the June 21st all-time recorded high of 105.56 metres. The situation is sustained but stable today.

At the basin's terminal dam at Carillon, total flow dropped about 10 percent to 985 cubic metres per second — the first reading below 1,000 cubic metres per second at that dam this season. This is a downstream signal of the overall basin recession, not a sudden operational change. The ORRPB forecast has not changed since July 14. It expects flows and levels to decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week. Nothing at the property today contradicts that forecast.
