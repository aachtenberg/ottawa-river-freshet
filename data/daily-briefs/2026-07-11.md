## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa basin has taken a decisive step deeper into summer. The outflow from Lake Timiscaming — the large Ontario–Quebec border lake that drains into the upper Ottawa — fell from 492 cubic metres per second yesterday to 387 today, crossing below 400 for the first time since the spring freshet season. This is now Day 71 since the primary freshet peak in early May, and the basin's summer recession is visibly accelerating rather than plateauing. The federal and provincial gauges agree within one cubic metre per second, confirming the reading is solid.

The sharpest signal came at Mattawa, roughly two-thirds of the way between Lake Timiscaming and the dam chain: water levels fell 23 centimetres overnight — the largest single-day drop in the recent record, roughly three times yesterday's 8-centimetre decline. This is the upper-valley recession wave working its way downstream, a normal and expected consequence of the lake outflow pulling back faster. Pembroke, another 130 kilometres downstream, continued its quieter fall of 2 centimetres, and the mid-valley remains entirely within normal summer range.

The basin's storage reservoirs are settling into a gentle drawing-down. Two of the fourteen tracked reservoirs fell by more than 2 centimetres overnight — Dozois and Lake Timiscaming itself — while the other twelve are essentially flat. This is a modest shift from yesterday's near-perfect balance, reflecting the accelerating outflow from the upper basin. No reservoir is actively rising, which means operators are not building storage against the recession; inflows everywhere are modest and management is following the natural drawdown. The basin is approaching the quietest summer configuration seen since before the spring freshet.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the Mansfield waterfront is in its second consecutive flat day. The Vigilance gauge read 106.231 metres at 4 PM Eastern on July 11 — about 0.7 centimetres *below* the same reading 24 hours earlier, which is essentially no change. The ORRPB midnight series confirms this: the July 11 midnight reading (106.24 m) is one centimetre *above* the July 10 midnight reading (106.23 m), also effectively flat. The lake is roughly 77 centimetres below the 107.00-metre level at which any advisory would begin, and over a metre and a quarter below the 107.50-metre minor-flood threshold. This is fully normal for mid-July.

Several kilometres below the property reach, the Bryson dam's water management showed an unusual pattern today. Operators cut the amount of water flowing through the spillway by about 31 cubic metres per second (a 15% reduction in spillway flow), while keeping turbine output essentially unchanged. This means the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — located well downstream of Lac Coulonge, reached by following the lake's south-end narrows several kilometres down to the dam — rose by 11 centimetres today, bringing it to 80 centimetres above the upper limit of its normal operating band. That is the highest the dam pond has been since early July, continuing a rise that has now lasted 44 consecutive days. The reason for cutting spillway flow despite an already-elevated dam pond is not apparent from the data alone; it may reflect turbine capacity planning, grid dispatch, or upstream coordination — but the trajectory bears watching.

The basin's terminal dam at Carillon stepped down sharply today, cutting its total release by about 268 cubic metres per second (nearly 15%), continuing a stepwise reduction over the past several days as the lower-valley water levels recede. The ORRPB forecast remains unchanged since July 7 (on a weekly update schedule, next update July 14) and describes conditions as near-seasonal values gradually declining — which matches today's picture precisely.
