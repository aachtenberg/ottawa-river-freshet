The pattern of systematic downward revisions to preliminary Témiscaming data continues: for the fifth time in a row, the prior-day estimate was too high (by 68 m³/s this time, the second-largest revision on record). Today's preliminary reading of 456 m³/s should be treated with the same skepticism — the true daily-average figure will likely be lower when confirmed tomorrow.

A notable shift today: Rapide-2 stepped its releases back up by nearly 14%, partially reversing the sharp 35% cut made two days ago. This is consistent with the operator beginning to normalize mid-reach flows after that brief storage-hold manoeuvre. The gap between the Des Joachims station (releasing about 430 cubic metres per second) and Rapide-2 (now 238 m³/s) has narrowed but remains significant, suggesting this re-normalization is still in progress. The Paugan generating station on the Gatineau River, which joins the main stem just upstream of Carillon, cut its releases by roughly 6%, reflecting easing flows in the Gatineau watershed.

One notable anomaly: the Ottawa River at Mattawa — a town about 200 kilometres downstream of Lake Témiscaming — rose 22 centimetres overnight, breaking a five-day streak of consecutive declines. The cause is uncertain. The ORRPB forecast had mentioned rainfall over the past week, and local tributaries between Témiscaming and Mattawa could be adding runoff. It is also possible that today's Mattawa midnight reading (which, like previous readings for this station, is preliminary) will be revised substantially when tomorrow's brief checks it — upward corrections of 12–25 cm on prior-day Mattawa data have appeared on three of the last four days. Pembroke, further downstream, continued its steady decline of about 3 centimetres. The system downstream of Mattawa (Pembroke, Bryson, Carillon) shows no sign of any upstream pulse arriving.

- **Sub-500 context:** At 456 m³/s, Témiscaming is now running below the typical late-June base flow range referenced in the ORRPB's seasonal comparisons. The secondary surge (peak June 20 = 785 m³/s) has been fully erased; the system is now winding down below even the pre-surge baseline. At typical late-summer base flows for the upper Ottawa (roughly 200–350 m³/s), the system would need to shed another 100–250 m³/s before reaching that threshold — a matter of days to weeks at current rate of decline, assuming no new precipitation events.

- **Mattawa anomaly:** The +22 cm rise at Mattawa is the first day-over-day rise since June 21 (secondary surge driven). With Témiscaming flowing 456 m³/s and the HQ cascade above Bryson releasing ~305 m³/s, there is no obvious upstream driver large enough to explain a 22 cm rise at Mattawa purely from main-stem transmission. Local tributary contributions (from smaller rivers between Témiscaming and Mattawa) following recent precipitation are the most plausible physical explanation. However, the prior pattern of large upward revisions to Mattawa overnight data (June 24, June 25, June 26 all revised upward) means the June 27 reading should not be considered confirmed. Pembroke's continued decline (−3 cm) argues against a large pulse reaching the lower mid-valley.
