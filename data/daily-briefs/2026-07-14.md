## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin crossed a significant milestone today — or rather, yesterday, as it turns out. The daily outflow from Lake Timiscaming fell below 400 cubic metres per second for the first time since before the spring flood peak on May 2nd. Yesterday's brief reported a preliminary reading of 448 that appeared to hold the basin above that threshold, but the figure has been confirmed overnight at just 370 cubic metres per second — a 78-unit downward revision and the biggest single correction in this dataset this season. Today's preliminary reading is 349, putting us at two consecutive days below 400.

The revisions continue to be a theme. The July 11 Témiscaming figure has been revised a fourth time — from the 422 reported in yesterday's brief back down to 403 — while July 12 nudged slightly upward from 428 to 433. The overall picture that emerges from the confirmed data is not the gentle plateau the preliminary figures suggested, but a fairly steady decline into the low-400s and now the high-300s. Even accounting for revision uncertainty, sub-400 appears to be real and established: the Timiscaming lake level is continuing to draw down (179.14 m at midnight, essentially flat day-over-day while outflow exceeds the roughly 200 cubic metres per second coming in from the Quinze generating station upstream).

At the gauge in Mattawa, which translates upper-valley outflow into a mid-river level, the overnight reading fell 12 centimetres to a preliminary 152.32 metres. After two weeks of oscillation in a narrow band between 152.39 and 152.54 metres, today's reading is the first to break below that floor. As always, this preliminary figure may revise significantly — the July 13 reading was initially 152.51 and confirmed at 152.44, a 7-centimetre downward revision — but if it holds, it would mark the first genuine sustained decline at Mattawa in the post–Canada Day period.

Of the 14 tracked storage reservoirs compared overnight, one showed a modest 3-centimetre draw-down (Dozois), 12 were essentially flat within measurement noise, and one (Des Joachims, the OPG facility directly upstream of Carillon) rose 3 centimetres while its midnight outflow showed zero — suggesting a brief hold at that station. The mid-cascade retention manoeuvre at Rapide-2 and Rapide-7 that began yesterday is holding; both stations continue releasing about 157–159 cubic metres per second, well below the 227–237 they were putting through 48 hours ago. The Rapide-7 forebay is essentially flat rather than rising, which means the cut in releases roughly matches the reduced inflow arriving from above — the system is passing through less, not accumulating.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The Vigilance gauge at the Mansfield waterfront read 106.02 metres at 4 PM today, about 6 centimetres lower than the same reading yesterday and just under a metre below the level at which any informal advisory would begin. The lake is thoroughly normal for mid-July. The ORRPB midnight series, which uses a different measurement agency and reports at midnight, confirms the same trend: 106.08 metres last night (revised slightly down from yesterday's preliminary of 106.11), with today's midnight preliminary at 106.03 metres.

Several kilometres downstream of the property — reached by following the south end of Lac Coulonge as it narrows and winds several kilometres down to the dam — the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station climbed 5 centimetres today, to 105.39 metres, reversing the two-day dip that followed the undisclosed evening excursion noted in yesterday's brief. The pond is now 72 centimetres above the top of its standard operating range and in its 47th consecutive day of operating above that ceiling. The operating pattern today was notably different from morning to afternoon: from around midnight until noon, the station was running mostly through its turbines with a modest share going through the spillway gates. After noon, the station shifted sharply — by 3 PM it was running roughly 56 percent of its release through the spillway and only 44 percent through the turbines. This afternoon high-spill posture has now appeared on multiple consecutive days.

The ORRPB updated its forecast this afternoon — the first revision since July 7. The language is nearly unchanged: water levels are "within seasonal values" and "expected to decrease gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week." The previous text's reference to the rain-driven rise from the July Canada-Day event has been quietly removed, signalling that operators consider that episode closed. The next update is scheduled for July 21.
