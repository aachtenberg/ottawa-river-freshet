### Upstream — the upper basin

Yesterday's preliminary reading at the Témiscaming gauge — the outlet of the northern basin — came in at 779 cubic metres per second and was flagged as a dramatic 22% reversal of the spring decline. Overnight revision tells a very different story: the actual June 11 flow was 639 m³/s, within 1 m³/s of the two days before it, and confirmed by two independent data sources. The upper basin was never reversing; it has been declining steadily for 42 days since the spring freshet peak and is now in its fourth consecutive day below 700 m³/s. The correction also means the sub-700 streak never broke — it has now run for four straight days.

The precipitation event from earlier in the week was real, however. The reservoirs confirmed it yesterday (five rose overnight), and the Carillon surge to 2,372 m³/s was genuine. Today that pulse is showing up in the mid-valley: the Mattawa gauge — roughly halfway along the Ottawa between the upper basin and Lac Coulonge — jumped 10 centimetres overnight after sitting flat for three consecutive days. This is a textbook downstream propagation signal. At the same time, the Des Joachims forebay (a large impoundment below Mattawa) saw its outflow cut sharply from 778 to 590 m³/s, as operators allowed the forebay to recover after routing the pulse through it. The Pembroke gauge, further downstream, continued its gradual 5-centimetre decline — the mid-valley pulse hasn't reached that far yet.

Reservoir refilling has continued but at a more modest pace than yesterday. Three of the fourteen tracked waterbodies are rising (Des Joachims +5 cm, Poisson Blanc +5 cm, and both Lake Timiskaming gauges +2–4 cm), while the rest hold steady and none are falling. No individual reservoir is rising fast enough to trigger the active-retention flag (more than 10 centimetres per day), but the pattern remains consistent with normal post-precipitation refill.

The regional authority's forecast, last updated June 9 and not due for refresh until June 16, called for "fairly stable or decreasing" levels.
