## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Day 33 since the May 2 freshet peak, and Lake Témiscaming crossed a round-number milestone today: preliminary data from the PSPC dam operator puts the daily average outflow at 1,000 cubic metres per second — the lowest since before the spring runoff began in earnest and roughly a third of what it was at the height of the flood season. Both the operator's own records and the river board's independent table agree on the 1,000 figure. The lake level itself barely shifted overnight (down just 1 cm), meaning inflows are closely matching outflows at this reduced rate. Even at 1,000 m³/s the river is still running above its typical summer base rate of roughly 500–700 m³/s, so the recession has further to run — but the upper basin is clearly winding down. Note that today's figure is a preliminary running-day average and will be revised once the full day finalizes; the prior day (June 2) revised upward from live estimates to 1,206 m³/s final, so some upward revision for June 3 is possible.

The wave of release cutbacks that Hydro-Québec began yesterday at the upper reaches of the cascade has now propagated one step further downstream. The two dams closest to the property — Rapide-2 and Rapide-7, which sit roughly 20–30 kilometres upstream of the dam pond at Lac Coulonge — each trimmed their releases by about 30% overnight (roughly 130 m³/s apiece), following yesterday's larger reductions at Première-Chute, Quinze, and Îles further up the chain. The Gatineau River cascade (Paugan and Rapides-Farmers, which feed the Ottawa just upstream of the basin's terminal dam at Carillon) also pulled back by 12–15%. These are normal post-freshet steps as seasonal snowmelt finishes and operators bring generation rates back toward summer levels. Among the 14 storage reservoirs tracked across the basin, every single one held within 2 centimetres of yesterday's level — the quietest reservoir day in weeks.

The mid-valley level at Mattawa — roughly halfway between Lake Témiscaming and the property — fell 11 centimetres overnight, the third consecutive day of decline after the temporary pause caused by last weekend's Ontario Power Generation release pulse from the Des Joachims reservoir. That pulse has now fully passed through. The gauge at Pembroke, further downstream, remains flat.

The midnight water level dropped 3 centimetres to 106.33 m, and live afternoon readings show it continuing to ease off through the day (106.32 m at 3 PM local time). The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson Generating Station's headpond) is holding its position for a second straight day, still sitting 39 centimetres above the top of its normal operating band (which runs 104.20–104.67 m), and technically in its sixth consecutive day of operating above that upper limit. The operator reduced the dam's total release by about 51 cubic metres per second compared to yesterday (about 8%), which closely matches the reduction arriving from Rapide-7 upstream (which cut ~126 m³/s). The result is near-equilibrium: the headpond barely moved (+1 cm). As the cascade cuts propagate further downstream, Bryson's incoming supply should ease over the next day or two — which would let the operator either reduce releases while the headpond drains naturally, or hold spill open and accelerate the drain. Either way, the breach is not worsening.

At the basin's terminal dam at Carillon, operators made a notable internal shift today: the total water passing through barely changed, but they routed dramatically more of it through the generating turbines and much less over the spillway (the spillway share dropped from about 25% yesterday to about 13% today). This is a classic post-freshet transition — when flood management is no longer the priority, the dam switches to maximising electricity generation. The ORRPB forecast was last updated June 2 and is unchanged, still on its new weekly schedule (next update June 9): the river is within seasonal norms and expected to remain stable or ease gradually.
