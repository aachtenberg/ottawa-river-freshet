## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

Something meaningful happened in the upper Ottawa basin over the past couple of days: the flow past Témiscaming — the gauge at the outlet of the northernmost part of the watershed — jumped from 638 cubic metres per second yesterday to 779 today. That's a 22% increase in a single day, ending a brief two-day spell below the 700 m³/s mark and reversing a 40-day declining trend from the May 2 spring freshet peak. The reading is still preliminary and may be revised slightly, but it's directionally confirmed by multiple other signals: the reservoir that feeds it (the Quinze dam, above Lake Témiscaming) is rising despite releasing water; the Des Joachims forebay in the mid-valley is rising despite releasing nearly 1,000 cubic metres per second; and Poisson Blanc, on the Gatineau tributary, rose 8 centimetres overnight. All of this points to a recent rainfall event across both the northern Ottawa basin and the Gatineau watershed that is now working its way through the river system.

The Mattawa gauge — about halfway down the valley from the upper basin — is flat at 152.44 metres, essentially the same level as yesterday. The downstream Pembroke gauge continues its gradual decline (-2 cm). Neither represents a flood concern; they simply confirm that the mid-valley is in transition, with yesterday's declining inflow being replaced by today's rising pulse. This upstream surge would typically take three to four days to propagate downstream to the Lac Coulonge area. The Carillon dam at the base of the basin has already felt the effect from mid-basin flows arriving: it pushed water out at 2,372 cubic metres per second today, up sharply from 1,883 yesterday, reversing four consecutive days of cuts. Its spillway briefly opened during the afternoon before being closed again.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the property continues to ease: the 4 PM reading was 106.31 metres, about 4.7 centimetres below the same time yesterday. The lake is fully normal, sitting 69 centimetres below even the lowest early-warning level (107.00 m), and is declining at roughly 3–5 centimetres per day. No flood concern applies.

The dam pond at the foot of Lac Coulonge — the Bryson Generating Station headpond — fell another 6 centimetres today to 105.27 metres. This is Day 14 of the headpond sitting above its normal operating ceiling. The ceiling is 104.67 metres, so the pond is still 60 centimetres above where it should be. The recovery is continuing but slowing (yesterday it dropped 11 centimetres; today only 6). The key watch item: if the upstream rainfall pulse propagates to Lac Coulonge in the next few days, the lake could stop declining and begin rising again, which would slow or stall the headpond recovery. Whether that happens depends on how much rain fell and where it collected. Hydro-Québec's release posture at Bryson is essentially unchanged from yesterday — about 525 cubic metres per second total, 45% through the spillway.

The regional authority's forecast, last updated June 9 and not refreshed this week, called for "fairly stable or decreasing" levels through mid-week. That forecast predates today's upstream reversal and should be read with caution — the next ORRPB update is not expected until June 16.
