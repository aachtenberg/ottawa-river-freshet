## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The third pulse from the upper basin is still growing. The town of Mattawa — where the northern half of the drainage area drains into the Ottawa's main stem — rose another 9 centimetres overnight to 152.73 metres, its highest reading since the primary freshet peak in early June. This is now 12 centimetres above the secondary peak recorded on June 12 (152.61 m) and still climbing. A third consecutive crest appears to be forming.

The reason is traceable to Témiscaming, at the head of the Ottawa system, where the river has been running well above the level of the past two weeks. An important correction from yesterday's brief: the June 17 midnight reading — which yesterday's brief reported as 684 cubic metres per second, apparently sustaining a nine-day stretch below 700 — has been revised upward to 724 cubic metres per second. That revision confirms that June 16 (666 m³/s) was actually the last sub-700 day; the eight-day streak ran from June 9 through June 16 and has now closed. Today's midnight reading stands at 777 cubic metres per second, and a mid-afternoon check confirmed a similar level. The head-of-basin is trending upward.

Further downstream, the most dramatic event today is at Ontario Power Generation's Des Joachims dam between Pembroke and the main Hydro-Québec cascade, which was ramping its releases sharply through the day — passing 1,160 cubic metres per second by mid-afternoon, double its overnight rate. That pulse is already visible at two smaller HQ stations just above the Bryson dam (Rapide-2 and Rapide-7), where flows jumped by 23 to 33 per cent between yesterday and today. It also reached Carillon, the basin's terminal dam near Hawkesbury, which increased its releases by 17 per cent to 2,344 cubic metres per second. This is the mid-valley pulse arriving at the lower basin, consistent with the timing expected from Des Joachims.

Reservoir levels across the upper basin are nearly all steady or within a centimetre. The Des Joachims impoundment itself rose 7 centimetres as inflows briefly outpaced releases; no other reservoir is moving enough to trigger a retention flag.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake at the foot of Lac Coulonge is holding almost exactly steady today, with the 4 PM reading at Fort-Coulonge sitting at 106.25 metres — down barely 2 centimetres from the same time yesterday afternoon. There was an overnight rise of about 3 centimetres (reflected in the midnight-to-midnight comparison), which then flattened and gently reversed as the afternoon progressed. The directional reversal visible in yesterday's brief (the lake was rising sharply) appears to have stalled; levels are now oscillating rather than trending clearly in either direction. At 75 centimetres below the lowest alert threshold, there is no near-term flood concern.

The Bryson dam — which controls the outlet of Lac Coulonge — remains in its turbine outage, now entering a fourth day. Yesterday the turbines were passing a token trickle of about 35 cubic metres per second; today that dropped to zero. The entire flow, roughly 473 cubic metres per second, is going through the open spillway. The dam's headpond ticked up 1 centimetre to 105.54 metres — barely extending the breach record set yesterday — and now sits 87 centimetres above the permitted upper limit of 104.67 metres, marking Day 21 of the headpond being outside its allowed operating range with no public timeline for recovery.

One positive development: a separate elevated-spillway episode at Première-Chute, the cascade station at the head of the HQ chain, has ended after three days. That station had been routing about a third of its flow through open spillway gates; it has now returned to near-normal turbine operation. This may reflect HQ grid management adjustments.

The ORRPB forecast, last updated in the early hours of June 17 and not yet revised, continues to warn that rapid increases are possible if heavy rainfall occurs. Given the mid-basin pulse now routing downstream and the turbine situation at Bryson, the lake's position warrants continued monitoring through the week.

## Notes

- **Des Joachims pulse routing**: The cascade sequence is clear. Des Joachims (OPG) midnight-to-intraday ramp (853→1163 m³/s, +36%) is flowing into Rapide-2 and Rapide-7 (the main-stem HQ stations between Des Joachims and Bryson), then passing Bryson's headpond with limited impact (headpond +1 cm), and arriving at Carillon.
