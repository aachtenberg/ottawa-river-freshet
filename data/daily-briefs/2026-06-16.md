## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The upper Ottawa basin is on Day 46 of its post-peak recession, but today delivered a new wrinkle: the river level at Mattawa — the town just below Lake Timiscaming, where the northern half of the basin funnels into the main stem — jumped 14 centimetres overnight to 152.66 metres. That is the highest reading at Mattawa since June 7, and it exceeds the secondary pulse peak of around 152.51–152.53 metres that was recorded last week. A third shallow rise is now working through the upper-basin reach. It is consistent with the outflow from Lake Timiscaming ticking back upward toward 700 cubic metres per second after spending seven days below that mark: the federal PSPC midnight cross-check puts today at 696 m³/s (technically still under 700), while the ORRPB's published figure for the day is 704 m³/s. Either way, the direction has turned upward.

Yesterday's brief noted that the June 9–10 precipitation pulse was "largely passed" at Mattawa — that assessment was based on a preliminary reading that has since been revised upward. With today's fresh 14 cm jump confirmed, the Mattawa recession has stalled and reversed; the third rise is real. Midway down the valley, the Des Joachims reservoir (a major Ontario Power Generation impoundment between Mattawa and Pembroke) continued its active pulse-routing posture, passing 925 cubic metres per second while its own forebay rose 8 centimetres. Farther downstream at Pembroke, the level fell 5 centimetres rather than rising — the Mattawa pulse has not yet arrived there. Across the rest of the basin's reservoir inventory, conditions are very quiet: only Des Joachims moved more than 2 centimetres. The basin is in post-flood equilibrium save for the mid-valley routing operation.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge itself is calm and easing. At 4 PM local time, the lake surface was at 106.16 metres near Fort-Coulonge — about 6 centimetres lower than the same time yesterday — and fully 84 centimetres below the lowest alert threshold of 107 metres. There is no flood concern at the property. The midnight reading was 106.20 metres, virtually unchanged from June 15's midnight reading of 106.21 metres; the steeper same-hour drop reflects normal diurnal variation and the lake's continuing gentle downward drift.

The dam at the foot of the lake — Bryson Generating Station — is now into its second full day of near-complete turbine shutdown. As of yesterday morning, Hydro-Québec effectively converted the plant from power generation to a simple water bypass: the turbines are handling only about 35 cubic metres per second (versus the normal ~288), while roughly 459 cubic metres per second — about 93 per cent of the total — passes through the open spillway. The key update today is that the dam's headpond, which surged 13 centimetres yesterday when the outage began, eased back by 4 centimetres to 105.43 metres — the first measurable decline since the failure started. The system has apparently reached a rough equilibrium between inflow and the spillway's discharge capacity. Even so, the headpond remains 76 centimetres above the dam's normal operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and this is now Day 19 of the overall breach. Full recovery to within the normal band requires the turbines to restart.

At the far end of the river, Carillon — the basin's terminal dam — cut its daily outflow by 12.5 per cent (from roughly 2,290 to 2,006 cubic metres per second), reversing yesterday's equally large 9.5 per cent surge. These large daily swings at Carillon are unusual and likely reflect difficulty forecasting inflows as mid-valley pulse routing completes and Gatineau tributary flows moderate. The ORRPB had been expected to update its weekly forecast today at 4 PM local time; as of 6 PM that update had not appeared (the current forecast dates from June 9 and calls for "fairly stable or decreasing" conditions — framing that does not capture the Mattawa third rise, the Bryson turbine anomaly, or Carillon's daily volatility).
