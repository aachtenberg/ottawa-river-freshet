## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The figure that yesterday's brief flagged as a preliminary "first below 300 cubic metres per second since before the spring event" has now been officially confirmed: water flowing out of Lake Timiscaming at the top of the basin was 295 cubic metres per second on July 18. That is roughly 11 percent of the peak that came in early May (around 2,700 cubic metres per second). We are now 79 days past that peak. Today's July 19 value has not yet appeared in the database at the time this brief was written, so the streak sits at one confirmed day below 300 cubic metres per second, with more expected.

Mid-valley gauges remain quiet. Mattawa's most recent overnight reading (July 17) was 152.31 metres; the July 18 overnight figure still hasn't arrived in the database. Pembroke's July 18 overnight reading confirmed at 111.14 metres, up one centimetre from the night before. Neither site is anywhere near a flood concern. All 12 reservoirs with comparable level readings remain essentially flat — a normal late-summer posture where inflows and releases are in balance.

The controlled hold at Rapide-2 and Rapide-7 — roughly midway between the upper-cascade dams and the Bryson dam pond — has now stretched to a seventh consecutive day. These dams are releasing only about 159 and 158 cubic metres per second respectively while the dams above them pass around 200 cubic metres per second, continuing to absorb roughly 40 to 50 cubic metres per second more than they release.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge read 106.09 metres at 4 PM today, up less than half a centimetre from the same hour yesterday. The lake is in its fully normal summer range — etat 0 — and sits 91 centimetres below the 107-metre level at which any informal advisory begins.

Several kilometres downstream — reached by following Lac Coulonge's south end as it narrows and winds down through the lake's outlet to the Bryson dam — the dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station was at 105.51 metres at 3 PM, up 2 centimetres from yesterday. That is 52 consecutive days above the dam's standard operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and now only 5 centimetres below the June 21st all-time recorded high of 105.56 metres. The more notable change at Bryson today was not the dam pond level but how water is being routed: Hydro-Québec operators increased the volume going through the spillway by about 20 percent — from around 81 to 97 cubic metres per second — while holding turbine output essentially flat. Total release was about 4 percent higher than yesterday. The dam pond rose only slightly (2 cm), suggesting the spillway increase was tracking a modest rise in inflow rather than drawing down the pond.

At Carillon — the basin's terminal dam at the bottom of the Ottawa system — today's reading reversed yesterday's notable milestone. After Carillon dropped below 1,000 cubic metres per second for the first time this season yesterday (985 m³/s), it bounced back to about 1,098 cubic metres per second today — an 11 percent increase. The source of that jump isn't directly visible from Hydro-Québec data; the mid-valley Ontario Power Generation dams between Bryson and Carillon likely dispatched more water overnight. No spill at Carillon; all turbined. The ORRPB forecast remains unchanged since July 14, calling for gradual decrease or stable conditions. Its next update is expected tomorrow (July 21).
