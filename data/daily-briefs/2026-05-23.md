## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The flow leaving Lake Temiscaming — the upper Ottawa's main pulse of snowmelt working its way south — fell to 1,453 cubic metres per second today, the eleventh straight day of decline and the second consecutive day below 1,500. For context: the freshet peaked around May 2 at roughly 2,741 cubic metres per second. Today's reading is 47% below that peak, and we're now 21 days past the high-water mark. The system is draining with impressive consistency: over the past seven days alone, the Temiscaming outflow has dropped 761 cubic metres per second (34%).

The mid-valley reach is following suit with equal determination. The gauge at Mattawa fell another 29 centimetres overnight — the fourth straight day of decline, bringing the total drop to 110 centimetres below its rain-driven high of May 19. Pembroke dropped 18 centimetres (Day 3 of decline). Both of these stations are responding to the relentlessly falling upper-basin snowmelt pulse; no bounce, no rain event in sight to interrupt the pattern. The flood management board's outlook from May 21 — "decline more rapidly starting next week" — appears to be right on schedule.

Most headwater reservoirs are either flat or inching up in small steps, which is exactly what you'd expect in post-freshet season: operators are now holding back a modest share of the diminishing inflow rather than routing it all downstream. Four tracked reservoirs rose meaningfully overnight (Des Joachims +7 cm, Baskatong +4 cm, Mitchinamecus +4 cm, Dozois +3 cm); two edged downward (Bark Lake −5 cm, Poisson Blanc −3 cm); eight were essentially flat. None is rising fast enough to trigger a concern — the active-retention watch threshold is 10 centimetres per day and the biggest mover is only 7 cm. The most notable development is at Des Joachims (a reservoir and generating station between Mattawa and Pembroke): it cut its outflow by 462 cubic metres per second overnight while accumulating 7 centimetres of level. This is OPG beginning to store water in earnest — a sign that Mattawa's rapid decline has opened enough headroom downstream to start refilling storage on this stretch of the river.
