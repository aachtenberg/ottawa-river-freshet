## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The clearest signal today comes from the head of the watershed, where outflow from Lake Temiscaming near the Quebec-Ontario border surged to 843 cubic metres per second — the highest daily reading since early May, and a jump of 53 cubic metres per second in a single day. This is now the fifth consecutive day that outflow at Temiscaming has exceeded 700 cubic metres per second, but the important detail is the pace: each of those five days has been higher than the last, with today's jump being the steepest single-day increase in the sequence. The large storage reservoirs across the basin remain essentially frozen in place (nearly all changed by 1–2 centimetres or less overnight), so this surge reflects genuine tributary inflow — precipitation from earlier in the week is now arriving at the head of the main stem — not coordinated operator releases.

The third-rise wave that crested at Mattawa on June 19 is still receding. The midnight level there on June 20 (the most recent reading available this evening) was 152.73 metres — 5 centimetres below the peak and 37 centimetres below the spring's primary flood crest in early June. The decline is now in its second day, which is encouraging. However, the fresh Temiscaming surge will take several days to propagate down the valley, so the current recession and the incoming upstream pulse could overlap before conditions at Mattawa stabilise. At Pembroke, where a mid-valley dam pulse has been routing through all week, the most recent midnight reading was 111.63 metres — still ticking upward as of the last available data.

The clearest downstream signature of the Temiscaming surge arrived at the basin's terminal dam at Carillon today: for the first time in weeks, the dam began spilling water through its spillway gates in addition to running its turbines, with about 291 cubic metres per second going over the gates this afternoon as inflows exceeded the level at which operators began diverting around the turbines. Upstream, the Chats Falls run-of-river facility (the last major step on the Ottawa before Carillon) saw its daily flow jump 26% to 1,527 cubic metres per second today. The Gatineau River at Paugan, the Gatineau's junction with the Ottawa just above Carillon, also stepped up 5% for the second consecutive day.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The lake gauge at the community level — Lac Coulonge proper, the wide lake visible from the shore — read 106.32 metres at 4 PM, up just 1 centimetre from the same time yesterday. That puts the lake 68 centimetres below the pre-alert level at 107 metres and firmly in normal territory. The gradual upward drift of the past few days continues, but at this pace there is no near-term concern for lakeshore properties.

The dam pond in front of the Bryson Generating Station — the backed-up water surface just downstream of the foot of Lac Coulonge — rose another centimetre today to 105.56 metres, the highest it has ever been in this now 24-day-old breach of the dam's permitted operating envelope. The permitted upper ceiling is 104.67 metres; the current level sits 89 centimetres above it. The dam's turbines remain completely shut down for the seventh straight day, and all 494 cubic metres per second of the river's flow is passing through the open spillway. No public timeline for turbine restart or return to normal operations has been announced.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's weekly forecast has not changed since June 17 and does not include any location-specific guidance for Lac Coulonge. The next scheduled update is Monday, June 23, which will clarify whether the board is maintaining the weekly freshet cadence or shifting to its lower-frequency summer monitoring posture.
