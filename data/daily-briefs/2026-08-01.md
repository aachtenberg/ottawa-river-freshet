## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The sub-300 milestone at Lake Timiscaming that appeared imminent last night did not arrive — and in fact the opposite happened. Yesterday's provisional reading of 301 cubic metres per second was revised upward to 317 when it was confirmed today, and today's first-published figure of 353 cubic metres per second is higher still. The revisions through most of this monitoring period have reliably come in below the previous day's estimate, so an upward revision of 16 cubic metres per second stands out. Whether the Aug 1 reading (also provisional) will revise similarly upward or downward is uncertain; the cross-check from the PSPC lake-level data at Lake Timiscaming itself puts the outflow at 342 cubic metres per second today, 11 units below the main published figure but pointing in the same direction. The two sources are broadly consistent, and the uptick from yesterday's confirmed 317 to today's estimated 353 is real in direction even if the exact magnitude is not yet settled. The basin is still well below the springtime levels — 353 cubic metres per second is the twentieth straight day below 400, and still only about 13% of the spring peak — but the long slide toward sub-300 levels has paused for at least one day.

Fourteen tracked storage reservoirs across the basin are nearly motionless: twelve are within a centimetre of yesterday's reading. Lake Timiscaming itself rose 4 centimetres while simultaneously releasing more water, which is consistent with inflow from the upper watershed nudging above outflow. The Des-Joachims pool, a reservoir operated by Ontario Power Generation in the central valley between the Rapide-7 and Rapide-2 generating stations, rose 5 centimetres with reported outflow at zero — also consistent with filling from upstream inflow. Neither reading is alarming; modest level rises during low-flow summer conditions are normal.

The Mattawa gauge, early in the day, shows 152.18 metres — a familiar placeholder that has been revised upward by exactly 20 centimetres on each of the past two reporting cycles (yesterday's 152.16 became 152.36 when confirmed; the day before, the same pattern). Tomorrow's confirmed Mattawa reading will almost certainly land near 152.35–152.55 metres, essentially unchanged from the level the river has held all week. Pembroke, further downstream and more reliable on the same day it is published, dropped 7 centimetres to 111.03 metres — a modest decline consistent with the ongoing post-rain recession in the mid-valley.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge reads 106.09 metres as of 4 PM local time — up just 1 centimetre from the same reading 24 hours earlier. The gentle rise that followed last weekend's rainfall has essentially stalled: the lake is nearly flat, and there is no flood concern at any threshold. The lowest flood-advisory level sits at 107.00 metres, still more than 90 centimetres above the current reading, and the level has been drifting only slowly in either direction for the past week.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — which sits well downstream of where the south end of Lac Coulonge narrows into the river channel, not at or near the lakefront properties — edged up another 3 centimetres to 105.41 metres today, the sixty-fifth straight day it has sat above the normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres, and now 74 centimetres above that limit. The spillway is passing 137 cubic metres per second, slightly less than yesterday's 142 — operators reduced spill marginally — but the dam pond continued creeping upward regardless. The slow upward drift has now persisted through the entire post-freshet period without operators restoring the dam pond to its permitted range.

Downstream at Carillon, the basin's terminal dam more than 300 kilometres to the southeast, flows are holding just above 1,230 cubic metres per second at the most recent reading — not the clean retreat from last week's rain-driven surge that had appeared to begin yesterday. The Rapides-des-Farmers station at the mouth of the Gatineau River, which feeds directly into Carillon, also ticked slightly upward (201 cubic metres per second, up about 4 from yesterday). The July 28–29 Gatineau rain pulse appears to be draining more slowly than anticipated, and the full retreat at Carillon is taking longer than a single day. The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board's forecast, which last referred to precipitation "today and tomorrow" as of July 28, has not been updated since — next update is August 4.

## Notes

- **Témiscaming uptick mechanics.** The confluence of an upward revision on Jul31 (first in the series) and a fresh provisional uptick on Aug1 suggests that actual inflow to Lake Timiscaming increased today — either from rain in the upper Timiscaming headwaters or from a shift in upstream reservoir releases. The Quinze (primary upstream cascade input to Lake Timiscaming) held essentially flat at 200.72 m³/s, so the Quinze cascade is not the direct driver. Tributary inflow from the Quebec/Ontario headwaters (Kipawa, Kinojevis basin, etc.) is the most likely source. The ORRPB live conditions page shows Kinojevis at 29 m³/s and Dumoine at 39 m³/s — both minor tributaries that would not by themselves explain +36 m³/s. A brief mid-summer rainfall event in the northern headwaters is the most plausible explanation.

- **Sub-300 watch reset.** The expectation of confirmed sub-300 at Témiscaming — which appeared likely after Jul30 confirmed at 315 and Jul31 provisional at 301 — has been deferred indefinitely. Jul31 revised to 317, and Aug1 is at 353. If the uptick is transient (one-day rain pulse), sub-300 may be re-approached within a few days. If inflow to Lake Timiscaming stays elevated, sub-300 may not occur before the summer baseflow season ends. Track the Aug1 confirmed value in tomorrow's brief.

- **Gatineau pulse trajectory.** Rapides-Farmers (201 m³/s) and Paugan (191 m³/s) both remain well above their pre-July-28 levels (~165–180 m³/s estimated). The Gatineau watershed is large enough that a two-day rainfall event can sustain elevated flows for a week or more. Carillon may remain above 1,200 m³/s for another 2–4 days before returning toward the 800–1,000 m³/s range.

- **Mattawa revision pattern.** Yesterday's brief correctly predicted the +20 cm revision to Mattawa Jul31 (152.16→152.36 m). The provisional Aug1 Mattawa (152.18 m) should similarly be read as ~152.35–152.55 m when confirmed tomorrow. The Mattawa level appears to be holding steady in the low-152 m range — not declining sharply.

- **Des-Joachims reservoir +5 cm with zero outflow.** This OPG-operated pool between Rapide-7 and Rapide-2 shows 0 m³/s outflow in the DB but +5 cm level gain. This could indicate a measurement gap in the outflow reporting or that the sluice is genuinely closed and the pool is filling from Rapide-7's ~156 m³/s upstream release. The Rapide-2 station immediately below des_joachims shows 161 m³/s outflow, suggesting normal throughput at that station — likely a reporting artifact rather than a genuine zero-outflow condition.
