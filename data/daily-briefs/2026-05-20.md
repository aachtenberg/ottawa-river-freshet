The rise isn't dramatic, but it is a reversal. A pulse of water has been working through the mid-Ottawa valley: Pembroke (upstream) rose about 10 centimetres overnight, and that water is now arriving at Fort-Coulonge.

More importantly, the dam pond recovered 5 centimetres to reach exactly the bottom of its normal 47-centimetre operating window (104.20 metres), reversing yesterday's dip below the floor. At the basin's terminal dam (Carillon, near Grenville, QC), total releases fell about 3% but the dam's own pond rose another 2 centimetres to 40.53 metres — now 45 centimetres above the spring-flood regulatory ceiling that is supposed to apply while the river remains high. This is the 16th consecutive day the ceiling has been exceeded.

The river management board has not issued a new forecast today — the next update is expected tomorrow afternoon (May 21). The most recent forecast (from yesterday) said levels would be "fairly stable before gradually declining again in all locations." No rain events or secondary peaks are flagged.

For property owners: the exit from minor flood has been delayed. The lake rose today instead of falling and now sits 9 centimetres above the minor-flood line. The pulse of upstream water that caused today's rise will clear the system within 24–48 hours, and the gradual decline should resume. At the pace the board forecasts, clearing minor flood likely takes another 2–5 days — longer if any rain arrives. Watch tomorrow's reading to see if the lake turns back down.
