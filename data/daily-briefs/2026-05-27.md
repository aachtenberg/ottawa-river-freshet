### Upstream — the upper basin

The flow leaving Lake Temiscaming hit a new milestone today: 1,319 cubic metres per second (m³/s, a unit of river volume per second) — the first confirmed day below 1,400 m³/s since the spring freshet peaked. A note on yesterday's brief: the preliminary reading published on the river management board's page at run time showed 1,314 m³/s for May 26, which led us to call sub-1,400 a day early. The board's finalized May 26 figure is 1,352 — just above the threshold. Today's reading of 1,319 is today's preliminary, but the direction is clear and unambiguous. We are now 25 days past the May 2 peak of roughly 2,741 m³/s. Today is Day 7 with flow below 2,000 m³/s, Day 5 below 1,500 m³/s, and Day 1 below 1,400 m³/s. The upper Ottawa is shedding about 33 m³/s per day right now — a slow, steady wind-down. The next round-number milestone to watch is sub-1,000, which at current rates is perhaps one to two weeks away.

One ongoing story in the mid-valley is the Des Joachims reservoir (operated by Ontario Power Generation, roughly halfway between Lake Temiscaming and the property). For the third consecutive day it is gaining water — up another 12 centimetres today (to 151.24 metres) after gains of 15 cm and 25 cm on the two prior days. OPG is deliberately holding and storing the northern inflow rather than passing it straight downstream. This is normal post-freshet behaviour — the operator rebuilds storage for the summer — and it gently cushions the downstream lake from any remaining pulse from the north. It is not a flood risk.

The mid-valley level gauges continue their broad decline. Mattawa (where the Ottawa descends out of the northern basin) fell another 3 centimetres to 152.97 m, broadly trending downward from 153.74 m a week ago. Pembroke fell another 4 centimetres to 112.13 m, down 62 centimetres from a week ago. Across 14 tracked headwater reservoirs, 2 are rising, 9 are holding steady, and 3 are falling modestly — the basin's normal post-freshet refill posture, nothing anomalous.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

At 106.79 metres as of 2 PM Eastern, the lake has crossed below every Vigilance threshold. Yesterday it was in the lowest monitoring state (vigilance watch); today it is fully normal. The river management board's own conditions table shows the current level at 106.75 m, which is 21.5 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold (107.0 m) and nearly a full metre below the minor-flood mark (107.5 m). The lake is declining at roughly 13–16 centimetres per day, depending on which comparison you use.

One development at the dam at the foot of Lac Coulonge (Bryson Generating Station) is worth noting — not because it is a problem, but because it marks a shift in posture. Yesterday, the dam was running with 68% of its water going through the spillway and only 32% through the turbines. Today that has reversed sharply: 44% through the spillway, 56% through the turbines. The operator dramatically increased turbine generation (up 63%) while cutting spillway flow by 40%. The total water released is down about 7%. This is consistent with the falling lake: as less water presses over the spillway naturally, the operator compensates by pushing more through the turbines to generate power. The dam's backed-up pond (between the lake and the turbines) fell another 8 centimetres to 104.01 m, which is 19 centimetres below the lower limit of its normal operating range (104.20 m). That breach has been ongoing since May 21 and is driven passively by the declining lake — the operator cannot fill the pond faster than the lake is draining.

The river management board's weekly bulletin (last updated Tuesday, May 26) remains unchanged: conditions are "within seasonal values" and expected to stay stable or decrease gradually. No precipitation or flood concerns are flagged. The next update is not until Tuesday, June 2. For the property owner: the water is going down about 13–16 cm per day, the monitoring flag is gone, and there is nothing on the horizon pointing to a reversal.
