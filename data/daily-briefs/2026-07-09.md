## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The Ottawa basin's post-event recession has accelerated significantly over the past three days. The water flowing out of Lake Timiscaming — the large lake straddling the Ontario–Quebec border that drains southward into the Ottawa main stem — has dropped sharply from its Canada Day weekend surge peak. On July 4 the outflow reached 928 cubic metres per second (roughly 70% above the normal July level). By July 8 it had fallen to 598 cubic metres per second — now slightly below the level of July 2, before the event even started. This is Day 69 since the spring freshet peak in early May, and the upper basin is well into normal summer drawdown.

The mid-valley stations that lie between Lake Timiscaming and the dam chain are also clearly receding. At Mattawa — the town roughly two-thirds of the way downstream from Lake Timiscaming — the river level fell five centimetres overnight (July 7 to July 8) to 152.45 metres. This is Day 3 of consecutive decline since the series high of 152.97 metres recorded on July 2. Pembroke, farther down the valley, has also turned the corner decisively: the July 7-to-8 drop was 18 centimetres, the sharpest single-day fall there since the event began.

The basin's storage reservoirs are in pure drainage mode. None of the fourteen tracked reservoirs rose more than a centimetre between July 7 and July 8, while seven fell between three and six centimetres. Lake Timiscaming itself is now essentially flat — its outflow and natural inflow have nearly balanced, at a much lower outflow rate (around 565–598 cubic metres per second) than the event peak.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

Lac Coulonge is falling rapidly and is now firmly in normal territory. The Vigilance gauge at the Mansfield waterfront read 106.25 metres at 8 AM Eastern on July 9 — about 16.5 centimetres below the same reading 24 hours earlier. Overnight and into this morning the lake dropped a further 11 centimetres from its July 8 midnight level of 106.36 metres. The overnight ORRPB midnight series shows the lake fell 15 centimetres in a single day on July 8 (from 106.51 to 106.36 metres), the fastest one-day descent since the event began. The lake remains more than 75 centimetres below the 107.00-metre level at which a pre-alert advisory would apply, and more than a metre and a quarter below the 107.50-metre minor-flood threshold. This body of water is not in any flood advisory category.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — located well below the property reach, reached by following Lac Coulonge's south-end narrows several kilometres down to the dam — held essentially flat at 105.30 metres on July 8 (one centimetre above the July 7 level). The dam pond has been above its operating ceiling of 104.67 metres for 42 consecutive days, and at the current pace of decline the return to compliance remains weeks away. On July 8, Hydro-Québec cut total releases at Bryson by about 75 cubic metres per second (down roughly 12%), with essentially all of that reduction coming from the spillway. The operators also made a large cut at Rapide-7 — the dam just upstream — reducing flow there by 35%.

The Ottawa River Regulation Planning Board published its first forecast update since July 2 yesterday afternoon (July 7, 3:50 PM Eastern). The updated text confirms what the data has been showing: "water levels and flows have started to decrease and are expected to continue decreasing gradually or remain fairly stable over the coming week depending on location." Importantly, the ORRPB has now shifted to a weekly update schedule (next update July 14), reflecting that the basin has exited active freshet management and entered the normal summer operating season. For Lac Coulonge specifically, the current trajectory of roughly 10–16 centimetres per day puts the lake well clear of any flood advisory in a matter of days, barring significant new precipitation.
