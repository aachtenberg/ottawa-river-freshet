## In plain language

### Upstream — the upper basin

The brief uptick at Lake Timiscaming that appeared yesterday has already begun to ease. Yesterday's provisional reading of 353 cubic metres per second at the Témiscaming outflow was revised down to 342 when confirmed today — consistent with the long-running pattern of provisional overestimates — and today's preliminary figure stands at 329 cubic metres per second, pointing the river back toward the slow descent that had characterized the whole month of July. A real-time reading from the ORRPB monitoring page at 9 AM local time today showed 366 cubic metres per second, which is notably higher than the daily average — meaning the confirmed August 2 number, published tomorrow, could revise upward. For now the basin is on its twenty-first consecutive day below 400 cubic metres per second, and the threshold most closely being watched — 300 cubic metres per second, the lowest level in the post-peak period — remains just out of reach at 29 metres per second above.

Storage reservoirs across the basin are remarkably quiet. Eleven of fourteen tracked reservoirs are within two centimetres of yesterday's reading. Three are rising modestly: the Des-Joachims pool in the mid-valley (which is filling from upstream inflow while its reported outflow shows zero — likely a measurement artifact at that station, since the downstream Rapide-2 station continues to pass normal flow), Kiamika, and Mitchinamecus. None of these rises exceeds five centimetres — well short of the ten-centimetre-per-day threshold that would flag active basin-wide water retention. Operators appear to be in a quiet summer refill posture rather than any deliberate holding action.

The Mattawa gauge presents the same provisional-versus-actual split seen all week. The database shows a preliminary morning reading of 152.20 metres, but the ORRPB's own live page — captured at 10 AM local time today — already shows 152.36 metres, identical to yesterday's confirmed level. The pattern of provisional readings running approximately 15–20 centimetres below the confirmed value has held every day this week; Mattawa is holding steady in the 152.35–152.36 metre range. Pembroke, well downstream and generally more stable on the same day it is published, sits at 111.09 metres — essentially unchanged from yesterday's revised figure of 111.08 metres. The mid-valley reach is flat.

### At the property — Lac Coulonge / Mansfield

The gauge at Fort-Coulonge reads 106.08 metres as of 4 PM local time, down less than one centimetre from the same reading 24 hours earlier. The lake continues to drift at the lowest levels it has seen since before the spring freshet — still 92 centimetres below the pre-alert threshold of 107.00 metres, and nearly two metres below the lowest flood level. There is nothing approaching a flood concern and no forecast change expected until the ORRPB publishes an update on August 4.

The dam pond at the Bryson Generating Station — which sits well downstream of where the south end of Lac Coulonge narrows into the river channel, well removed from the lakefront properties at Mansfield and Fort-Coulonge — edged down one centimetre today to 105.40 metres. This is the first day in recent memory the headpond has not ticked upward or held flat; it is still 73 centimetres above the normal summer operating ceiling of 104.67 metres and has been above that ceiling for 66 consecutive days. Operators increased turbine output by roughly 8 cubic metres per second (from 287 to 295 cubic metres per second) while keeping spillway flow nearly unchanged, which accounts for the minor drawdown. The 1-centimetre drop is too small to signal any deliberate management change, but it is the first reversal in the slow upward drift.

The biggest story today is at Carillon, the basin's terminal dam more than 300 kilometres to the southeast: flows dropped from roughly 1,234 cubic metres per second yesterday to 1,131 cubic metres per second today — a decline of about 103 cubic metres per second, or 8.4 percent in one day. This is the clean retreat of the July 28–29 Gatineau rainfall pulse that was expected but took a day longer to appear than anticipated. The Gatineau River's main upstream station (Paugan) and its mouth station (Rapides-des-Farmers) are both essentially flat at around 190 and 201 cubic metres per second respectively, so the bulk of the Carillon decline reflects the broader Ottawa Valley drainage recession rather than the Gatineau alone.

## Notes

- **Carillon retreat mechanics.** The 103 m³/s decline at Carillon is large enough to be clearly real rather than a measurement artefact. Interestingly, neither Paugan (Gatineau generating station, 190 m³/s flat) nor Rapides-Farmers (Gatineau mouth, 201 m³/s flat) contributed meaningfully to the drop — meaning the Ottawa Valley tributaries between Bryson and Carillon (Madawaska, Bonnechère, Mississippi, Rideau, etc.) are where the rain-pulse drainage is materializing. The net basin-wide recession is reaching Carillon through distributed tributary drainage rather than the single Gatineau channel.

- **Témiscaming intraday vs daily discrepancy.** The ORRPB live page reported 366.28 m³/s at Témiscaming at T13:00 UTC (9 AM local time). The provisional daily average in the DB is 329 m³/s (ORRPB) and 333 m³/s (PSPC reservoir). A gap of 33–37 m³/s between a morning real-time reading and the daily average is large and suggests either (a) intraday variability at the dam (release operations typically vary through the day), or (b) the provisional daily value is using a prior-day figure not yet updated for today. The Aug2 confirmed daily average (published tomorrow) will clarify. Given Jul31's first upward revision (+16 m³/s) and the live signal of 366 today, there is a real chance the Aug2 confirmed value will be above 340 m³/s rather than near 329 m³/s.

- **Sub-300 trajectory.** The minimum confirmed value remains 315 m³/s (Jul30). If Aug2 confirms near 329 m³/s (daily average) and the trend resumes declining, sub-300 could be reached within the next 3–7 days. If the live intraday signal of 366 m³/s holds as a daily average, sub-300 is further off. The oscillatory pattern in the 315–342 range (±27 m³/s swings) makes precise forecasting difficult; the general trend remains downward but the approach is uneven.

- **Des-Joachims zero-outflow second day.** The Des-Joachims pool shows +5 cm gain for the second consecutive day with reported outflow at zero. The downstream Rapide-2 station (161.31 m³/s) shows normal throughput — this is almost certainly a reporting lag or telemetry issue at the des_joachims station rather than a genuine dam closure.
