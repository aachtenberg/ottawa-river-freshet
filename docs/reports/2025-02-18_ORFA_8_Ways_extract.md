## Why this is on file

This is **the canonical ORFA policy statement** as of Feb 2025 — written specifically to be acted on before the 2025 freshet. The case-file analytical work in this repo has independently arrived at findings that are consistent with ORFA's whitepaper, but ORFA's document carries authoritative weight that homemade analysis does not: it is the community's *own articulated position*, signed off by the alliance, with named officials to contact and specific demands.

Five things this document does that nothing else in the case file does:

1. **States the policy ask explicitly** — the **eight actions** (below) are the canonical demand framework. Every analytical piece in the case file ought to support one or more of these actions or be discarded as off-frame.
2. **Names the targets.** Steven Guilbeault (federal ECCC), Benoit Charette (Quebec MELCCFP), Hon. Graydon Smith (Ontario MNR) — with direct email addresses and phone numbers (p.10). The whitepaper makes the case that ORRPB/ORRC are *not* the right addressees.
3. **Documents the ORRPB mandate change.** The mandate moved from "protection against flooding" to **"reducing flood and drought impacts"** (p.7) — a textual confirmation of the operational-drift thesis the case file argues.
4. **Records the 2024 freshet anomaly.** Despite a record-low annual snowpack and only one significant rain event, "the decisions made by operators along the watershed resulted in weeks of dangerously high levels and flows. Specifically, opting not to empty the primary reservoirs and instead choosing an early refill which caused a situation where, on April 1st, when the reservoirs should normally be empty, they were filled to 50% capacity" (p.7). This is a separate-from-2026 data point on the same operational pattern.
5. **Includes the verbatim Carillon §2.1 table** (p.3). Independent confirmation of the same source document already transcribed in [`2004_Carillon_IWMP_operating_envelope.md`](2004_Carillon_IWMP_operating_envelope.md) — chain of custody from the 2004 IWMP Summary is unambiguous.

## Eight actions (ORFA's policy ask, pp.8–9)

ORFA's eight actions, each annotated with where this case file has analytical evidence:

1. **Enforce immediate regulatory intervention** — implement existing WMP / freshet drawdown directives now. *Maps to:* the Carillon §2.1 39-day non-compliance finding in [`2004_Carillon_IWMP_operating_envelope.md`](2004_Carillon_IWMP_operating_envelope.md) — the strongest single piece of "framework exists / enforcement doesn't" evidence we have.
2. **Establish a new governing body** with enforcement authority and representation from riparians/municipalities/businesses, not just energy lobbyists. *Maps to:* the "no entity whose job is to look at the whole picture" diagnosis in [`../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/2026-05-22_mitigation_diagnosis.md`](../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/2026-05-22_mitigation_diagnosis.md).
3. **Ensure transparency and oversight** — keep hydro-company hydrologists out of governmental priority-setting teams.
4. **Re-evaluate the 1998 Flood Reserves Policy and water-diversion policies** — including a trial removal. *Maps to:* the case-file 2017 diversion record and [`2016_management_change_claim.md`](2016_management_change_claim.md).
5. **Reinstate on-site qualified officials** — monitor and communicate flow/levels through the freshet.
6. **Maintain and expand hydrometric equipment** — keep gauges in service and the data public.
7. **Lower reservoirs to their absolute minimum and delay refill.** "The 13 principal reservoirs have not consistently been lowered to empty status prior to the spring Freshet. In addition to this, reservoirs have been refilling at a much faster pace in the last decade than the previous 35 years." *Maps to:* the [`../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/orrpb-2026-drawdown/reservoir_drawdown_apr1.csv`](../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/orrpb-2026-drawdown/reservoir_drawdown_apr1.csv) computed April-1 census (Cabonga 60%, Kiamika 45%, Mitchinamecus 43%) and the [Haxton-Chubbuck 2002 extract](2002_Haxton_Chubbuck_extract.md) historical drawdown figures.
8. **Acknowledge all Ottawa River reaches as reservoirs** — challenge the "run-of-river" framing on headponds; adjust each reach (Bryson / Lac Coulonge among them) to lowest possible pre-freshet levels. *Maps to:* the Lac Coulonge deliberate-elevation finding (winter 2026 setpoint flat at 106.55 m; early-March 2026 drawdown-and-refill cycle) in [`../data/community-notes/2026-05-22_reservoir_drawdown.md`](../../data/community-notes/2026-05-22_reservoir_drawdown.md) and the broader reach-baseline analysis in the mitigation diagnosis.

**Action 8 is the explicit framing for everything we have on Bryson and Lac Coulonge.** ORFA's whitepaper independently arrived at the same "head-pond management greatly influences upstream backwater effects" mechanism that the case file documents from the data side.

## Specific documented findings worth citing

Useful anchor points from the document for FB posts / letters / further correspondence:

- **2017, 2019, 2023 spring super-floods + Nov 2023 + Apr 2024 — 5 major flood events in 8 years** (p.2).
- **Deaths attributable to floods:** Pontiac woman 2019; two first responders, Charlevoix QC, 2023 (p.7).
- **2019 federal military deployment:** 2,000 Canadian Armed Forces personnel (p.7).
- **Gatineau, 2017 super-flood:** $5.95 M municipal damages + $3.4 M Quebec compensation to property owners (p.8).
- **Wilderness Tours, 2019:** > $1 M loss in a single business in a single year (p.7).
- **2024 freshet:** record-low annual snowpack + one significant rain event, yet reservoirs at **50 % capacity on April 1** (when they "should normally be empty") and "weeks of dangerously high levels and flows" (p.7). This is the cleanest single-year evidence that "the operating regime no longer matches the snowpack-indexed pre-positioning the system needs."
- **ORRPB mandate change:** "protection against flooding" → "reducing flood and drought impacts" (p.7).
- **In-person water management removed** — the whitepaper notes Action 5 calls to *reinstate* on-site officials, implying their removal as part of the operational drift.
- **Ottawa River basin ≈ 75 % of reservoirs controlled by HQ + Evolugen + MELCCFP** (p.6).
- **Hazard Potential of most Ottawa River dams:** "Very High" per the various Dam Safety Acts (p.6).
- **Clarence-Rockland CAO Pierre Voisine** — quote authorised for ORFA use about the municipal preparedness gap (p.7).

## How this changes the case-file framing

Before this document was filed, the case file's analytical work and ORFA's policy work were running in parallel without explicit alignment. Filing the whitepaper here makes the alignment explicit:

- **The data work** in [`../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/`](../../../ottawa-river-vigilance/) and [`../../data/community-notes/`](../../data/community-notes/) is the **evidence base** for ORFA's claims.
- **The whitepaper** is the **policy ask** that this evidence supports.
- The **eight actions** are the framework against which any new finding should be evaluated for relevance.

Practically: whenever a new community-note draft or a new analytical exhibit is added, it should be testable against the question *"which of the eight actions does this support?"* — and if the answer is none, the work is off-frame and should be reconsidered or re-scoped.
