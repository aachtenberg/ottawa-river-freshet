# Proposal: install a new Ottawa River stage gauge at Waltham (QC), opposite the decommissioned WSC 02KC005

**Case-file policy ask. Compiled 2026-05-26.**

## Plain language summary

The Water Survey of Canada gauge at Ottawa River near Westmeath (WSC 02KC005) was decommissioned by Ontario Power Generation in 1993 and refused for reinstatement in 2021. It is the missing public-record stage instrument in the long Ottawa main-stem reach upstream of Carillon, and its absence is the load-bearing data gap behind multiple findings in this case file. The 2017, 2019, and 2026 freshets all hit Pontiac communities hard, and the audit of operator decisions during those events cannot be closed without this gauge.

The cleanest path to fill the gap is **not** to re-engage OPG. OPG walked away from this station, refused the community's 2021 reinstall request, and is an Ontario Crown corporation with no operational need for the data. Re-litigating that decision is unlikely to succeed.

The cleanest path is **a new Quebec-side station at Waltham**, directly across the Ottawa River from the historic 02KC005 site, installed by Centre d'expertise hydrique du Québec (CEHQ) under a standard WSC-CEHQ cost-sharing agreement. Same reach, same hydraulic signal, no OPG involvement required, no Ontario MPP coordination required, fully within the existing federal-provincial hydrometric cooperative funding model.

This document is the case file's articulated ask for that install. It is intended to be relayed to the Pontiac MNA, the Quebec Minister of Environment (MELCC, currently Benoit Charette), the federal Minister of Environment and Climate Change (ECCC, currently Steven Guilbeault), and ORFA leadership for coordination with the alliance's *Eight Ways to End the Super Floods* policy frame.

## The ask in one paragraph

Install a permanent stage and discharge hydrometric station on the Quebec bank of the Ottawa River at or near Waltham (approximate coordinates 45.895° N, 76.911° W, opposite the historic WSC 02KC005 location), operated by CEHQ with cost-sharing through the existing CEHQ-WSC bilateral hydrometric agreement. The station should be configured to publish real-time stage data on Vigilance and historical daily data through WSC, with a published rating-curve program for discharge using ADCP gaugings on a multi-year maintenance schedule. The vertical datum should be CGVD2013 with a surveyed benchmark tying the new record to the historic 02KC005 datum so the 1935 to 1995 stage record at the old station can be linked to the new continuous series.

## What the missing gauge means for the case file

The Westmeath gauge sits at a hydraulically privileged location for auditing the operating decisions of multiple Ottawa River cascade operators. Its presence would enable, and its absence currently blocks, three specific analyses already documented in this case file:

1. **The Pembroke 2023 versus 2026 attribution** ([data/community-notes/2026-05-23_pembroke_2023_vs_2026.md](../../data/community-notes/2026-05-23_pembroke_2023_vs_2026.md)). Same OPG dam releases (Des Joachims peak flow at 3,324 m³/s in both years), same Petawawa tributary input, yet 2023 peaked 14 cm higher at Pembroke than 2026. The only remaining lever is peak-time outflow scheduling at Des Joachims. The Westmeath gauge would have constrained this directly through the level response to release patterns.

2. **The post-2017 freshet regime change at the property gauge** ([data/community-notes/2026-05-26_lake_coulonge_2026.md](../../data/community-notes/2026-05-26_lake_coulonge_2026.md)). Across the per-day historic high record at Lake Coulonge, 2019 alone holds the record on 61 days of the year, with 2016 (35 days), 2008 (22), 2012 (21), 2001 (20) following. Six of the top eight record-setting years come from the post-2008 window. The Westmeath stage record would extend this signal upstream into the Allumette reach, where the post-2017 regime change has the same structural cause but no public-record gauge to measure it.

3. **The Bryson no-drawdown finding** ([data/community-notes/2026-05-26_bryson_no_drawdown.md](../../data/community-notes/2026-05-26_bryson_no_drawdown.md)). The 1991 to 2020 daily median at Lac Coulonge shows just 7 cm of seasonal variation. The Westmeath gauge upstream would let analysts verify whether the no-drawdown posture extends through the Allumette reach as well, or whether the operators above Westmeath (OPG at Des Joachims) draw down in a way OPG at Bryson does not.

The broader structural argument is in [docs/analysis/Canada_US_Hydrometric_Comparison.md](../analysis/Canada_US_Hydrometric_Comparison.md): the cooperative cost-recovery funding model that governs the Canadian hydrometric program makes underfunding invisible (a single line of HYDAT metadata changes when a station dies), and Westmeath 02KC005 is the canonical case study. A QC-side install rebuilds the public-record capability without requiring the broader structural fix.

## Why the QC side is structurally preferable

Five arguments, ordered by importance:

1. **It bypasses OPG entirely.** The 1993 decommissioning and 2021 reinstall refusal both originated with OPG. An Ontario Crown corporation that walked away from a station has no operational reason to come back, and Ontario provincial-shareholder pressure has a long path through the Ministry of Energy that the case file should not bet on. A QC-side install never asks OPG anything.

2. **The CEHQ-WSC cost-sharing model is already in place.** CEHQ operates many Vigilance stations on the Ottawa River basin (station 1195 at Fort-Coulonge is a few kilometres downstream and uses this same arrangement). No new institutional pathway needs to be invented. The 2023-2024 Canada Water Act Annual Report documents 12 active federal-provincial-territorial bilateral hydrometric agreements; the CEHQ-WSC one runs through MELCC.

3. **The Pontiac flood-management constituency is in Quebec.** The 2017 and 2019 floods devastated Pontiac communities. The 2024 Quebec flood-zone update expanded the high-risk zones in this region. The political case for a Quebec-side install is anchored in Quebec constituents and Quebec ministers, not an Ontario-side ask routed through a provincial Crown corporation.

4. **The hydraulic signal is identical.** Waltham and the historic 02KC005 location are roughly 600 metres apart across the same Ottawa River cross-section. Stage at either bank measures the same water surface to within centimetres. For discharge, the rating curve would be developed on the new station's cross-section directly, which is preferable to inheriting an "ASSUMED DATUM" from the 1935-1995 station.

5. **The historic record can be continuous.** The 02KC005 record (1935 to 1995, level-only) is 60 years of pre-decommissioning data. A surveyed benchmark on the new station tying its zero to the historic 02KC005 zero would let analysts append the new continuous record to the old, producing a near-90-year level series through this reach.

## Cost estimate

Order-of-magnitude figures based on the 2023 Treasury Board *Evaluation of the National Hydrological Services* (which puts ECCC's per-station annual average around $11,000 to $17,000 in current dollars, with active rating-curve stations on large rivers at the higher end of that range):

| Cost line | Estimate (CAD, 2026) |
|---|---|
| One-time installation (stage gauge + telemetry + benchmark survey + datum tie) | $20,000 to $40,000 |
| Initial rating-curve startup (3 to 6 ADCP gaugings, bathymetric survey, cross-section work) | $15,000 to $40,000 |
| **Total one-time** | **$35,000 to $80,000** |
| Annual ongoing (maintenance, telemetry fees, data QC, ratification) | $6,000 to $12,000 |
| Annual rating-curve maintenance (2 to 4 gaugings per year) | $5,000 to $15,000 |
| **Total annual ongoing** | **$11,000 to $27,000** |

Under the CEHQ-WSC cost-sharing model, these costs are typically split with federal and provincial shares determined by the data-use need; municipalities or conservation authorities can join as additional partners if the case is made. The order of magnitude does not justify the policy-level delay of trying to re-engage OPG on a station they declined to reinstate.

## Why not just trust a private install at the same coordinates

A private pressure transducer on a public-shore footing could begin generating real-time stage data within weeks at one or two orders of magnitude lower cost. We considered this option ([docs/analysis/Canada_US_Hydrometric_Comparison.md](../analysis/Canada_US_Hydrometric_Comparison.md), "Private-stage-gauge installation is a tactical fit, not a substitute"). It is a useful interim measure for generating evidence-while-lobbying, but not a substitute, because:

1. Private data carries no regulatory or legal pedigree. It cannot be cited in MNRF / MELCC / ECCC regulatory proceedings without an accepted-standard institutional source.
2. Operators are free to dismiss private data publicly without consequence ("not our data, no quality guarantee").
3. The case-file's structural argument is precisely that the *absence of a public-record instrument* is the problem. Filling that absence with a private gauge would let operators argue the gap is solved while leaving the structural pattern untouched at every other comparable station in the basin.

A QC-side WSC-cost-shared install at Waltham solves the immediate gap and also strengthens the broader structural argument by demonstrating that the cooperative model CAN work when a partner steps up.

## Who this ask is for

The case file suggests a coordinated multi-channel relay:

1. **Pontiac MNA (provincial, Quebec)**. The local constituent channel into MELCC. The constituent argument: 2017, 2019, 2026 floods all affected Pontiac; the QC-side install is the lowest-friction path to filling the public-record gap upstream of where Vigilance currently covers.

2. **Quebec Minister of Environment (MELCC, Benoit Charette as of 2026 per ORFA targeting)**. The institutional decision-maker. The framing here matches ORFA's *Eight Ways* whitepaper: this is part of basin-wide flood-management infrastructure that QC has both the authority and the interest to fund.

3. **Federal Minister of Environment and Climate Change (ECCC, Steven Guilbeault as of 2026 per ORFA targeting)**. The federal cost-share counterpart. The argument: ECCC already cost-shares dozens of CEHQ stations under the existing bilateral; this is one more in a hydrologically important reach where the federal side has long-standing data continuity from 02KC005.

4. **ORFA leadership**. To incorporate into the alliance's coordinated ministerial-relations work alongside the existing *Eight Ways* asks. This is structurally compatible with Action 1 (basin-wide pre-freshet drawdown protocols) and Action 8 (basin-wide observational expansion), not in tension with any of the eight.

5. **CCing the Pontiac MP (federal)**. As a federal channel into ECCC; secondary to ORFA's direct ministerial path.

The case file does **not** recommend routing this ask through OPG, the Ontario Minister of Energy, the Ontario MPP for Renfrew-Nipissing-Pembroke, or the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources. Those channels are available if QC-side advocacy stalls, but they are not the lowest-friction first path.

## What success looks like

In sequence:

1. **2026 to 2027**: MELCC budget cycle includes a small-station expansion line item. CEHQ confirms the install is in their 2027 work plan. A benchmark survey is conducted at the proposed site. ECCC confirms WSC cost-share participation under the bilateral.
2. **2027**: Installation. First stage data flows on Vigilance. Initial ADCP rating-curve gaugings begin. The Allumette-reach observational gap is functionally closed for stage.
3. **2028 onwards**: Multi-year rating-curve maturation. The first full freshet-season audit using the new station's flow data lands in the 2028 community brief cycle.
4. **Structural payoff**: One more cooperatively-funded station, designated for long-term operation, with a published rating-curve protocol. The case-file argument that "the public-record gap on the Ottawa main stem is fixable when partners step up" gets its working example.
